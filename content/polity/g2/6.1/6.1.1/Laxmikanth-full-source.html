Chapter 1 — Historical Background

M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity, 8th Edition — Full-Depth Bilingual Study Notes
From a Trading Company to a Territorial Ruler

The British first arrived in India in 1608, not as rulers but as traders, operating through the East India Company — a private commercial body that held an exclusive right to trade with India under a charter granted by Queen Elizabeth I in 1600. For over a century and a half the Company remained essentially a business venture. That changed in 1765, when the Company obtained the 'diwani' of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa — the right to collect revenue and administer civil justice in those territories, won in the aftermath of the Company's military victory at Buxar. This grant is the pivotal turning point the book flags: it marked the Company's transformation from a purely trading firm into a territorial power — an entity that now governed land and people, not just goods. This dual identity — merchant and ruler at once — is what eventually forced the British Parliament in London to start regulating the Company's affairs, since a private company answerable only to its shareholders was now, in effect, running a colonial government.

British వారు మొదట India కు 1608లో వచ్చారు — పాలకులుగా కాదు, వ్యాపారులుగా — East India Company ద్వారా — ఇది 1600లో Queen Elizabeth I ఇచ్చిన charter కింద India తో వ్యాపారం చేసే exclusive హక్కు కలిగిన ఒక ప్రైవేట్ వ్యాపార సంస్థ. ఒకటిన్నర శతాబ్దం పైగా Company ప్రధానంగా ఒక business venture గానే ఉంది. ఈ పరిస్థితి 1765లో మారింది — Battle of Buxar విజయం తర్వాత Company Bengal, Bihar, Orissa 'diwani' (revenue వసూలు, civil justice నిర్వహణ హక్కు) పొందింది. పుస్తకం ప్రత్యేకంగా గుర్తించే మలుపు ఇదే — ఇది Company ను కేవలం వ్యాపార సంస్థ నుండి territorial power గా మార్చింది — ఇక అది వస్తువులు మాత్రమే కాదు, భూమి, ప్రజలను కూడా పాలించే సంస్థ అయ్యింది. వ్యాపారి, పాలకుడు — ఈ రెండు గుర్తింపులూ ఒకేసారి ఉండటమే చివరకు London లోని British Parliament ను Company వ్యవహారాలను నియంత్రించమని ఒత్తిడి చేసింది — ఎందుకంటే కేవలం తన shareholders కు మాత్రమే జవాబుదారీ అయిన ఒక ప్రైవేట్ కంపెనీ ఇప్పుడు వాస్తవానికి ఒక వలస ప్రభుత్వాన్ని నడుపుతోంది.

In 1858, in the wake of the 'sepoy mutiny', the British Crown finally took over direct responsibility for governing India from the Company — and this Crown rule continued until India was granted independence on August 15, 1947. With independence came the urgent need for a home-grown Constitution — a Constituent Assembly was formed for this purpose in 1946, and on January 26, 1950 the Constitution of India finally came into being. Yet, as this chapter sets out to show, many features of Indian polity and the Constitution itself have deep roots in the legal and administrative framework the British built during their rule. The book organises this constitutional history chronologically under two broad headings: (1) The Company Rule (1773–1858) and (2) The Crown Rule (1858–1947).

1858లో 'sepoy mutiny' తర్వాత, British Crown చివరకు Company నుండి India పాలన బాధ్యతను నేరుగా తీసుకుంది — ఈ Crown rule ఆగస్టు 15, 1947 న India కు స్వాతంత్ర్యం వచ్చేవరకూ కొనసాగింది. స్వాతంత్ర్యంతో పాటు సొంత రాజ్యాంగం అవసరం తీవ్రంగా వచ్చింది — ఇందుకోసం 1946లో Constituent Assembly ఏర్పడింది, జనవరి 26, 1950న చివరకు India రాజ్యాంగం అమలులోకి వచ్చింది. అయితే, ఈ అధ్యాయం చూపించదలచినట్టు, Indian polity, రాజ్యాంగం లక్షణాలు అనేకం British పాలనలో నిర్మించిన legal, administrative framework లోనే లోతైన మూలాలు కలిగి ఉన్నాయి. పుస్తకం ఈ constitutional history ను రెండు broad headings కింద కాలక్రమంలో వివరిస్తుంది — (1) The Company Rule (1773-1858), (2) The Crown Rule (1858-1947).

Regulating Act of 1773

The Regulating Act of 1773 was of great constitutional importance for three reasons the book highlights: it was the first step taken by the British Government to control and regulate the Company's affairs in India; it recognised, for the first time, the Company's political and administrative functions (not just its commercial ones); and it laid the foundations of central administration in India. Concretely, it designated the Governor of Bengal as the 'Governor-General of Bengal', aided by an Executive Council of 4 members — the first person to hold this post was Lord Warren Hastings. It also subordinated the Governors of Bombay and Madras to this Bengal Governor-General, a sharp change from the earlier arrangement where the three presidencies operated entirely independently of one another. The Act further provided for a Supreme Court at Calcutta (established 1774), comprising one chief justice and three other judges; prohibited Company servants from private trade or accepting bribes/presents from Indians; and required the Company's Court of Directors to report regularly to the British Government on revenue, civil and military affairs in India.

పుస్తకం ఎత్తిచూపిన మూడు కారణాల వల్ల Regulating Act of 1773 constitutional గా చాలా ముఖ్యమైనది — ఇది Company India వ్యవహారాలను నియంత్రించడానికి British Government తీసుకున్న మొదటి అడుగు; Company political, administrative functions ను (కేవలం commercial కాకుండా) మొదటిసారి గుర్తించింది; India లో central administration పునాదులు వేసింది. ప్రత్యేకంగా చెప్పాలంటే — ఇది Bengal Governor ను 'Governor-General of Bengal' గా పేర్కొంది, సహాయానికి 4 మంది సభ్యుల Executive Council ఏర్పాటు చేసింది — ఈ పదవి చేపట్టిన మొదటి వ్యక్తి Lord Warren Hastings. Fixing 1773's Defects — Amending Act 1781 & Pitt's India Act 1784
Amending Act of 1781 (Act of Settlement)

Within just eight years, the British Parliament had to pass the Amending Act of 1781 — also called the Act of Settlement — to fix defects that had emerged in the Regulating Act of 1773. It exempted the Governor-General and Council, as well as ordinary Company servants, from the Supreme Court's jurisdiction for acts carried out in their official capacity, and excluded revenue matters from the Court's jurisdiction altogether. It also clarified that while the Supreme Court had jurisdiction over all inhabitants of Calcutta, it had to apply each defendant's own personal law — Hindus tried under Hindu law, Muslims under Mohammedan law. Finally, it redirected appeals from the Provincial Courts to the Governor-General-in-Council instead of the Supreme Court, and empowered the Governor-General-in-Council to frame regulations for the Provincial Courts and Councils.

కేవలం ఎనిమిదేళ్లలోనే, British Parliament Regulating Act 1773 లో వచ్చిన లోపాలను సరిదిద్దడానికి Amending Act of 1781 — దీన్నే Act of Settlement అని కూడా అంటారు — పాస్ చేయాల్సి వచ్చింది. ఇది Governor-General, Council తో పాటు సాధారణ Company ఉద్యోగులను కూడా, వారి official capacity లో చేసిన చర్యలకు Supreme Court jurisdiction నుండి మినహాయించింది, revenue సంబంధిత విషయాలను కూడా Court jurisdiction నుండి పూర్తిగా తీసివేసింది. Pitt's India Act of 1784 & the Act of 1786

The next important act was Pitt's India Act of 1784, introduced by then British Prime Minister William Pitt. It drew a sharp distinction between the Company's commercial functions and its political functions — letting the Court of Directors continue managing commercial affairs, while creating an entirely new body, the Board of Control, to manage political affairs. This established what the book calls a system of double government: two separate authorities running the same territory, one commercial, one political. The Board of Control was empowered to supervise and direct all operations of the civil and military government, and of revenues, in the British possessions in India. The Act was significant for two further reasons: for the first time, the Company's Indian territories were officially called the 'British possessions in India', and the British Government was given supreme control over the Company's affairs and administration. Two years later, in 1786, when Lord Cornwallis was appointed Governor-General of Bengal, he made two conditions for accepting the post — the power to override his council's decisions in special cases, and to simultaneously hold the post of Commander-in-Chief. The Act of 1786 was passed specifically to grant him both.

తర్వాతి ముఖ్యమైన చట్టం — అప్పటి British Prime Minister William Pitt ప్రవేశపెట్టిన Pitt's India Act of 1784. ఇది Company commercial functions, political functions మధ్య స్పష్టమైన తేడాను చూపింది — Court of Directors commercial వ్యవహారాలు కొనసాగించేలా, political వ్యవహారాల కోసం పూర్తిగా కొత్త body — Board of Control — సృష్టించింది. దీనితో పుస్తకం చెప్పే double government వ్యవస్థ మొదలైంది — ఒకే territory ని నడిపే రెండు వేర్వేరు అధికారులు, ఒకటి commercial, మరొకటి political. India లోని British possessions civil, military government, revenues కార్యకలాపాలన్నింటినీ పర్యవేక్షించి నిర్దేశించే అధికారం Board of Control కు ఇచ్చింది. ఈ చట్టం మరో రెండు కారణాలవల్ల ముఖ్యమైనది — Company India territories మొదటిసారి అధికారికంగా 'British possessions in India' అని పిలవబడ్డాయి, Company వ్యవహారాలు, administration పై British Government కు supreme control లభించింది. రెండేళ్ల తర్వాత, 1786లో, Lord Cornwallis ను Bengal Governor-General గా నియమించినప్పుడు, ఆయన పదవి స్వీకరించడానికి రెండు షరతులు పెట్టారు — ప్రత్యేక సందర్భాల్లో తన council నిర్ణయాలను override చేసే అధికారం, అదే సమయంలో Commander-in-Chief పదవి కూడా. Charter Act 1793 & Charter Act 1813
Charter Act of 1793

The Charter Act of 1793 mainly consolidated and extended the arrangements of the previous decade. It extended Cornwallis's override power over his council to all future Governor-Generals and Governors of Presidencies, and gave the Governor-General more control over the subordinate Bombay and Madras Presidencies. It extended the Company's trade monopoly in India for another 20 years, provided that the Commander-in-Chief was not to sit on the Governor-General's council unless specifically appointed, and — notably — laid down that the Board of Control's members and staff were henceforth to be paid out of Indian revenues, rather than by the British Government itself.

Charter Act of 1793 ప్రధానంగా ముందటి దశాబ్దం ఏర్పాట్లను consolidate చేసి విస్తరించింది. Cornwallis కు తన council పై ఉన్న override అధికారాన్ని భవిష్యత్తులోని అన్ని Governor-Generals, Presidency Governors కు విస్తరించింది, Bombay, Madras subordinate presidencies పై Governor-General కు మరింత నియంత్రణ ఇచ్చింది. India లో Company trade monopoly ను మరో 20 సంవత్సరాలు పొడిగించింది, ప్రత్యేకంగా నియమిస్తే తప్ప Commander-in-Chief Governor-General council లో సభ్యుడు కాకూడదని నిర్దేశించింది — ప్రత్యేకంగా గమనించదగినది — Board of Control సభ్యులు, staff జీతాలు ఇక నుండి British Government కు బదులుగా Indian revenues నుండే చెల్లించాలని పేర్కొంది.
Charter Act of 1813

The Charter Act of 1813 marked the first major dent in the Company's commercial privileges: it abolished the Company's trade monopoly in India, throwing Indian trade open to all British merchants — though it notably continued the Company's monopoly over trade in tea and trade with China. It also formally asserted the sovereignty of the British Crown over the Company's Indian territories — a sovereignty claim distinct from actual administration, which the Company still ran. The Act allowed Christian missionaries to come to India "for the purpose of enlightening the people," provided for the spread of western education among British India's inhabitants, and authorised Local Governments to impose taxes on persons, with power to punish non-payment.

Charter Act of 1813 Company commercial ప్రత్యేక హక్కులకు మొదటి పెద్ద దెబ్బ కొట్టింది — ఇది India లో Company trade monopoly ను రద్దు చేసింది, Indian trade ను అందరు British merchants కు తెరిచింది — అయితే గమనించదగినది, tea, China తో వ్యాపారంపై Company monopoly కొనసాగింది. ఇది Company India territories పై British Crown సార్వభౌమత్వాన్ని అధికారికంగా నొక్కి చెప్పింది — వాస్తవ administration ఇంకా Company చేతిలోనే ఉన్నప్పటికీ ఇది వేరైన sovereignty వాదన. ఈ చట్టం Christian missionaries ప్రజలను "enlighten" చేసే ఉద్దేశంతో India రావడానికి అనుమతించింది, British India నివాసుల్లో western education వ్యాప్తికి నిబంధన పెట్టింది, వ్యక్తులపై పన్నులు విధించే అధికారం Local Governments కు ఇచ్చింది, పన్ను చెల్లించనివారిని శిక్షించే అధికారం కూడా ఇచ్చింది.

Charter Act 1833 — Peak Centralisation
Charter Act of 1833 — "the final step towards centralisation"

The book calls the Charter Act of 1833 "the final step towards centralisation" in British India, and its provisions justify the label. It elevated the Governor-General of Bengal to Governor-General of India, vesting him with all civil and military powers — for the first time, one office held authority over the entire territory the British possessed in India; Lord William Bentinck became the first Governor-General of India under this title. Bombay and Madras were stripped of their legislative powers entirely, with exclusive legislative authority for the whole of British India now resting with the Governor-General of India — and from this point on, laws made under the new dispensation were called 'Acts', while those made under previous arrangements were retroactively termed 'Regulations'. The Act also ended the Company's role as a commercial body altogether, turning it into a purely administrative body holding Indian territories "in trust" for the Crown — completing the process the 1813 Act had begun. It also attempted, for the first time, to introduce open competition for selecting civil servants and to state that Indians should not be barred from any office under the Company — though this specific provision was defeated by opposition from the Company's own Court of Directors and did not take effect.

పుస్తకం Charter Act of 1833 ను British India లో "the final step towards centralisation" అని పిలుస్తుంది, దీని నిబంధనలు ఈ పేరును సమర్థిస్తాయి. ఇది Bengal Governor-General ను Governor-General of India గా ఎదిగించింది, అన్ని civil, military అధికారాలు ఆయనకు ఇచ్చింది — మొదటిసారి, British India భూభాగం మొత్తంపై ఒకే పదవికి అధికారం వచ్చింది; ఈ బిరుదులో మొదటి Governor-General of India — Lord William Bentinck. Bombay, Madras తమ legislative powers పూర్తిగా కోల్పోయాయి, British India మొత్తానికి exclusive legislative అధికారం ఇప్పుడు Governor-General of India వద్దే ఉంది — ఇక్కడి నుండి కొత్త ఏర్పాటు కింద చేసిన చట్టాలను 'Acts' అని, ముందటి ఏర్పాట్ల కింద చేసినవాటిని retroactively 'Regulations' అని పిలిచేవారు. ఈ చట్టం Company commercial body పాత్రను పూర్తిగా ముగించింది, దాన్ని Crown కోసం 'trust లో' Indian territories ఉంచుకునే పూర్తి administrative body గా మార్చింది — 1813 చట్టం మొదలుపెట్టిన ప్రక్రియను ఇది పూర్తి చేసింది. civil servants ఎంపికకు open competition ప్రవేశపెట్టడానికి, Company కింద ఏ పదవి నుండైనా Indians ను దూరంగా ఉంచకూడదని పేర్కొనడానికి మొదటిసారి ప్రయత్నించింది — అయితే ఈ నిర్దిష్ట నిబంధన Company సొంత Court of Directors వ్యతిరేకత వల్ల ఓడిపోయింది, అమలుకాలేదు.

Charter Act 1853 & the End of Company Rule
Charter Act of 1853 — the Last of the Charter Acts

The Charter Act of 1853 was the last in the series of Charter Acts passed between 1793 and 1853, and the book calls it a significant constitutional landmark. For the first time, it separated the legislative and executive functions of the Governor-General's council, adding 6 new members called legislative councillors and thereby establishing a separate Indian (Central) Legislative Council — a body that functioned like a mini-Parliament, following the same procedures as the British Parliament. In the book's words, legislation was for the first time treated as a special function of government, requiring dedicated machinery and process, rather than something an all-purpose executive council handled alongside everything else. The Act also introduced an open competition system for selecting and recruiting civil servants, throwing the covenanted civil service open to Indians too — the Macaulay Committee (the Committee on the Indian Civil Service) was duly appointed in 1854 to work out the details. It extended Company rule but — unlike every previous Charter — did not specify a fixed period for it, a clear signal that Parliament could terminate Company rule whenever it chose. And it introduced, for the first time, an element of local representation in the legislative council — of the six new legislative members, four were appointed by the local (provincial) governments of Madras, Bombay, Bengal and Agra.

Charter Act of 1853, 1793-1853 మధ్య వచ్చిన Charter Acts క్రమంలో చివరిది — పుస్తకం దీన్ని ఒక ముఖ్యమైన constitutional landmark అని పిలుస్తుంది. ఇది Governor-General council legislative, executive functions ను మొదటిసారి వేరు చేసింది, 6 మంది కొత్త legislative councillors చేర్చి, ప్రత్యేక Indian (Central) Legislative Council ఏర్పాటు చేసింది — ఇది British Parliament విధానాలనే అనుసరిస్తూ mini-Parliament లా పనిచేసేది. పుస్తకం మాటల్లో చెప్పాలంటే, legislation మొదటిసారి ప్రభుత్వం యొక్క ప్రత్యేక function గా పరిగణించబడింది, దీనికి ప్రత్యేక machinery, ప్రక్రియ అవసరం — అన్నీ చూసుకునే executive council ఒక భాగంగా కాదు. ఈ చట్టం civil servants ఎంపిక, నియామకానికి open competition పద్ధతి ప్రవేశపెట్టింది, covenanted civil service ను Indians కు కూడా తెరిచింది — వివరాలు రూపొందించడానికి 1854లో Macaulay Committee (Committee on the Indian Civil Service) నియమించారు. The Crown Rule Begins — Government of India Act, 1858
Government of India Act of 1858

This landmark Act was enacted in the wake of the Revolt of 1857 — also known as the First War of Independence, or the 'sepoy mutiny'. Officially titled the Act for the Good Government of India, it abolished the East India Company altogether, transferring its powers of government, its territories and its revenues to the British Crown. India was henceforth to be governed by, and in the name of, Her Majesty; the title Governor-General of India was changed to Viceroy of India, the direct representative of the British Crown — Lord Canning thereby became the first Viceroy of India. The Act ended the system of double government by abolishing both the Board of Control and the Court of Directors, and created an entirely new office — Secretary of State for India — vested with complete authority over Indian administration; this Secretary of State was a member of the British Cabinet, responsible ultimately to the British Parliament. A 15-member Council of India was established to assist him in an advisory capacity, with the Secretary of State as its Chairman; the Secretary of State-in-Council was also constituted as a body corporate, capable of suing and being sued in both India and England.

ఈ landmark చట్టం Revolt of 1857 తర్వాత వచ్చింది — దీన్నే First War of Independence లేదా 'sepoy mutiny' అని కూడా అంటారు. అధికారికంగా Act for the Good Government of India గా పిలిచే ఈ చట్టం, East India Company ను పూర్తిగా రద్దు చేసింది, దాని Government, territories, revenues అధికారాలను British Crown కు బదిలీ చేసింది. ఇక India ను Her Majesty పేరుతో, ఆమె తరపునే పరిపాలించాలి; Governor-General of India బిరుదు Viceroy of India గా మారింది — British Crown ప్రత్యక్ష ప్రతినిధి — దీనితో Lord Canning మొదటి Viceroy of India అయ్యారు. ఈ చట్టం Board of Control, Court of Directors రెండింటినీ రద్దు చేసి double government వ్యవస్థను ముగించింది, Indian administration పై పూర్తి అధికారం కలిగిన కొత్త పదవి — Secretary of State for India — సృష్టించింది; ఈ Secretary of State British Cabinet సభ్యుడు, చివరకు British Parliament కు జవాబుదారీగా ఉంటారు. ఆయనకు సహాయంగా advisory capacity లో 15 మంది సభ్యుల Council of India ఏర్పాటు చేసింది, Secretary of State దానికి Chairman గా ఉన్నారు; Secretary of State-in-Council ను కూడా body corporate గా చేసింది — India, England రెండింటిలోనూ దావా వేయగలదు, దావాకు గురికాగలదు.

The book pointedly notes — drawing on constitutional historian M.V. Pylee's assessment — that this Act, for all its dramatic transfer of sovereignty, actually changed very little about how India was administered day-to-day: it left the machinery of governance within India largely untouched, confining itself mainly to improving how that machinery was superintended and controlled from England.

Indian Councils Act 1861 & 1892 — First Steps toward Representation
Indian Councils Act of 1861

After the great revolt of 1857, the British Government felt it necessary to seek the cooperation of Indians in administering their own country. This policy of association produced three Acts — in 1861, 1892 and 1909 — and the Indian Councils Act of 1861 is an important landmark among them. It made a beginning of representative institutions by associating Indians with the law-making process: the Viceroy was to nominate some Indians as non-official members of his expanded council, and in 1862, Lord Canning nominated three Indians to his legislative council — the Raja of Benaras, the Maharaja of Patiala and Sir Dinkar Rao. The Act also initiated a process of decentralisation by restoring legislative powers to the Bombay and Madras Presidencies — directly reversing the centralising tendency that had begun with the Regulating Act of 1773 and reached its climax under the Charter Act of 1833. This shift toward legislative devolution eventually resulted in the grant of almost complete internal autonomy to the provinces by 1937. It provided for new legislative councils in Bengal, the North-western Provinces and Punjab (established in 1862, 1886 and 1897 respectively), empowered the Viceroy to make procedural rules for council business, gave formal recognition to the 'portfolio' system Lord Canning had introduced in 1859 (each council member in charge of specific departments, authorised to issue final orders on their behalf), and empowered the Viceroy to issue emergency ordinances without the legislative council's concurrence, valid for up to 6 months.

1857 తిరుగుబాటు తర్వాత, తమ దేశ administration లో Indians సహకారం తీసుకోవాల్సిన అవసరాన్ని British Government భావించింది. ఈ association policy మూడు చట్టాలకు దారితీసింది — 1861, 1892, 1909 లలో — వీటిలో Indian Councils Act of 1861 ముఖ్యమైన landmark. Indians ను law-making ప్రక్రియలో భాగస్వాములను చేస్తూ representative institutions ప్రారంభమయ్యాయి — Viceroy తన expanded council లో కొంతమంది Indians ను non-official members గా నామినేట్ చేయాలి, 1862లో Lord Canning ముగ్గురు Indians ను తన legislative council కు నామినేట్ చేశారు — Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao. Bombay, Madras Presidencies కు legislative powers తిరిగి ఇచ్చి decentralisation ప్రక్రియను కూడా ఈ చట్టం ప్రారంభించింది — Regulating Act of 1773 నుండి మొదలై Charter Act of 1833 దాకా చేరుకున్న centralising ధోరణిని ఇది నేరుగా reverse చేసింది. legislative devolution వైపు ఈ మార్పు చివరకు 1937 నాటికి provinces కు almost complete internal autonomy తీసుకొచ్చింది. ఇది Bengal, North-western Provinces, Punjab లో కొత్త legislative councils (వరుసగా 1862, 1886, 1897లలో స్థాపించారు) కోసం నిబంధన పెట్టింది, council వ్యవహారాల కోసం procedural rules చేసే అధికారం Viceroy కు ఇచ్చింది, Lord Canning 1859లో ప్రవేశపెట్టిన 'portfolio' వ్యవస్థకు (నిర్దిష్ట departments చూసే council member, వాటి తరపున తుది ఆర్డర్లు ఇచ్చే అధికారం) అధికారిక గుర్తింపు ఇచ్చింది, legislative council concurrence లేకుండా, 6 నెలల వరకూ చెల్లుబాటయ్యే emergency ordinances జారీ చేసే అధికారం Viceroy కు ఇచ్చింది.
Indian Councils Act of 1892

The Indian Councils Act of 1892 was a cautious next step. It increased the number of additional (non-official) members in the Central and provincial legislative councils, but carefully maintained the official majority in them — Indians and other non-officials still could not outvote the government's own appointees. It expanded the councils' functions to include discussing the budget and addressing questions to the executive, and provided for nomination of non-official members: to the Central Legislative Council by the Viceroy, on the recommendation of provincial councils and the Bengal Chamber of Commerce; and to provincial councils by the Governors, on the recommendation of district boards, municipalities, universities, trade associations, zamindars and chambers. The book notes, quoting scholar V.N. Shukla, that this arrangement amounted to a limited and indirect step toward election in substance, even though the Act's text deliberately avoided the term "election" itself, describing the process only as nomination made on the recommendation of certain bodies.

Central, provincial legislative councils లో additional (non-official) members సంఖ్యను పెంచింది, కానీ వాటిలో official majority ను జాగ్రత్తగా కొనసాగించింది — Indians, ఇతర non-officials ఇప్పటికీ government సొంత appointees ను ఓటుతో ఓడించలేకపోయేవారు. budget చర్చించడం, executive కు ప్రశ్నలు అడగడం వంటివి చేర్చి councils functions ను విస్తరించింది, non-official members నామినేషన్ కోసం నిబంధన పెట్టింది — Central Legislative Council కోసం Viceroy, provincial councils, Bengal Chamber of Commerce సిఫారసుపై; provincial councils కోసం Governors, district boards, municipalities, universities, trade associations, zamindars, chambers సిఫారసుపై. పుస్తకం, scholar V.N. Indian Councils Act 1909 — Morley-Minto Reforms & Separate Electorates
Indian Councils Act of 1909 — Morley-Minto Reforms

The Indian Councils Act of 1909, popularly called the Morley-Minto Reforms after Lord Morley (Secretary of State for India) and Lord Minto (Viceroy of India), considerably increased the size of the legislative councils at both levels — the Central legislative council's membership rose from 16 to 60, while provincial council sizes varied. It retained the official majority in the Central council, but allowed the provincial legislative councils to have a non-official majority for the first time. It enlarged the councils' deliberative functions — members could now ask supplementary questions and move resolutions on the budget — and provided, for the first time, for the association of Indians with the executive councils of the Viceroy and Governors: Satyendra Prasad Sinha became the first Indian to join the Viceroy's executive council, appointed as Law Member. Most consequentially, it introduced a system of communal representation for Muslims by accepting the concept of a 'separate electorate' — Muslim members were now to be elected only by Muslim voters. As the book bluntly puts it, this act 'legalised communalism', and Lord Minto came to be known as the Father of Communal Electorate. Government of India Act of 1919 — Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms

On August 20, 1917, the British Government declared, for the first time, that its objective was the gradual introduction of responsible Government in India. The Government of India Act of 1919, which followed, came into force in 1921 and is popularly known as the Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (Montagu was Secretary of State, Lord Chelmsford the Viceroy). It classified all subjects of administration into central and provincial subjects through the 'Devolution Rules' framed under the Act, facilitating delegation of authority from the Centre to the provinces — though the overall structure of government remained centralised and unitary. Provincial subjects were further split into transferred subjects (public health, education, local self-government, agriculture etc. — administered by the Governor with ministers responsible to the legislative council) and reserved subjects (police, administration of justice, prisons, land revenue, finance etc. — administered by the Governor and his executive council without any responsibility to the legislative council). This dual scheme was called 'dyarchy' — from the Greek di-arche, meaning double rule — and the book records that the experiment was largely unsuccessful. The Act introduced, for the first time, bicameralism and direct elections — replacing the Indian legislative council with a bicameral legislature comprising an Upper House (Council of State) and a Lower House (Legislative Assembly), the majority of whose members were now chosen by direct election.

In November 1927 — two years ahead of the schedule the 1919 Act had itself prescribed — the British Government announced the appointment of a 7-member statutory commission under the chairmanship of Sir John Simon, tasked with reporting on India's condition under the 1919 Act. Government of India Act 1935 — "Blueprint" of the Constitution
Government of India Act of 1935

The Government of India Act of 1935 marked a second milestone towards a completely responsible government in India — a lengthy, detailed document running to 321 Sections and 10 Schedules. It provided for an All-India Federation of provinces and princely states as constituent units, dividing powers between the Centre and the units through 3 lists — the Federal List (for the Centre, 59 items), the Provincial List (for the provinces, 54 items) and the Concurrent List (for both, 36 items), with residuary powers given to the Viceroy — though the federation itself never actually came into being, since the princely states declined to join it. It abolished dyarchy in the provinces and introduced 'provincial autonomy' in its place: provinces now acted as autonomous units within defined spheres, with the Governor required to act on the advice of ministers responsible to the provincial legislature (in effect from 1937, discontinued 1939). It also provided for the adoption of dyarchy at the Centre — splitting federal subjects into reserved and transferred categories — though, since the federation itself never formed, this particular provision never came into operation at all.

On June 3, 1947, the British Government made clear that any Constitution framed by the Constituent Assembly of India (formed in 1946) could not apply to parts of the country unwilling to accept it — and on that same day, Lord Mountbatten, the Viceroy of India, put forth the partition plan known as the Mountbatten Plan, accepted by both the Congress and the Muslim League. Immediate legal effect was given to this plan through the Indian Independence Act, 1947.

ఫిబ్రవరి 20, 1947న, British Prime Minister Clement Atlee — British rule India లో జూన్ 30, 1948 నాటికి ముగుస్తుందని, ఆ తర్వాత అధికారం responsible Indian చేతులకు బదిలీ అవుతుందని ప్రకటించారు. ఈ ప్రకటన తర్వాత Muslim League partition డిమాండ్ చేస్తూ agitation తీవ్రతరమైంది. జూన్ 3, 1947న, British Government — Constituent Assembly of India (1946లో ఏర్పడింది) రూపొందించే ఏ Constitution అయినా, దాన్ని అంగీకరించని దేశ భాగాలకు వర్తించదని స్పష్టం చేసింది — అదే రోజు, Viceroy of India Lord Mountbatten, Mountbatten Plan అని పిలిచే partition ప్రణాళికను ప్రతిపాదించారు, దీన్ని Congress, Muslim League రెండూ అంగీకరించాయి. ఈ ప్రణాళికకు Indian Independence Act, 1947 ద్వారా తక్షణ చట్టపరమైన ప్రభావం ఇచ్చారు.

The Act's key features: it ended British rule and declared India an independent state from August 15, 1947; provided for partition and the creation of two independent dominions, India and Pakistan, each with the right to secede from the British Commonwealth; abolished the office of Viceroy, providing each dominion a Governor-General appointed by the British King on the advice of the dominion's cabinet, with His Majesty's Government in Britain bearing no responsibility for either government; empowered both dominions' Constituent Assemblies to frame and adopt any constitution and to repeal any British Act (including the Independence Act itself); authorised both dominions to legislate for their own territories until new constitutions were enforced; abolished the office of Secretary of State for India, transferring its functions to the Secretary of State for Commonwealth Affairs; proclaimed the lapse of British paramountcy over princely states and treaty relations with tribal areas from 15 August 1947; granted princely states freedom to join either dominion or remain independent; provided that each dominion and its provinces would continue to be governed under the Government of India Act, 1935 until new constitutions were framed (subject to modification by the dominions); removed the British Monarch's power to veto or reserve bills, vesting that power instead in the Governor-General; designated the Governor-General and provincial governors as constitutional (nominal) heads bound to act on ministerial advice; dropped 'Emperor of India' from the King's royal titles; and discontinued the Secretary of State's role in civil service appointments, while protecting the entitlements of those already appointed.

At the stroke of midnight of 14–15 August 1947, British rule came to an end and power was transferred to the two new independent Dominions. Lord Mountbatten became the first Governor-General of the new Dominion of India, and swore in Jawaharlal Nehru as independent India's first Prime Minister. The Constituent Assembly of India, formed in 1946, itself became the Parliament of the Indian Dominion. The boundaries between the two Dominions were determined by a Boundary Commission headed by Radcliffe.

The Demand for a Constituent Assembly

The idea that India should write its own Constitution through a dedicated Constituent Assembly, rather than have one imposed by the British Parliament, took shape gradually over more than a decade. M.N. Roy, a pioneer of the communist movement in India, was the first to float the idea, in 1934. The Indian National Congress (INC) made it an official demand in 1935, and in 1938 Jawaharlal Nehru, speaking for the INC, sharpened the demand into a specific procedural condition: the Constitution of free India had to be framed, without outside interference, by an assembly elected on adult franchise — a position the INC reaffirmed by resolution in 1939. The British Government accepted the demand only in principle, in the 'August Offer' of 1940. In 1942, Sir Stafford Cripps brought a concrete draft proposal (the Cripps Proposals) for a post-war constitution-making process, but the Muslim League rejected it because it did not offer what the League wanted — two separate Constituent Assemblies for two separate autonomous states. Finally, a three-member Cabinet Mission was sent to India in 1946; it rejected the two-Assembly idea outright, but designed a scheme for a single Assembly that was crafted to more or less satisfy the League's underlying concerns about safeguards for Muslim representation.

Roy (India communist movement pioneer) 1934లో మొదటిసారి ఈ ఆలోచనను ప్రతిపాదించారు. Composition of the Constituent Assembly

The Assembly was constituted in November 1946 under the Cabinet Mission Plan's scheme. Its total strength was fixed at 389: 296 seats for British India (292 from the 11 governors' provinces and 4 from the four Chief Commissioners' provinces, one each) and 93 for the princely states. Seats were allotted roughly in proportion to population (about one seat per million), and each province's seats were further sub-divided among three communities — Muslims, Sikhs, and General (everyone else) — again in proportion to population, with each community's own representatives in the provincial legislative assembly electing its bloc's members by proportional representation via the single transferable vote (STV — voters rank candidates by preference instead of casting a single vote). Princely-state representatives, by contrast, were simply nominated by the rulers of those states. The Assembly was, therefore, a partly elected, partly nominated body, and even its "elected" members were indirectly elected — chosen by the members of provincial legislative assemblies, who themselves had been elected under the limited franchise (tax and property qualifications) of the Government of India Act, 1935. In the actual elections (July–August 1946, for the 296 British Indian seats), Congress won 208 seats, the Muslim League 73, and small groups/independents the remaining 15; the 93 princely-state seats stayed vacant, as those states chose to stay away altogether. Even so, the Assembly ended up including representatives of every section of Indian society — Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs, Parsis, Anglo-Indians, Indian Christians, Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and women from all these groups — and virtually every leading Indian political figure of the time, with the notable exception of Mahatma Gandhi.

Assembly నవంబర్ 1946లో Cabinet Mission Plan scheme కింద ఏర్పాటైంది. మొత్తం strength 389 గా నిర్ణయించారు — 296 సీట్లు British India కు (292 11 governors' provinces నుండి, 4 నాలుగు Chief Commissioners' provinces నుండి, ఒక్కొక్కటి) మరియు 93 princely states కు. సీట్లు దాదాపు జనాభా ప్రాతిపదికన కేటాయించబడ్డాయి (సుమారు ప్రతి 10 లక్షలకు ఒక సీటు), ప్రతి province సీట్లు మళ్ళీ మూడు communities మధ్య (Muslims, Sikhs, General) జనాభా ప్రాతిపదికన విభజించబడ్డాయి — ఆయా community సభ్యులే provincial legislative assembly లో proportional representation (single transferable vote — ఓటర్లు ఒకే candidate కు కాకుండా, candidates కు ప్రాధాన్యత క్రమంలో ఓటు వేసే పద్ధతి) ద్వారా తమ bloc సభ్యులను ఎన్నుకున్నారు. Princely-state representatives ను మాత్రం ఆయా రాజులు కేవలం నామినేట్ చేశారు. కాబట్టి Assembly పాక్షికంగా ఎన్నికైనది, పాక్షికంగా నామినేట్ అయినది — "ఎన్నికైన" సభ్యులు కూడా పరోక్షంగా ఎన్నికయ్యారు — provincial legislative assembly సభ్యులే వారిని ఎన్నుకున్నారు, ఆ సభ్యులే Government of India Act, 1935 limited franchise (tax, property qualifications) పై ఎన్నికయ్యారు. వాస్తవ ఎన్నికల్లో (జూలై-ఆగస్టు 1946, 296 British Indian సీట్లకు), Congress 208, Muslim League 73, చిన్న గ్రూపులు/independents 15 సీట్లు గెలిచారు; princely states 93 సీట్లు ఖాళీగా ఉండిపోయాయి, ఆ రాష్ట్రాలు పూర్తిగా దూరంగా ఉండాలని నిర్ణయించుకున్నాయి. అయినా, Assembly చివరకు India సమాజంలోని ప్రతి వర్గం ప్రతినిధులను కలిగి ఉంది — Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs, Parsis, Anglo-Indians, Indian Christians, SCs, STs, ఈ వర్గాలన్నింటి మహిళలు — ఆనాటి దాదాపు ప్రతి ప్రధాన రాజకీయ నాయకుడూ ఇందులో ఉన్నారు, Mahatma Gandhi మాత్రం మినహాయింపు.

Working of the Assembly & the Objectives Resolution
Working of the Constituent Assembly

The Assembly held its first meeting on December 9, 1946. The Muslim League boycotted it, still insisting on a separate Pakistan, so only 207 members were actually present. Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha, the oldest member present, was elected temporary Chairman, following the French practice of letting the most senior member preside until a permanent Chairman is chosen. That happened two days later — on December 11, 1946, Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected the Assembly's permanent Chairman (President), and on January 25, 1947, H.C. Mukherjee was elected Vice-President. The Assembly later decided it needed two Vice-Presidents, and on July 16, 1947, V.T. Krishnamachari was elected as the second one.

Assembly మొదటి సమావేశం డిసెంబర్ 9, 1946 న జరిగింది. Muslim League ఇంకా ప్రత్యేక Pakistan కోసం పట్టుబడుతూ దీన్ని బహిష్కరించింది — కాబట్టి 207 మంది సభ్యులు మాత్రమే హాజరయ్యారు. హాజరైన సభ్యుల్లో అత్యంత వృద్ధుడైన Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha — French practice ప్రకారం (శాశ్వత Chairman ఎన్నికయ్యే వరకూ senior-most సభ్యుడు అధ్యక్షత వహించడం) — తాత్కాలిక Chairman గా ఎన్నికయ్యారు. రెండు రోజుల తర్వాత — డిసెంబర్ 11, 1946 న Dr. Rajendra Prasad Assembly శాశ్వత Chairman (President) గా ఎన్నికయ్యారు, జనవరి 25, 1947 న H.C. Mukherjee Vice-President గా ఎన్నికయ్యారు. తర్వాత Assembly ఇద్దరు Vice-Presidents ఉండాలని నిర్ణయించింది — జూలై 16, 1947 న V.T. Krishnamachari రెండో Vice-President గా ఎన్నికయ్యారు.
Objectives Resolution

On December 13, 1946 — just four days after the Assembly's very first meeting, and long before any drafting committee existed — Jawaharlal Nehru moved the historic Objectives Resolution. It did not attempt to write actual constitutional text; instead it laid down the fundamentals and philosophy that the eventual Constitution should embody: India to be declared an Independent Sovereign Republic; a Union formed of British India's territories, the Indian princely states, and any other willing territories; the constituent units to retain autonomy and residuary powers (powers not explicitly assigned anywhere, left by default with the units); all power and authority of government to be derived from the people; justice, equality of status and opportunity, and freedoms of thought, expression, belief, faith, worship, vocation, association and action to be guaranteed to everyone, subject to law and public morality; adequate safeguards to be provided for minorities, backward and tribal areas, and depressed and other backward classes; the territorial integrity of the Republic to be maintained; and India to take its rightful place in the world and contribute to world peace. The Resolution was adopted unanimously on January 22, 1947. It went on to influence the shaping of the Constitution through every subsequent stage of drafting, and its modified version eventually became the Preamble of the Constitution as we know it today.

డిసెంబర్ 13, 1946 న — Assembly మొదటి సమావేశం జరిగిన నాలుగే రోజుల్లో, ఏ drafting committee ఏర్పాటుకాకముందే — Nehru చారిత్రక Objectives Resolution ప్రవేశపెట్టారు. ఇది వాస్తవ constitutional text రాయలేదు — బదులుగా, రాబోయే Constitution కలిగి ఉండాల్సిన fundamentals, philosophy ను నిర్దేశించింది — India ను Independent Sovereign Republic గా ప్రకటించడం; British India ప్రాంతాలు, Indian princely states, ఇష్టపడే ఇతర ప్రాంతాలతో కూడిన Union; units కు autonomy, residuary powers (ప్రత్యేకంగా ఎక్కడా కేటాయించని అధికారాలు, డిఫాల్ట్ గా units దగ్గరే ఉండేవి); ప్రభుత్వ అధికారం అంతా ప్రజల నుండే రావడం; అందరికీ justice, status-opportunity సమానత్వం, ఆలోచన-భావ వ్యక్తీకరణ-విశ్వాసం-ఆరాధన-వృత్తి-సంఘం-కార్యాచరణ స్వేచ్ఛలు (చట్టం, public morality కు లోబడి); minorities, backward/tribal areas, depressed/backward classes కు తగిన రక్షణలు; Republic territorial integrity కాపాడటం; India ప్రపంచంలో తన రైతైన స్థానం పొంది world peace కు తోడ్పడటం. Resolution జనవరి 22, 1947 న ఏకగ్రీవంగా ఆమోదించబడింది. తర్వాతి ప్రతి drafting దశనూ ఇది ప్రభావితం చేసింది, దీని సవరించిన రూపమే నేటి Preamble గా మారింది.

Indian Independence Act, 1947 & Other Functions of the Assembly
Changes by the Indian Independence Act, 1947

Princely-state representatives, who had initially stayed away, gradually began joining the Assembly — six states had joined by April 28, 1947, and after the Mountbatten Plan (June 3, 1947) for partition was accepted, most of the remaining princely states, along with the Muslim League members from the territory that would remain in the Indian Dominion, also took their seats. The Indian Independence Act, 1947 then made three structural changes to the Assembly's status. First, it made the Assembly a fully sovereign body — free to frame any Constitution it wished, and empowered to abrogate or alter any law the British Parliament had made in relation to India. Second, it turned the Assembly into a legislative body as well — it now had two distinct jobs (constitution-making, and enacting ordinary laws for the country), performed on separate days, making it effectively the first Parliament of free India (the Dominion Legislature). Whenever it met to make the Constitution, it was chaired by Dr. Rajendra Prasad; whenever it met as the ordinary legislature, it was chaired by G.V. Mavalankar. Both functions continued side by side until November 26, 1949, when the constitution-making task was finished. Third, Muslim League members from the areas that went to Pakistan withdrew from the Assembly altogether — as a result, its total strength fell from 389 to 299 (British Indian provinces from 296 to 229, princely states from 93 to 70), as recorded as on December 31, 1947.

మొదట్లో దూరంగా ఉన్న princely-state ప్రతినిధులు క్రమంగా Assembly లో చేరసాగారు — ఏప్రిల్ 28, 1947 నాటికి ఆరు రాష్ట్రాలు చేరాయి, Mountbatten Plan (జూన్ 3, 1947) partition ఆమోదం పొందాక, మిగతా ఎక్కువ princely states, Indian Dominion లో ఉండే ప్రాంతాల Muslim League సభ్యులు కూడా చేరారు. తర్వాత Indian Independence Act, 1947 Assembly స్థితిలో మూడు structural మార్పులు చేసింది. మొదటిది — Assembly పూర్తిగా sovereign అయ్యింది — తనకు నచ్చిన ఏ Constitution నైనా రూపొందించే స్వేచ్ఛ, India కు సంబంధించి British Parliament చేసిన ఏ చట్టాన్నైనా మార్చే/రద్దు చేసే అధికారం. రెండోది — Assembly ఒక legislative body గా కూడా మారింది — ఇప్పుడు రెండు వేర్వేరు పనులు (constitution-making, సాధారణ చట్టాల enact చేయడం) వేర్వేరు రోజుల్లో చేసేది, ఇది సమర్థవంతంగా స్వేచ్ఛా India మొదటి Parliament (Dominion Legislature) అయ్యింది. Constitution చేసేటప్పుడు Rajendra Prasad అధ్యక్షత వహించారు; సాధారణ legislature గా కలిసినప్పుడు G.V. Mavalankar అధ్యక్షత వహించారు. Constitution-making పని పూర్తయ్యే నవంబర్ 26, 1949 వరకూ రెండు విధులు కలిసి కొనసాగాయి. మూడోది — Pakistan లో కలిసే ప్రాంతాల Muslim League సభ్యులు పూర్తిగా Assembly నుండి వైదొలిగారు — దీనివల్ల మొత్తం strength 389 నుండి 299 కు తగ్గింది (British provinces 296→229, princely states 93→70), డిసెంబర్ 31, 1947 నాటి లెక్క ప్రకారం.
Other Functions Performed & Session Statistics

Beyond making the Constitution and enacting ordinary laws, the Assembly performed several other functions: it ratified India's membership of the Commonwealth in May 1949; adopted the national flag on July 22, 1947; adopted the national anthem and the national song, both on January 24, 1950; and elected Dr. Rajendra Prasad as the first President of India, also on January 24, 1950. In all, the Assembly held 11 sessions spread over 2 years, 11 months and 18 days. Its members went through the constitutions of around 60 countries while working, and the Draft Constitution alone was under consideration for 114 days; the entire exercise cost about ₹64 lakh. The Assembly's final formal session was on January 24, 1950 — but it did not simply dissolve. It continued to function as the provisional parliament of India from January 26, 1950 until a newly elected Parliament took over after the country's first general elections of 1951–52.

Constitution చేయడం, సాధారణ చట్టాలు enact చేయడంతో పాటు, Assembly ఇతర పలు విధులు నిర్వర్తించింది — India Commonwealth సభ్యత్వాన్ని మే 1949 లో ratify చేసింది; national flag ను జూలై 22, 1947 న ఆమోదించింది; national anthem, national song రెండింటినీ జనవరి 24, 1950 న ఆమోదించింది; Dr. Rajendra Prasad ను మొదటి India President గా, అదే జనవరి 24, 1950 న ఎన్నుకుంది. మొత్తం మీద, Assembly 11 సెషన్లు, 2 సంవత్సరాల 11 నెలల 18 రోజుల పాటు జరిగింది. సభ్యులు పనిచేస్తున్నప్పుడు సుమారు 60 దేశాల constitutions ను పరిశీలించారు, Draft Constitution ఒక్కటే 114 రోజులు చర్చకు వచ్చింది; మొత్తం ఖర్చు సుమారు ₹64 లక్షలు. Assembly తుది అధికారిక session జనవరి 24, 1950 న జరిగింది — కానీ అది కేవలం రద్దు కాలేదు. జనవరి 26, 1950 నుండి, 1951-52 మొదటి సాధారణ ఎన్నికల తర్వాత కొత్తగా ఎన్నికైన Parliament బాధ్యతలు తీసుకునేవరకూ, ఇది India provisional parliament గా కొనసాగింది.

Committees of the Constituent Assembly
Committees — Major and Minor

To handle the different tasks of constitution-making, the Assembly appointed a large number of committees — 8 of these were classified as major committees, and the rest as minor ones. The major committees, together with their chairmen, were: the Union Powers Committee and the Union Constitution Committee (both chaired by Jawaharlal Nehru); the Provincial Constitution Committee (Sardar Patel); the Drafting Committee (Dr. B.R. Ambedkar); the Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal and Excluded Areas (Sardar Patel), which itself had five further sub-committees (Fundamental Rights — J.B. Kripalani; Minorities — H.C. Mukherjee; North-East Frontier Tribal Areas and Assam Excluded Areas — Gopinath Bardoloi; Excluded Areas other than Assam — A.V. Thakkar; North-West Frontier Tribal Areas); the Rules of Procedure Committee and the Steering Committee (both Dr. Rajendra Prasad); and the States Committee for negotiating with the princely states (Jawaharlal Nehru). The minor committees — thirteen in number — handled more specific administrative or procedural tasks, and interestingly, a few of their chairmen (S. Varadachari, Nalini Ranjan Sarkar, S.K. Dar) were not even members of the Assembly — they were outside experts brought in for their specialised knowledge.

Constitution-making వివిధ పనుల కోసం, Assembly అనేక committees ఏర్పాటు చేసింది — వాటిలో 8 major committees గా వర్గీకరించారు, మిగతావి minor. The Drafting Committee & Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
The Drafting Committee

Among all the committees of the Assembly, the single most important one was the Drafting Committee, set up on August 29, 1947 — barely two weeks after independence — and entrusted with the actual task of preparing a draft of the new Constitution. It consisted of 7 members: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as Chairman, along with N. Gopalaswamy Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar, Dr. K.M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad Saadullah, N. Madhava Rau (who replaced B.L. Mitter after Mitter resigned owing to ill-health), and T.T. Krishnamachari (who replaced D.P. Khaitan after Khaitan died in 1948). Taking into account the proposals already worked out by the various other committees, the Drafting Committee prepared a first draft of the Constitution, published in February 1948; the public was then given eight months to discuss it and propose amendments. In light of the public comments, criticisms and suggestions received, the Committee then prepared a second draft, published in October 1948. Remarkably, despite producing the document that would define India's entire legal-political order, the Committee took less than six months of actual working time to prepare its draft — it sat for only 141 days in total.

Enactment of the Constitution
Enactment — the Three Readings

The Constitution was formally enacted through a three-stage process, much like an ordinary bill. In the first reading, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar introduced the final draft in the Assembly on November 4, 1948, and the House held a general discussion on it for five days, until November 9, 1948. The second reading — clause-by-clause consideration — was by far the longest stage, running from November 15, 1948 to October 17, 1949; during this stage as many as 7,653 amendments were proposed, of which 2,473 were actually taken up and discussed in the Assembly. The third reading began on November 14, 1949, when Ambedkar moved the concluding motion — that "the Constitution as settled by the Assembly be passed." That motion was declared passed on November 26, 1949, and the Constitution then received the signatures of the members and the President. Of the Assembly's total 299 members, only 284 were actually present that day to sign. November 26, 1949 is also the very date the Preamble itself names as the day the people of India, through the Constituent Assembly, adopted, enacted and gave the Constitution to themselves. As adopted, the Constitution contained a Preamble, 395 Articles and 8 Schedules — and, notably, the Preamble was enacted only after the entire rest of the Constitution had already been enacted.

Constitution ఒక సాధారణ bill లాగే మూడు-దశల ప్రక్రియ ద్వారా అధికారికంగా enact చేయబడింది. First reading లో Ambedkar నవంబర్ 4, 1948 న final draft ను Assembly లో ప్రవేశపెట్టారు, House దానిపై 5 రోజుల పాటు (నవంబర్ 9, 1948 వరకూ) general discussion జరిపింది. Second reading — clause-by-clause పరిశీలన — అత్యంత సుదీర్ఘమైన దశ, నవంబర్ 15, 1948 నుండి అక్టోబర్ 17, 1949 వరకూ కొనసాగింది; ఈ దశలో 7,653 amendments ప్రతిపాదించగా, వాటిలో 2,473 మాత్రమే Assembly లో చర్చించారు. Third reading నవంబర్ 14, 1949 న ప్రారంభమైంది, అప్పుడు Ambedkar చివరి motion ప్రవేశపెట్టారు — "Assembly settle చేసిన Constitution పాస్ చేయాలి" అని. ఈ motion నవంబర్ 26, 1949 న పాస్ అయినట్టు ప్రకటించారు, తర్వాత Constitution సభ్యులు, President సంతకాలు పొందింది. మొత్తం 299 సభ్యుల్లో 284 మంది మాత్రమే ఆ రోజు హాజరై సంతకం చేశారు. నవంబర్ 26, 1949 అనే తేదీనే Preamble కూడా India ప్రజలు, Constituent Assembly ద్వారా, Constitution ను తమకు తామే adopted, enacted, ఇచ్చుకున్న రోజుగా పేర్కొంటుంది. Adopted అయిన Constitution లో Preamble, 395 Articles, 8 Schedules ఉన్నాయి — గమనించదగినది, మిగిలిన Constitution మొత్తం enact అయ్యాకే Preamble enact అయ్యింది.
Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's Role & Constitution Day

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, who was then serving as the country's Law Minister, personally piloted the Draft Constitution through the Assembly, and was well known for his logical, forceful and persuasive floor arguments. He is recognised today as the 'Father of the Constitution of India' and, as the book puts it, the "chief architect of the Constitution of India." Decades later, on November 19, 2015, the Government of India decided to celebrate November 26 every year as "Constitution Day" (Samvidhan Divas), specifically to promote constitutional values among citizens.

అప్పుడు దేశ Law Minister గా ఉన్న Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, Draft Constitution ను స్వయంగా Assembly ద్వారా pilot చేశారు, logical, forceful, persuasive floor వాదనలకు ప్రసిద్ధి చెందారు. ఈరోజు ఆయనను 'Father of the Constitution of India' గా, పుస్తకం చెప్పినట్టు "Constitution of India chief architect" గా గుర్తిస్తారు. దశాబ్దాల తర్వాత, నవంబర్ 19, 2015 న, Government of India ప్రతి సంవత్సరం నవంబర్ 26 ను "Constitution Day" (Samvidhan Divas) గా జరుపుకోవాలని నిర్ణయించింది, constitutional values ను పౌరుల్లో ప్రచారం చేయడానికి ప్రత్యేకంగా.

Enforcement of the Constitution
Enforcement of the Constitution

The Constitution did not come into force all at once. A specific set of provisions — those dealing with citizenship, elections, the provisional parliament, various temporary and transitional matters, and the short title (Articles 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 60, 324, 366, 367, 379, 380, 388, 391, 392 and 393) — came into force immediately, on November 26, 1949 itself, the very day the Constitution was adopted. The remaining, and much larger, part of the Constitution came into force only on January 26, 1950 — the day the Constitution itself names as its 'date of commencement', and which is celebrated every year as Republic Day. January 26 was not an arbitrary or merely convenient date — it was specifically chosen for its historical resonance: it was on this same day in 1930 that Purna Swaraj Day (complete independence day) had been celebrated, following the resolution passed at the Lahore Session of the INC in December 1929. With the Constitution's commencement, both the Indian Independence Act, 1947 and the Government of India Act, 1935 (along with every enactment that had amended or supplemented the 1935 Act) were repealed. One specific piece of legislation was, however, deliberately kept alive — the Abolition of Privy Council Jurisdiction Act, 1949 continued in force.

Constitution అంతా ఒకేసారి అమల్లోకి రాలేదు. citizenship, elections, provisional parliament, వివిధ temporary/transitional విషయాలు, short title (Articles 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 60, 324, 366, 367, 379, 380, 388, 391, 392, 393) కు సంబంధించిన నిర్దిష్ట నిబంధనలు వెంటనే, నవంబర్ 26, 1949 నే — Constitution adopted అయిన అదే రోజు — అమల్లోకి వచ్చాయి. Constitution లోని మిగిలిన, చాలా పెద్ద భాగం జనవరి 26, 1950 నే అమల్లోకి వచ్చింది — Constitution స్వయంగా దీన్ని తన 'commencement తేదీ' అని పేర్కొంటుంది, దీన్నే ప్రతి సంవత్సరం Republic Day గా జరుపుకుంటారు. జనవరి 26 అనేది ఏదో ఏకపక్ష లేదా కేవలం అనుకూలమైన తేదీ కాదు — దీన్ని దాని చారిత్రక ప్రాముఖ్యత కోసం ప్రత్యేకంగా ఎంచుకున్నారు — 1930లో ఇదే రోజున Purna Swaraj Day (సంపూర్ణ స్వాతంత్ర్య దినం) జరుపుకున్నారు, INC Lahore Session (డిసెంబర్ 1929) తీర్మానం ప్రకారం. Constitution commencement తో Indian Independence Act, 1947, Government of India Act, 1935 (1935 చట్టాన్ని సవరించిన/అనుబంధించిన అన్ని చట్టాలతో సహా) రద్దయ్యాయి. అయితే ఒక నిర్దిష్ట చట్టాన్ని ఉద్దేశపూర్వకంగా కొనసాగించారు — Abolition of Privy Council Jurisdiction Act, 1949 కొనసాగింది.

Congress Experts Committee & Criticism of the Constituent Assembly
Experts Committee of the Congress

While elections to the Constituent Assembly were still under way, on July 8, 1946, the Indian National Congress separately appointed an Experts Committee to prepare background material for the Assembly's use. It was chaired by Jawaharlal Nehru, and its members included M. Asaf Ali, K.M. Munshi, N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, K.T. Shah, D.R. Gadgil, Humayun Kabir and K. Santhanam; Krishna Kripalani was later co-opted as a member and convener. The committee met twice — first in New Delhi (July 20–22, 1946), then in Bombay (August 15–17, 1946) — and, beyond a number of notes prepared by individual members, it discussed the procedure the Assembly should adopt, the question of which committees should be appointed, and drafted a resolution on the Constitution's objectives that closely resembled what would become the Objectives Resolution. Granville Austin, the American constitutional expert, credited this committee with having "set India on the road to" her present Constitution.

Criticism of the Constituent Assembly

The Assembly has faced several strands of criticism over the years. First, that it was not a representative body, since its members were not directly elected by the people on universal adult franchise. Second, that it was not a sovereign body, since it was created by British Government proposals and initially held its sessions with British Government permission. Third, that it was time-consuming — critics pointed out that the framers of the American Constitution finished in just four months, and Naziruddin Ahmed, a member of the Assembly itself, coined a mocking nickname for the Drafting Committee to express his contempt: a "Drifting Committee." Fourth, that it was dominated by Congress — Granville Austin's often-quoted remark was that "the Assembly was a one-party body in an essentially one-party country." Fifth, lawyer-politician domination — critics argued other sections of society were not sufficiently represented, and blame this for the Constitution's bulk and complicated language. Sixth, that it was dominated by Hindus — Lord Viscount Simon called it "a body of Hindus," and Winston Churchill remarked that it represented "only one major community" in India.

Assembly పై సంవత్సరాలుగా అనేక విమర్శలు వచ్చాయి. మొదటిది — representative body కాదు అనేది, ఎందుకంటే సభ్యులు ప్రజల ద్వారా universal adult franchise పై నేరుగా ఎన్నికకాలేదు. రెండోది — sovereign body కాదు అనేది, ఎందుకంటే ఇది British Government ప్రతిపాదనల ద్వారానే ఏర్పడింది, మొదట్లో British Government అనుమతితోనే sessions జరిపేది. మూడోది — సమయం ఎక్కువ తీసుకుంది అనేది — విమర్శకులు American Constitution framers కేవలం 4 నెలల్లోనే పూర్తి చేశారని చెప్పారు, Assembly సభ్యుడైన Naziruddin Ahmed తన contempt చూపడానికి Drafting Committee కు వెటకారంగా "Drifting Committee" అనే పేరు పెట్టారు. నాలుగోది — Congress ఆధిపత్యం — దీన్ని Granville Austin తరచుగా ఇలా వ్యాఖ్యానిస్తారు: "Assembly ఒక ముఖ్యంగా ఒకే-పార్టీ దేశంలో ఒకే-పార్టీ body" అని. ఐదోది — lawyer-politician ఆధిపత్యం — సమాజంలోని ఇతర వర్గాలకు తగినంత ప్రాతినిధ్యం లేదని విమర్శకుల వాదన, దీనివల్లే Constitution bulky గా, భాష complicated గా ఉందని నిందిస్తారు. ఆరోది — Hindus ఆధిపత్యం — Lord Viscount Simon దీన్ని "a body of Hindus" అని పిలిచారు, Winston Churchill ఇది India లో "ఒకే ప్రధాన community" ను మాత్రమే representa చేస్తుందని అన్నారు.

Important Facts & the Hindi Text of the Constitution
Important Facts

A handful of facts about the Constitution's physical creation are frequently tested and worth remembering distinctly. The elephant was adopted as the symbol (seal) of the Constituent Assembly. Sir B.N. Rau served as the constitutional advisor (Legal Advisor) to the Assembly; H.V.R. Iyengar was its Secretary; and S.N. Mukherjee was the chief draftsman of the Constitution. Prem Behari Narain Raizada was the calligrapher of the Indian Constitution — the original document was entirely handwritten by him, in a flowing italic style. The original was then beautified and decorated by artists from Shantiniketan, including Nand Lal Bose and Beohar Rammanohar Sinha; Sinha in particular illuminated and ornamented the original Preamble that Raizada had calligraphed. A separate Hindi version was also produced: its calligraphy was done by Vasant Krishan Vaidya, and it too was elegantly decorated and illuminated by Nand Lal Bose.

Hindi Text of the Constitution

Originally, the Constitution of India made no provision at all for an authoritative text in the Hindi language. This gap was filled much later, by the 58th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1987, which inserted a new Article 394-A into the very last part of the Constitution, Part XXII. Under this Article, the President must cause to be published: (i) a Hindi translation of the Constitution — modified as necessary to match the language, style and terminology already used in the authoritative Hindi texts of Central Acts, and incorporating every amendment made before that publication; and (ii) a Hindi translation of every future amendment of the Constitution made in English. The translation, and every future amendment's translation, is to be construed as carrying the same meaning as the original English text — and if any difficulty arises in that regard, the President is empowered to have the Hindi text suitably revised. Finally, once published, the Hindi translation is deemed, for all purposes, to be the Constitution's authoritative text in Hindi.

మొదట్లో, Indian Constitution లో authoritative Hindi text కోసం ఏ నిబంధనా లేదు. ఈ లోటును చాలా తర్వాత, 58వ Constitutional Amendment Act, 1987, Constitution చివరి భాగం, Part XXII లో కొత్త Article 394-A చేర్చడం ద్వారా పూరించింది. ఈ Article ప్రకారం, President ఇవి publish చేయించాలి — (i) Constitution Hindi translation — Central Acts authoritative Hindi texts లో వాడే language, style, terminology కు అనుగుణంగా అవసరమైనంత మార్చి, ఆ publication కు ముందు జరిగిన ప్రతి amendment తో సహా; (ii) ఆంగ్లంలో చేసిన ప్రతి భవిష్యత్తు Constitution amendment కు Hindi translation. ఈ translation, భవిష్యత్తు ప్రతి amendment translation, ఆంగ్ల original తో సమాన అర్థం కలిగినదిగా భావించాలి — ఏదైనా ఇబ్బంది వస్తే, President Hindi text ను తగినట్టుగా సవరించే అధికారం కలిగి ఉంటారు. చివరగా, ఒకసారి publish అయ్యాక, Hindi translation అన్ని ప్రయోజనాల కోసం Constitution Hindi లో authoritative text గా పరిగణించబడుతుంది.

Meaning of the Constitution

The word 'constitution' traces back to the Latin verb constituere, meaning 'to establish' or 'to set up'. In its modern political sense, a constitution refers to the body of principles that lay down how a government is organised and how it functions, and that also fix the relationship between the government and the people — specifically, the people's rights and duties toward each other. Because it plays this dual role — organising the state and defining the government-citizen relationship — the Constitution is described using several overlapping labels: the 'fundamental law of the land', the 'supreme law of the state', the 'basic law of the country', the 'instrument of the government', the 'basic structure of the polity', and even the 'grundnorm' of the country (a German-origin term for the foundational norm from which every other law in the legal system draws its validity).

'constitution' పదం Latin క్రియ constituere నుండి వచ్చింది — దీని అర్థం 'to establish' లేదా 'to set up' అంటే స్థాపించడం. ఆధునిక రాజకీయ అర్థంలో, constitution అంటే ప్రభుత్వం ఎలా organised అవుతుందో, ఎలా పనిచేస్తుందో నిర్దేశించే సూత్రాల సమూహం, అలాగే ప్రభుత్వానికి ప్రజలకు మధ్య సంబంధాన్ని — వారి rights, dutiesను — నిర్ణయించేది. ఇది రెండు పాత్రలు పోషిస్తుంది కాబట్టి — state ను organise చేయడం మరియు govt-citizen సంబంధాన్ని నిర్వచించడం — దీన్ని అనేక అతివ్యాప్తి పదాలతో వర్ణిస్తారు — 'fundamental law of the land', 'supreme law of the state', 'basic law of the country', 'instrument of the government', 'basic structure of the polity', చివరికి దేశపు 'grundnorm' (జర్మన్ మూలం గల పదం — legal system లోని ప్రతి ఇతర చట్టం తన చెల్లుబాటును దీని నుండే పొందుతుంది).

Functions of a Constitution

Constitutional scholar Elliot Bulmer lists a fairly comprehensive set of purposes (or functions) a constitution can serve. Not every constitution performs every one of these — the word "can" in each point matters — but together they show that a constitution's job is much wider than simply organising a government machine. A constitution can: (1) declare and define the boundaries of the political community; (2) declare and define the nature and authority of the political community; (3) express the identity and values of a national community; (4) declare and define the rights and duties of citizens; (5) establish and regulate the political institutions of the community; (6) divide or share power between different layers of government, or between the centre and sub-state communities; (7) declare an official religious identity of the state and mark out the relationship between religious and secular authority; and (8) commit the state to particular social, economic or developmental goals.

Elliot Bulmer constitution చేయగల purposes (functions) ను ఒక సమగ్రమైన జాబితాగా ఇస్తారు. ప్రతి constitution ఈ 8 functions అన్నీ చేయదు — ప్రతి పాయింట్లోని "can" అనే పదం ముఖ్యం — కానీ కలిపి చూస్తే, constitution పని కేవలం government machine ను organise చేయడం కంటే చాలా విస్తృతమని ఇవి చూపిస్తాయి. Constitution ఇలా చేయగలదు: (1) political community boundaries ను నిర్వచించడం; (2) దాని nature, authority ను నిర్వచించడం; (3) national community identity, values ను వ్యక్తపరచడం; (4) citizens rights, duties ను నిర్వచించడం; (5) political institutions ను స్థాపించి regulate చేయడం; (6) government layers మధ్య, లేదా centre-substate communities మధ్య power ను పంచడం/విభజించడం; (7) రాజ్యానికి official religious identity ప్రకటించడం, religious-secular authority మధ్య సంబంధాన్ని గుర్తించడం; (8) రాజ్యాన్ని నిర్దిష్ట social, economic, developmental లక్ష్యాలకు కట్టుబడేలా చేయడం.

Classification of Constitutions — Overview

Constitutions across the world can be classified in several different ways, depending on which criterion is used — how the constitution came about, how its provisions are recorded, how easily it can be amended, how power is shared between national and regional governments, and what the document itself is fundamentally meant to achieve. The book covers five such classifications.

ప్రపంచవ్యాప్తంగా constitutions ను అనేక విధాలుగా వర్గీకరించవచ్చు, ఏ criterion వాడతారో దాన్ని బట్టి — constitution ఎలా ఉద్భవించింది, దాని provisions ఎలా రికార్డ్ అవుతాయి, ఎంత సులభంగా amend చేయవచ్చు, national-regional governments మధ్య power ఎలా పంచబడుతుంది, document తానే ప్రాథమికంగా దేన్ని సాధించాలని అనుకుంటుంది. పుస్తకం ఐదు రకాల వర్గీకరణలను చర్చిస్తుంది.
1. Evolved and Enacted

Based on how a constitution came about, constitutions are classified as evolved or enacted. An evolved constitution is the outcome of a slow, gradual evolutionary process — its provisions live in the form of conventions, practices, principles and judicial decisions rather than a single drafted text. It is also called a cumulative constitution, and the British Constitution is the standard example. An enacted constitution, by contrast, is deliberately drafted by a constituent assembly or constitutional council, or promulgated by a king or parliament, with its provisions incorporated into a book, document, or series of documents. It is also called a conventional constitution, and the American and Indian constitutions are the standard examples.

Constitution ఎలా ఉద్భవించిందో దాన్ని బట్టి, evolved లేదా enacted గా వర్గీకరించబడతాయి. Evolved constitution నెమ్మదిగా, క్రమంగా జరిగిన evolutionary process ఫలితం — దీని provisions ఒకే draft చేసిన టెక్స్ట్ లో కాకుండా, conventions, practices, principles, judicial decisions రూపంలో ఉంటాయి. దీన్ని cumulative constitution అని కూడా అంటారు, British Constitution దీనికి ప్రామాణిక ఉదాహరణ. Enacted constitution దీనికి భిన్నంగా — constituent assembly లేదా constitutional council ఉద్దేశపూర్వకంగా draft చేస్తుంది, లేదా రాజు/parliament ప్రకటిస్తుంది, దీని provisions ఒక పుస్తకం, document, లేదా series of documents లో పొందుపరచబడతాయి. దీన్ని conventional constitution అని కూడా అంటారు, American, Indian constitutions దీనికి ప్రామాణిక ఉదాహరణలు.
2. Written and Unwritten

Based on how provisions are incorporated, constitutions are classified as written or unwritten. A written constitution has its provisions incorporated into a book, document or series of documents — consciously formulated and adopted by a body known as a constituent assembly or constitutional convention (or any other name). It is also called a documentary or codified constitution, and the constitutions of the USA, Canada, Japan, France and India are good examples. An unwritten constitution, on the other hand, has most of its provisions not incorporated into a single document — it is found instead in conventions, practices, principles, charters, statutes and judicial decisions, and is a product of historical evolution rather than deliberate formulation. It is also called a non-documentary or uncodified constitution, and the constitutions of the UK, New Zealand and Israel are good examples. The book itself notes that this written/unwritten distinction is more or less the same as the evolved/enacted distinction above.

Provisions ఎలా పొందుపరచబడతాయో దాన్ని బట్టి, written లేదా unwritten గా వర్గీకరించబడతాయి. Written constitution provisions ఒక పుస్తకం, document, లేదా series of documents లో పొందుపరచబడతాయి — constituent assembly లేదా constitutional convention (లేదా వేరే పేరు గల body) ఉద్దేశపూర్వకంగా రూపొందించి ఆమోదిస్తుంది. దీన్ని documentary లేదా codified constitution అని కూడా అంటారు, USA, Canada, Japan, France, India constitutions దీనికి మంచి ఉదాహరణలు. Unwritten constitution దీనికి భిన్నంగా — దీని చాలా provisions ఒకే document లో పొందుపరచబడవు — బదులుగా conventions, practices, principles, charters, statutes, judicial decisions రూపంలో కనిపిస్తాయి, ఇది ఉద్దేశపూర్వక formulation కంటే historical evolution ఫలితం. దీన్ని non-documentary లేదా uncodified constitution అని కూడా అంటారు, UK, New Zealand, Israel constitutions దీనికి మంచి ఉదాహరణలు. Written/unwritten వ్యత్యాసం పైన చెప్పిన evolved/enacted వ్యత్యాసంతో దాదాపు ఒకటే అని పుస్తకమే గమనిస్తుంది.

3. Rigid and Flexible

Based on the method of amendment, constitutions are classified as rigid or flexible — a classification given by Lord James Bryce. A rigid constitution cannot be amended in the same manner as ordinary laws; it requires a special procedure for amendment, which means it draws a distinction between constitutional law and ordinary law. A flexible constitution, on the other hand, can be amended in the same manner as ordinary laws, requiring no special procedure — so it makes no such distinction. A flexible constitution is also called an elastic constitution (examples: UK, New Zealand), while a rigid constitution is also called an inelastic constitution (examples: USA, Australia, Switzerland). The Constitution of India is neither purely rigid nor purely flexible, but a synthesis of both.

Amendment పద్ధతిని బట్టి, rigid లేదా flexible గా వర్గీకరించబడతాయి — ఈ classification Lord James Bryce ఇచ్చారు. Rigid constitution ఆర్డినరీ చట్టాల మాదిరిగా amend చేయబడదు; దీనికి amendment కోసం special procedure అవసరం — అంటే constitutional law, ordinary law మధ్య తేడాను ఇది గుర్తిస్తుంది. Flexible constitution దీనికి భిన్నంగా — ఆర్డినరీ చట్టాల మాదిరిగానే amend చేయవచ్చు, special procedure అవసరం లేదు — కాబట్టి ఈ తేడాను ఇది గుర్తించదు. Flexible constitution ను elastic constitution అని కూడా అంటారు (ఉదాహరణలు: UK, New Zealand), rigid constitution ను inelastic constitution అని కూడా అంటారు (ఉదాహరణలు: USA, Australia, Switzerland). India Constitution పూర్తిగా rigid కాదు, పూర్తిగా flexible కాదు — రెండింటి synthesis.

Classification — Federal/Unitary, Procedural/Prescriptive
4. Federal and Unitary

Based on the nature of the relationship between the national government and regional governments, constitutions are classified as federal or unitary. A federal constitution provides for a division of power between the national government and regional governments, enabling each to operate independently within its own jurisdiction — prevalent in the USA, Switzerland, Australia, Canada, Russia, Brazil and some other states. A unitary constitution, by contrast, provides for the concentration of power in the hands of the national government, making regional governments operate as subordinate agencies that exercise only such powers as may be delegated to them by the national government — prevalent in the UK, France, Japan, China, Italy, Norway and other states. The Constitution of India contains both federal and unitary provisions, and for this reason was described as quasi-federal by K.C. Wheare.

National government కు regional governments కు మధ్య సంబంధ స్వభావాన్ని బట్టి, federal లేదా unitary గా వర్గీకరించబడతాయి. Federal constitution national government, regional governments మధ్య power ను విభజిస్తుంది, ప్రతి ఒక్కటి తన సొంత జురిస్డిక్షన్ లో స్వతంత్రంగా పనిచేయడానికి వీలు కల్పిస్తుంది — USA, Switzerland, Australia, Canada, Russia, Brazil వంటి రాష్ట్రాల్లో ఇది ఎక్కువ. Unitary constitution దీనికి భిన్నంగా — power ను national government చేతుల్లో కేంద్రీకరిస్తుంది, regional governments national government ఇచ్చిన అధికారాలు మాత్రమే వినియోగించే subordinate agencies గా పనిచేస్తాయి — UK, France, Japan, China, Italy, Norway వంటి రాష్ట్రాల్లో ఇది ఎక్కువ. India Constitution లో federal, unitary provisions రెండూ ఉన్నాయి, అందుకే దీన్ని K.C. Wheare quasi-federal అని వర్ణించారు.

5. Procedural and Prescriptive

According to constitutional scholar Elliot Bulmer, two broad constitutional archetypes can be identified — procedural and prescriptive — and the difference between them relates to the underlying nature and purpose of the document itself. A procedural constitution defines the legal and political structures of public institutions and sets out the legal limits of government power, in order to protect democratic processes and fundamental human rights. A prescriptive constitution does this too, but goes further — it assumes (or attempts to impose) a broad consensus on common societal goals that public authorities must strive to achieve.

Elliot Bulmer ప్రకారం, రెండు విస్తృత constitutional archetypes గుర్తించవచ్చు — procedural, prescriptive — వీటి మధ్య తేడా document స్వభావం, ఉద్దేశానికి సంబంధించినది. Procedural constitution public institutions యొక్క legal, political structures ను నిర్వచించి, government power యొక్క legal పరిమితులను నిర్దేశిస్తుంది, democratic processes, fundamental human rights ను రక్షించడం కోసం. Prescriptive constitution ఇది కూడా చేస్తుంది, కానీ ఇంకా ముందుకెళ్తుంది — public authorities సాధించడానికి ప్రయత్నించాల్సిన common societal goals పై విస్తృత ఏకాభిప్రాయాన్ని ఊహిస్తుంది (లేదా విధించడానికి ప్రయత్నిస్తుంది).

Constitutionalism and Constitutional Government
Constitutionalism and Constitutional Government — Meaning

The concepts of constitution and constitutionalism are closely related, but they are not the same thing, and the book draws out this difference across four connected points. First, a country may well have a constitution but not constitutionalism — a dictatorship is the standard illustration — because unlimited power endangers people's freedom, and absolute power tends to corrupt absolutely; a constitution, therefore, should be permeated with constitutionalism, not merely exist alongside unchecked rule. Second, constitutionalism essentially means limited government, or a limitation on government — it is the direct opposite of arbitrary power. Only when a country's constitution seeks to decentralise power (rather than concentrating it at one point) and also imposes other restraints on that power, does the country have not just a constitution, but genuine constitutionalism. Third, constitutionalism describes a polity governed under a constitution that ordains essentially limited government and the rule of law, as opposed to arbitrary, despotic, authoritarian or totalitarian rule — constitutional government must necessarily be democratic government, and arbitrary power in the hands of any individual or institution, even if that power is formally conferred by a constitutional document, is itself a negation of constitutionalism. Fourth, constitutionalism is another name for the idea of "civilized," limited government — the real justification for having a constitution at all lies in achieving limited government, and in requiring those who govern to conform to laws and rules rather than to their own will.

Constitution, constitutionalism భావనలు దగ్గరి సంబంధం కలిగినవి, కానీ ఒకటి కాదు — పుస్తకం ఈ తేడాను నాలుగు అనుసంధాన పాయింట్ల ద్వారా వివరిస్తుంది. మొదటిది — ఒక దేశానికి constitution ఉండి, constitutionalism ఉండకపోవచ్చు — dictatorship దీనికి ప్రామాణిక ఉదాహరణ — ఎందుకంటే అపరిమిత power ప్రజల స్వేచ్ఛను ప్రమాదంలో పడేస్తుంది, absolute power పూర్తిగా corrupt చేస్తుంది; అందుకే constitution constitutionalism తో నిండి ఉండాలి, కేవలం అదుపులేని పరిపాలనతో పాటు ఉండడం సరిపోదు. రెండోది — constitutionalism అంటే ప్రాథమికంగా limited government, government పై పరిమితి — ఇది arbitrary power కు నేరుగా వ్యతిరేకం. ఒక దేశ constitution power ను (ఒకచోట కేంద్రీకరించకుండా) decentralise చేయాలని ప్రయత్నించి, ఆ power పై ఇతర restraints కూడా విధించినప్పుడే, ఆ దేశానికి కేవలం constitution మాత్రమే కాదు, నిజమైన constitutionalism ఉంటుంది. మూడోది — constitutionalism అనేది essentially limited government, rule of law ను ఆదేశించే constitution కింద పాలించబడే polity ను వర్ణిస్తుంది, arbitrary, despotic, authoritarian, లేదా totalitarian పాలనకు వ్యతిరేకంగా — constitutional government తప్పనిసరిగా democratic government అయి ఉండాలి, ఏ వ్యక్తి లేదా institution చేతిలోనైనా arbitrary power — అది constitutional document ద్వారా అధికారికంగా ఇవ్వబడినా సరే — constitutionalism ను negate చేస్తుంది. నాలుగోది — constitutionalism "civilized," limited government భావనకు మరో పేరు — constitution ఉండటానికి నిజమైన సమర్థన limited government సాధించడంలో, పాలించేవారు తమ ఇష్టం కంటే చట్టాలు, నియమాలకు కట్టుబడాలని కోరడంలో ఉంది.
