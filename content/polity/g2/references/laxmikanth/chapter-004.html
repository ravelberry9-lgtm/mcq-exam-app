Chapter 4 — Salient Features of the Constitution

M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity, 8th Edition — Full-Depth Bilingual Study Notes
Introduction

The Indian Constitution is unique in both contents and spirit. Though it borrows freely from almost every Constitution in the world, it has several salient features that distinguish it from the Constitutions of other countries. It is worth noting at the outset that many of the original (1949) features have undergone substantial change through amendments — particularly the 7th, 42nd, 44th, 73rd, 74th, 97th and 101st Amendments. The 42nd Amendment Act (1976) in particular is known as the 'Mini-Constitution' because of the sheer number and importance of the changes it made across the document. However, in the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973), the Supreme Court ruled that Parliament's constituent power under Article 368 does not extend to altering the 'basic structure' of the Constitution — a limit that has shaped every amendment since.

India రాజ్యాంగం contents, spirit రెండింటిలోనూ ప్రత్యేకమైనది. ప్రపంచంలోని దాదాపు ప్రతి రాజ్యాంగం నుండి స్వేచ్ఛగా తీసుకున్నా, దీన్ని ఇతర దేశాల Constitutions నుండి వేరు చేసే అనేక salient features ఉన్నాయి. మొదట్లోనే గమనించాల్సింది — 1949 నాటి అనేక original features amendments వల్ల గణనీయంగా మారిపోయాయి — ముఖ్యంగా 7th, 42nd, 44th, 73rd, 74th, 97th, 101st Amendments. 42nd Amendment Act (1976) ను ప్రత్యేకంగా 'Mini-Constitution' అంటారు — ఇది రాజ్యాంగంలో చేసిన మార్పుల సంఖ్య, ప్రాముఖ్యత చాలా ఎక్కువ కాబట్టి. అయితే, Kesavananda Bharati కేసు (1973) లో, Article 368 కింద Parliament constituent power రాజ్యాంగం 'basic structure' ను మార్చడానికి అనుమతించదని Supreme Court తీర్పు ఇచ్చింది — అప్పటి నుండి ప్రతి amendment ఈ పరిమితికి లోబడే జరుగుతుంది.
1. Lengthiest Written Constitution

Constitutions are classified as written (like the American Constitution) or unwritten (like the British Constitution). India's Constitution is the lengthiest of all written Constitutions in the world — a very comprehensive, elaborate and detailed document. Originally (1949), it contained a Preamble, 395 Articles (in 22 Parts) and 8 Schedules. Presently, it consists of a Preamble, about 470 Articles (in 25 Parts) and 12 Schedules — amendments since 1951 have deleted about 20 Articles and one Part (VII), while adding about 95 Articles, four Parts (IVA, IXA, IXB, XIVA) and four Schedules (9, 10, 11, 12). No other Constitution in the world has this many Articles and Schedules.

Constitutions written (American Constitution వంటివి) లేదా unwritten (British Constitution వంటివి) గా వర్గీకరించబడతాయి. India రాజ్యాంగం world లో అన్ని written Constitutions లో అత్యంత lengthy — చాలా comprehensive, elaborate, detailed document. మొదట్లో (1949) — Preamble, 395 Articles (22 Parts లో), 8 Schedules ఉండేవి. ప్రస్తుతం — Preamble, సుమారు 470 Articles (25 Parts లో), 12 Schedules ఉన్నాయి — 1951 నుండి amendments సుమారు 20 Articles, ఒక Part (VII) delete చేశాయి, సుమారు 95 Articles, నాలుగు Parts (IVA, IXA, IXB, XIVA), నాలుగు Schedules (9,10,11,12) add చేశాయి. ప్రపంచంలో మరే రాజ్యాంగానికి ఇన్ని Articles, Schedules లేవు.

Four factors have contributed to this "elephantine size": (1) Geographical factors — the vastness of the country and its diversity; (2) Historical factors — the influence of the bulky Government of India Act, 1935; (3) a single Constitution for both the Centre and the states (unlike the USA, where each state has its own constitution besides the federal one); (4) dominance of legal luminaries in the Constituent Assembly, who tended to provide for every possible contingency and used precise legal language. The Constitution contains not just fundamental governance principles but also detailed administrative provisions — matters left to ordinary legislation or political convention in other democracies have been written directly into India's constitutional text.

ఈ "elephantine size" కు 4 factors దోహదం చేశాయి — (1) Geographical factors — దేశం విస్తీర్ణం, వైవిధ్యం; (2) Historical factors — bulky Government of India Act, 1935 ప్రభావం; (3) Centre, states రెండింటికీ single Constitution (USA లో లాగా ప్రతి state కు సొంత constitution కాదు); (4) Constituent Assembly లో legal luminaries ఆధిపత్యం — వారు ప్రతి సాధ్యమైన పరిస్థితినీ ముందుగానే ఊహించి, ఖచ్చితమైన legal భాష వాడారు. రాజ్యాంగంలో fundamental governance principles మాత్రమే కాక detailed administrative provisions కూడా ఉన్నాయి — ఇతర democracies లో ordinary legislation/political convention కు వదిలేసిన విషయాలు India constitutional text లోనే రాశారు.

Till 2019, the erstwhile State of Jammu and Kashmir had its own constitution and enjoyed a special status under Article 370. In 2019, this special status was abolished by a presidential order, "The Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 2019", superseding the earlier 1954 order — the 2019 order extended all provisions of India's Constitution to J&K. Further, the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 bifurcated the erstwhile state into two Union territories — J&K and Ladakh.

2019 వరకూ, పూర్వపు Jammu and Kashmir రాష్ట్రానికి సొంత constitution ఉండేది, Article 370 కింద ప్రత్యేక హోదా అనుభవించేది. 2019లో, ఈ ప్రత్యేక హోదాను ఒక presidential order — "The Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 2019" — రద్దు చేసింది, ఇది 1954 నాటి పాత order స్థానంలో వచ్చింది — 2019 order India Constitution నిబంధనలన్నింటినీ J&K కు వర్తింపజేసింది. అలాగే, Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 పూర్వపు రాష్ట్రాన్ని రెండు Union Territories గా విభజించింది — J&K, Ladakh (దీని రాజ్యాంగబద్ధతపై SC తీర్పు కోసం చివరి Current Affairs page చూడండి).

2. Drawn from Various Sources

The Constitution of India has borrowed most of its provisions from the Constitutions of various other countries, as well as from the Government of India Act, 1935. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar proudly acclaimed that it was framed after "ransacking all the known Constitutions of the World." The structural part of the Constitution is, to a large extent, derived from the GoI Act, 1935. The philosophical part — Fundamental Rights and the Directive Principles of State Policy — draws inspiration from the American and Irish Constitutions respectively. The political part — Cabinet Government and Executive-Legislature relations — is largely drawn from the British Constitution.

India రాజ్యాంగం చాలా నిబంధనలను వివిధ దేశాల Constitutions నుండి, అలాగే Government of India Act, 1935 నుండి తీసుకుంది. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar ఇది world లోని తెలిసిన అన్ని Constitutions ను "ransack" చేసి రూపొందించారని గర్వంగా చెప్పారు. రాజ్యాంగంలోని structural part ఎక్కువగా GoI Act, 1935 నుండి తీసుకున్నారు. Philosophical part — Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles of State Policy — American, Irish Constitutions నుండి inspiration తీసుకుంది. Political part — Cabinet Government, Executive-Legislature సంబంధాలు — ఎక్కువగా British Constitution నుండి తీసుకున్నారు.

Other provisions have been drawn from the Constitutions of Canada, Australia, Germany, USSR (now Russia), France, South Africa, Japan, and others (see Table 4.4). The most profound influence and material source, however, is the Government of India Act, 1935 — the Federal Scheme, Judiciary, Governors, Emergency Powers, the Public Service Commissions and most administrative details are drawn from this Act. More than half of the Constitution's provisions are identical to, or closely resemble, the 1935 Act.

ఇతర నిబంధనలు Canada, Australia, Germany, USSR (ఇప్పుడు Russia), France, South Africa, Japan Constitutions నుండి తీసుకున్నారు (Table 4.4 చూడండి). అయితే, అత్యంత ప్రభావం, material source Government of India Act, 1935 దే — Federal Scheme, Judiciary, Governors, Emergency Powers, Public Service Commissions, చాలా administrative details ఈ Act నుండి తీసుకున్నారు. రాజ్యాంగంలో సగానికి పైగా నిబంధనలు 1935 Act ను పోలి ఉంటాయి లేదా identical గా ఉంటాయి.

3. Blend of Rigidity and Flexibility
3. Blend of Rigidity and Flexibility

Constitutions are also classified as rigid — requiring a special procedure for amendment, as in the American Constitution — or flexible — amendable in the same manner as ordinary laws, as in the British Constitution. The Constitution of India is neither rigid nor flexible, but a synthesis of both. Article 368 provides for two types of amendment: (1) some provisions can be amended by a special majority of Parliament — a two-thirds majority of members of each House present and voting, and a majority of the total membership of each House; (2) some other provisions require this same special majority plus ratification by half of the total states. At the same time, some provisions of the Constitution can be amended by a simple majority of Parliament, in the manner of ordinary legislation — notably, these amendments do not come under Article 368 at all.

Constitutions rigid (special amendment procedure అవసరం, American Constitution వంటివి) లేదా flexible (ordinary laws లాగే amend చేయవచ్చు, British Constitution వంటివి) గా కూడా వర్గీకరించబడతాయి. India రాజ్యాంగం rigid కాదు, flexible కాదు — రెండింటి synthesis. Article 368 రెండు రకాల amendment ఇస్తుంది — (1) కొన్ని నిబంధనలు special majority తో — ప్రతి House లో present&voting సభ్యుల్లో 2/3 మెజారిటీ + మొత్తం సభ్యత్వంలో మెజారిటీ; (2) కొన్ని ఇతర నిబంధనలు ఇదే special majority + సగం రాష్ట్రాల ratification. అదే సమయంలో, కొన్ని నిబంధనలను Parliament simple majority తో, ordinary legislation పద్ధతిలోనే amend చేయవచ్చు — ఇవి Article 368 పరిధిలోకి రావు.

4. Federal System with Unitary Bias
4. Federal System with Unitary Bias

The Constitution of India establishes a federal system of government, containing all the usual features of a federation — two governments, division of powers, a written Constitution, supremacy of the Constitution, rigidity of the Constitution, an independent judiciary, and bicameralism. However, it also contains many unitary or non-federal features — a strong Centre, single Constitution, single citizenship, flexibility of the Constitution, integrated judiciary, appointment of state governors by the Centre, all-India services, emergency provisions, and so on.

India రాజ్యాంగం federal system ఏర్పాటు చేస్తుంది, federation యొక్క usual features అన్నీ కలిగి ఉంటుంది — రెండు governments, అధికార విభజన, written Constitution, Constitution supremacy, Constitution rigidity, independent judiciary, bicameralism. అయితే, చాలా unitary లేదా non-federal features కూడా ఉన్నాయి — బలమైన Centre, single Constitution, single citizenship, Constitution flexibility, integrated judiciary, Centre నియమించే state governors, all-India services, emergency provisions వంటివి.

Moreover, the term 'Federation' has nowhere been used in the Constitution. Article 1, on the other hand, describes India as a 'Union of States' — implying two things: one, the Indian Federation is not the result of an agreement by the states; and two, no state has the right to secede from the federation. Hence, the Indian Constitution has been variously described as 'federal in form but unitary in spirit'; 'quasi-federal' by K.C. Wheare; 'bargaining federalism' by Morris Jones; 'co-operative federalism' by Granville Austin; 'federation with a centralising tendency' by Ivor Jennings, and so on.

అంతేకాక, 'Federation' అనే పదం రాజ్యాంగంలో ఎక్కడా వాడలేదు. దీనికి బదులు, Article 1 India ను 'Union of States' అని వర్ణిస్తుంది — దీని అర్థం రెండు విషయాలు — ఒకటి, Indian Federation రాష్ట్రాల మధ్య ఒప్పందం ఫలితం కాదు; రెండు, ఏ రాష్ట్రానికీ federation నుండి విడిపోయే హక్కు లేదు. కాబట్టి, India రాజ్యాంగాన్ని వివిధ రకాలుగా వర్ణించారు — 'federal in form but unitary in spirit'; K.C. Wheare 'quasi-federal'; Morris Jones 'bargaining federalism'; Granville Austin 'co-operative federalism'; Ivor Jennings 'federation with a centralising tendency' అని పిలిచారు.

5. Parliamentary Form of Government
5. Parliamentary Form of Government

The Constitution of India has opted for the British Parliamentary System of government rather than the American Presidential System. The parliamentary system is based on the principle of co-operation and co-ordination between the legislative and executive organs, while the presidential system is based on the doctrine of separation of powers between the two organs. Also known as the 'Westminster' Model of government, responsible government, or Cabinet government, this system is established by the Constitution not only at the Centre, but also in every state.

India రాజ్యాంగం American Presidential System కంటే British Parliamentary System ను ఎంచుకుంది. Parliamentary system legislative, executive organs మధ్య co-operation, co-ordination సూత్రం పైన ఆధారపడుతుంది, presidential system రెండు organs మధ్య separation of powers doctrine పైన ఆధారపడుతుంది. దీన్నే 'Westminster' Model of government, responsible government, Cabinet government అని కూడా అంటారు — ఇది Centre లోనే కాక ప్రతి state లో కూడా ఏర్పాటు చేయబడింది.

India has 6 features of parliamentary government: (1) presence of nominal and real executives; (2) majority party rule; (3) collective responsibility of the executive to the legislature; (4) membership of ministers in the legislature; (5) leadership of the Prime Minister or Chief Minister; (6) dissolution of the lower House (Lok Sabha or Assembly). Even though largely based on the British pattern, there are fundamental differences — the Indian Parliament is not a sovereign body like the British Parliament; further, India has an elected head (republic) while Britain has a hereditary head (monarchy). In both countries, the role of the Prime Minister has become so significant that political scientists call it a 'Prime Ministerial Government'.

India లో 6 parliamentary government features ఉన్నాయి — (1) nominal, real executives ఉండటం; (2) మెజారిటీ పార్టీ పాలన; (3) executive legislature కు collective responsibility; (4) ministers legislature లో సభ్యులుగా ఉండటం; (5) PM/CM నాయకత్వం; (6) lower House (LS/Assembly) dissolution. ఎక్కువగా British పద్ధతి ఆధారంగా ఉన్నా, ప్రాథమిక తేడాలు ఉన్నాయి — Indian Parliament British Parliament లాగా sovereign body కాదు; India కు elected head (republic), Britain కు hereditary head (monarchy). రెండు దేశాల్లోనూ, PM పాత్ర ఎంత ముఖ్యమైందంటే political scientists దీన్ని 'Prime Ministerial Government' అని పిలుస్తారు.

6. Synthesis of Parliamentary Sovereignty and Judicial Supremacy
6. Synthesis of Parliamentary Sovereignty and Judicial Supremacy

The doctrine of sovereignty of Parliament is associated with the British Parliament, while the principle of judicial supremacy is associated with the American Supreme Court. Just as India's parliamentary system differs from Britain's, the scope of the Indian Supreme Court's judicial review power is narrower than what exists in the US — this is because the American Constitution provides for 'due process of law', against the 'procedure established by law' contained in the Indian Constitution (Article 21).

Sovereignty of Parliament doctrine British Parliament తో ముడిపడి ఉంటుంది, judicial supremacy principle American Supreme Court తో ముడిపడి ఉంటుంది. India parliamentary system Britain దానికంటే భిన్నంగా ఉన్నట్టే, India Supreme Court judicial review శక్తి పరిధి US లో ఉన్నదానికంటే ఇరుకైనది — ఎందుకంటే American Constitution 'due process of law' ఇస్తుంది, India Constitution (Article 21) 'procedure established by law' ఇస్తుంది.

Therefore, the framers of the Indian Constitution have preferred a proper synthesis between the British principle of parliamentary sovereignty and the American principle of judicial supremacy. The Supreme Court, on the one hand, can declare parliamentary laws as unconstitutional through its power of judicial review. Parliament, on the other hand, can amend the major portion of the Constitution through its constituent power.

కాబట్టి, India రాజ్యాంగం framers British parliamentary sovereignty principle, American judicial supremacy principle మధ్య సరైన synthesis ను ఎంచుకున్నారు. Supreme Court, ఒక వైపు, judicial review అధికారం ద్వారా Parliament చట్టాలను unconstitutional అని ప్రకటించగలదు. Parliament, మరో వైపు, తన constituent power ద్వారా రాజ్యాంగంలో ఎక్కువ భాగాన్ని amend చేయగలదు.

7. Integrated and Independent Judiciary
7. Integrated and Independent Judiciary

The Indian Constitution establishes a judicial system that is both integrated and independent. The Supreme Court stands at the top of this single, integrated judicial system; below it are high courts at the state level; and under a high court is a hierarchy of subordinate courts — district courts and other lower courts. This single system of courts enforces both Central laws and state laws, unlike the USA, where federal laws are enforced by the federal judiciary and state laws by the state judiciary.

India రాజ్యాంగం integrated, independent రెండూ అయిన judicial system ను ఏర్పాటు చేస్తుంది. ఈ ఒకే, integrated judicial system పైన Supreme Court ఉంటుంది; దాని కింద state స్థాయిలో high courts; ఒక high court కింద district courts, ఇతర lower courts వంటి subordinate courts hierarchy ఉంటుంది. ఈ ఒకే court వ్యవస్థ Central, state చట్టాలు రెండూ అమలు చేస్తుంది — USA కు భిన్నంగా, అక్కడ federal చట్టాలను federal judiciary, state చట్టాలను state judiciary వేర్వేరుగా అమలు చేస్తాయి.

The Supreme Court is a federal court, the highest court of appeal, the guarantor of the fundamental rights of citizens, and the guardian of the Constitution. Hence, the Constitution provides various safeguards for its independence — security of tenure of judges, fixed service conditions, all expenses of the SC charged on the Consolidated Fund of India, prohibition on discussing judges' conduct in the legislatures, a ban on practice after retirement, the power to punish for its own contempt vested in the SC itself, and separation of the judiciary from the executive.

8. Fundamental Rights

Part III of the Constitution guarantees 6 Fundamental Rights to all citizens: (1) Right to Equality (Articles 14-18); (2) Right to Freedom (Articles 19-22); (3) Right against Exploitation (Articles 23-24); (4) Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25-28); (5) Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29-30); and (6) Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32). Originally, the Constitution provided for 7 Fundamental Rights — however, the Right to Property (Article 31) was deleted from this list by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978, and made a legal right instead, under Article 300-A in Part XII.

రాజ్యాంగం Part III పౌరులందరికీ 6 Fundamental Rights guarantee చేస్తుంది — (1) Right to Equality (Articles 14-18); (2) Right to Freedom (Articles 19-22); (3) Right against Exploitation (Articles 23-24); (4) Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25-28); (5) Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29-30); (6) Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32). మొదట్లో రాజ్యాంగం 7 Fundamental Rights ఇచ్చింది — అయితే, Right to Property (Article 31) ను 44th Amendment Act, 1978 ఈ జాబితా నుండి delete చేసింది, బదులుగా Part XII లోని Article 300-A కింద legal right గా మార్చింది.

Fundamental Rights are meant for promoting political democracy — they operate as limitations on the tyranny of the executive and arbitrary laws of the legislature. They are justiciable in nature — enforceable by courts for their violation — and an aggrieved person can go directly to the Supreme Court, which can issue writs of habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo warranto for restoration of rights. However, FRs are not absolute and are subject to reasonable restrictions; they can be curtailed or repealed by Parliament through a Constitutional Amendment; and they can be suspended during a National Emergency — except the rights guaranteed by Articles 20 and 21.

Fundamental Rights political democracy ను ప్రోత్సహించడానికి ఉద్దేశించినవి — executive tyranny, legislature arbitrary చట్టాలపై పరిమితులుగా పనిచేస్తాయి. ఇవి justiciable స్వభావం కలిగినవి — ఉల్లంఘనకు courts ద్వారా అమలు చేయగలరు — బాధిత వ్యక్తి నేరుగా Supreme Court కు వెళ్లవచ్చు, హక్కుల పునరుద్ధరణ కోసం habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto వంటి writs జారీ చేయగలదు. అయితే, FRs absolute కావు, reasonable restrictions కు లోబడి ఉంటాయి; Parliament Constitutional Amendment ద్వారా curtail/repeal చేయవచ్చు; National Emergency సమయంలో suspend చేయవచ్చు — Articles 20, 21 హక్కులు తప్ప.
9. Directive Principles of State Policy

According to Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, the Directive Principles of State Policy is a 'novel feature' of the Indian Constitution. They are enumerated in Part IV, and can be classified into three broad categories — socialistic, Gandhian and liberal-intellectual. The Directive Principles are meant for promoting the ideal of social and economic democracy, seeking to establish a 'welfare state' in India. However, unlike Fundamental Rights, the directives are non-justiciable in nature — they are not enforceable by courts for their violation. Yet the Constitution itself declares that "these principles are fundamental in the governance of the country, and it shall be the duty of the state to apply these principles in making laws" — imposing a moral obligation on state authorities. The real force (sanction) behind them is political — public opinion. In the Minerva Mills case (1980), the Supreme Court held that "the Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of the balance between the Fundamental Rights and the Directive Principles."

Ambedkar ప్రకారం, Directive Principles of State Policy India రాజ్యాంగం యొక్క 'novel feature'. ఇవి Part IV లో ఇవ్వబడ్డాయి, మూడు broad categories గా వర్గీకరించవచ్చు — socialistic, Gandhian, liberal-intellectual. 10. Fundamental Duties
10. Fundamental Duties

The original Constitution did not provide for Fundamental Duties of citizens. These were added during the operation of the internal Emergency (1975-77) by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976, on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee. The 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002 added one more Fundamental Duty. Part IV-A of the Constitution — consisting of only one Article, 51-A — specifies 11 Fundamental Duties, such as: to respect the Constitution, national flag and national anthem; to protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of the country; to promote the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all people; to preserve the rich heritage of the composite culture, and so on.

11. A Secular State
11. A Secular State

The Constitution of India stands for a Secular State — it does not uphold any particular religion as the official religion of the Indian State. The term 'secular' was added to the Preamble by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976, and the Preamble secures to all citizens liberty of belief, faith and worship. Several other provisions reveal the secular character of the Indian State — Article 14 (equality before law), Article 15 (no discrimination on ground of religion), Article 16 (equality of opportunity in public employment), Articles 25-28 (freedom of religion, religious denominations' rights, no compelled religious tax, no religious instruction in State-run institutions), Articles 29-30 (rights of minorities/communities to conserve culture and run institutions), and Article 44 (State to endeavour a Uniform Civil Code).

India రాజ్యాంగం Secular State కోసం నిలుస్తుంది — ఏ ఒక్క మతాన్నీ India State అధికారిక మతంగా భావించదు. 'secular' పదాన్ని 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 Preamble కు add చేసింది, Preamble పౌరులందరికీ belief, faith, worship స్వేచ్ఛను కల్పిస్తుంది. మరికొన్ని నిబంధనలు India State secular స్వభావాన్ని చూపిస్తాయి — Article 14 (చట్టం ముందు సమానత్వం), Article 15 (మతం ఆధారంగా వివక్ష లేకపోవడం), Article 16 (public employment లో సమాన అవకాశం), Articles 25-28 (మత స్వేచ్ఛ, religious denominations హక్కులు, బలవంతపు మత పన్ను లేకపోవడం, State institutions లో religious instruction లేకపోవడం), Articles 29-30 (minorities/communities culture కాపాడుకోవడం, institutions నడపడం హక్కులు), Article 44 (Uniform Civil Code కోసం State ప్రయత్నం).

The Western concept of secularism connotes a complete separation between religion (the church) and the state (the politics) — this negative concept is inapplicable in the Indian situation, where society is multi-religious. Hence, the Indian Constitution embodies the positive concept of secularism — giving equal respect to all religions, or protecting all religions equally. 12. Universal Adult Franchise

The Indian Constitution adopts universal adult franchise as the basis of elections to the Lok Sabha and the state legislative assemblies. Every citizen not less than 18 years of age has a right to vote, without any discrimination of caste, race, religion, sex, literacy or wealth. The voting age was reduced from 21 to 18 years in 1989, by the 61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988. This introduction of universal adult franchise was a bold experiment, remarkable given the vast size of the country, its huge population, high poverty, social inequality and overwhelming illiteracy at the time. It makes democracy broad-based, enhances the self-respect and prestige of common people, upholds the principle of equality, enables minorities to protect their interests, and opens new hope for weaker sections.

India రాజ్యాంగం Lok Sabha, state legislative assemblies ఎన్నికలకు universal adult franchise ను ఆధారంగా తీసుకుంటుంది. 18 సంవత్సరాలు నిండిన ప్రతి citizen — కులం, జాతి, మతం, లింగం, అక్షరాస్యత, సంపద ఆధారంగా ఎలాంటి వివక్ష లేకుండా — ఓటు వేసే హక్కు కలిగి ఉంటారు. Voting age 1989లో 21 నుండి 18 సంవత్సరాలకు 61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1988 ద్వారా తగ్గించారు. Universal adult franchise ప్రవేశపెట్టడం ఒక bold experiment — ఆ సమయంలో దేశం విస్తీర్ణం, భారీ జనాభా, అధిక పేదరికం, social అసమానత, విస్తృత నిరక్షరాస్యత దృష్ట్యా ఇది గమనార్హం. ఇది democracy ను broad-based చేస్తుంది, సామాన్య ప్రజల self-respect, prestige పెంచుతుంది, equality సూత్రాన్ని నిలబెడుతుంది, minorities తమ ప్రయోజనాలు కాపాడుకోవడానికి వీలు కల్పిస్తుంది, weaker sections కు కొత్త ఆశలు తెరుస్తుంది.
13. Single Citizenship

Though the Indian Constitution is federal and envisages a dual polity (Centre and states), it provides for only single citizenship — that is, Indian citizenship. In countries like the USA, each person is not only a US citizen but also a citizen of the particular state to which they belong — owing allegiance to both, and enjoying dual sets of rights, one conferred by the national government and another by the state government. In India, all citizens — irrespective of the state in which they are born or reside — enjoy the same political and civil rights of citizenship all over the country, with no discrimination between them.

India రాజ్యాంగం federal అయి, dual polity (Centre, states) ను ఊహించినా, కేవలం single citizenship — అంటే Indian citizenship మాత్రమే — ఇస్తుంది. USA వంటి దేశాల్లో, ప్రతి వ్యక్తి US citizen మాత్రమే కాక తాను చెందిన state citizen కూడా — రెండింటికీ allegiance, national govt, state govt ఇచ్చే రెండు రకాల rights అనుభవిస్తారు. India లో, అన్ని citizens — ఏ రాష్ట్రంలో పుట్టినా/నివసించినా — దేశమంతటా ఒకే political, civil rights అనుభవిస్తారు, వారి మధ్య వివక్ష ఉండదు.

Despite this constitutional provision for single citizenship and uniform rights for all people, India has witnessed communal riots, class conflicts, caste wars, linguistic clashes and ethnic disputes — meaning the cherished goal of the Constitution-makers to build a united and integrated Indian nation has not been fully realised.

14. Independent Bodies
14. Independent Bodies

The Indian Constitution not only provides for the legislative, executive and judicial organs of government (Central and state) but also establishes certain independent bodies, envisaged as the bulwarks of the democratic system of government in India. These are: (1) the Election Commission, to ensure free and fair elections to Parliament, the state legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President; (2) the Comptroller and Auditor-General (CAG), to audit the accounts of the Central and state governments, acting as guardian of the public purse and commenting on the legality/propriety of government expenditure; (3) the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), to conduct examinations for recruitment to All-India and higher Central services, and to advise the President on disciplinary matters; and (4) a State Public Service Commission (SPSC) in every state, to conduct examinations for recruitment to state services and advise the Governor on disciplinary matters. The Constitution ensures the independence of these bodies through various provisions — security of tenure, fixed service conditions, and expenses charged on the Consolidated Fund of India.

India రాజ్యాంగం government legislative, executive, judicial organs (Central, state) మాత్రమే కాక కొన్ని independent bodies కూడా ఏర్పాటు చేస్తుంది, వీటిని India democratic government వ్యవస్థ యొక్క bulwarks గా భావిస్తారు. ఇవి — (1) Election Commission — Parliament, state legislatures, President, Vice-President offices కు free, fair ఎన్నికలు నిర్ధారించడానికి; (2) Comptroller and Auditor-General (CAG) — Central, state governments accounts audit చేయడానికి, public purse కు guardian గా, govt expenditure legality/propriety పై వ్యాఖ్యానించడానికి; (3) Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) — All-India, higher Central services recruitment కోసం పరీక్షలు నిర్వహించడానికి, disciplinary విషయాల్లో President కు సలహా ఇవ్వడానికి; (4) ప్రతి రాష్ట్రంలో State Public Service Commission (SPSC) — state services recruitment పరీక్షలు, Governor కు disciplinary సలహా. రాజ్యాంగం వీటి independence ను — tenure security, fixed service conditions, Consolidated Fund of India ఖర్చులు వంటి నిబంధనల ద్వారా నిర్ధారిస్తుంది.

15. Emergency Provisions
15. Emergency Provisions

The Indian Constitution contains elaborate emergency provisions to enable the President to meet any extraordinary situation effectively. The rationale behind these provisions is to safeguard the sovereignty, unity, integrity and security of the country, the democratic political system, and the Constitution itself. The Constitution envisages 3 types of emergencies: (1) National Emergency, on the ground of war, external aggression or armed rebellion (Article 352); (2) State Emergency (President's Rule), on the ground of failure of constitutional machinery in a state (Article 356) or failure to comply with directions of the Centre (Article 365); and (3) Financial Emergency, on the ground of a threat to the financial stability or credit of India (Article 360).

India రాజ్యాంగంలో అసాధారణ పరిస్థితులను President సమర్థవంతంగా ఎదుర్కోవడానికి elaborate emergency provisions ఉన్నాయి. వీటి వెనుక rationale — దేశ sovereignty, unity, integrity, security, democratic political system, రాజ్యాంగం స్వయంగా రక్షించడం. రాజ్యాంగం 3 రకాల emergencies ను ఊహిస్తుంది — (1) National Emergency — war, external aggression, లేదా armed rebellion కారణంగా (Article 352); (2) State Emergency (President's Rule) — రాష్ట్రంలో constitutional machinery failure (Article 356) లేదా Centre ఆదేశాలు పాటించకపోవడం (Article 365) కారణంగా; (3) Financial Emergency — India financial stability/credit కు ముప్పు కారణంగా (Article 360).

During an emergency, the Central Government becomes all-powerful and the states go into total control of the Centre — it converts the federal structure into a unitary one, without a formal amendment of the Constitution. This kind of transformation of the political system, from federal (during normal times) to unitary (during emergency), is a unique feature of the Indian Constitution.

Emergency సమయంలో Central Government అన్ని అధికారాలూ కలిగి ఉంటుంది, states పూర్తిగా Centre నియంత్రణలోకి వస్తాయి — ఇది federal structure ను ఎలాంటి formal amendment లేకుండానే unitary గా మారుస్తుంది. Political system యొక్క ఈ మార్పు — normal సమయంలో federal, emergency సమయంలో unitary — India రాజ్యాంగం unique feature.

16. Three-tier Government

Originally, the Indian Constitution, like any other federal Constitution, provided for a dual polity and contained provisions regarding the organisation and powers of only the Centre and the states. Later, the 73rd and 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts (1992) added a third tier of government — local government — which is not found in any other Constitution in the world. The 73rd Amendment gave constitutional recognition to panchayats (rural local governments), adding a new Part IX and Schedule 11, and provides for a three-tier system of panchayati raj — village, intermediate and district levels — in every state. The 74th Amendment similarly recognised municipalities (urban local governments), adding a new Part IX-A and Schedule 12, providing for three types of municipalities — nagar panchayat, municipal council, and municipal corporation.

మొదట్లో, India రాజ్యాంగం, ఇతర federal Constitutions లాగే, dual polity ఇచ్చింది, Centre, states సంస్థ, అధికారాల గురించి మాత్రమే నిబంధనలు కలిగి ఉండేది. తర్వాత, 73rd, 74th Constitutional Amendment Acts (1992) government మూడో tier — local government — add చేశాయి — ఇది ప్రపంచంలో మరే Constitution లోనూ లేదు. 73rd Amendment panchayats (rural local governments) కు constitutional గుర్తింపు ఇచ్చింది, కొత్త Part IX, Schedule 11 add చేసింది, ప్రతి రాష్ట్రంలో village, intermediate, district levels తో three-tier panchayati raj వ్యవస్థ ఇస్తుంది. 74th Amendment అలాగే municipalities (urban local governments) కు గుర్తింపు ఇచ్చింది, కొత్త Part IX-A, Schedule 12 add చేసింది, మూడు రకాల municipalities ఇస్తుంది — nagar panchayat, municipal council, municipal corporation.

17. Co-operative Societies

The 97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011 gave constitutional status and protection to co-operative societies, making 3 changes: (1) it made the right to form co-operative societies a Fundamental Right under Article 19; (2) it included a new Directive Principle on promotion of co-operative societies (Article 43-B); and (3) it added a new Part IX-B, "The Co-operative Societies" (Articles 243-ZH to 243-ZT), containing provisions to ensure co-operative societies function in a democratic, professional, autonomous and economically sound manner — empowering Parliament for multi-state co-operative societies and state legislatures for other co-operative societies.

Criticism of the Constitution
Criticism of the Constitution

The Constitution of India, as framed and adopted by the Constituent Assembly, has been criticised on several grounds. (1) A Borrowed Constitution — critics describe it as a 'bag of borrowings', a 'hotch-potch Constitution', or a 'patchwork' of world constitutions, containing nothing new or original. This criticism is generally considered unfair, since the framers modified borrowed features to suit Indian conditions while avoiding their known faults — Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, replying in the Constituent Assembly, said that given how settled constitutional fundamentals already were by the late 1940s, "all Constitutions in their main provisions must look similar", and any charge of blind copying reflects only an inadequate study of the Constitution.

India రాజ్యాంగం, Constituent Assembly రూపొందించి ఆమోదించినట్టు, అనేక కారణాలపై విమర్శించబడింది. (1) Borrowed Constitution — విమర్శకులు దీన్ని 'bag of borrowings', 'hotch-potch Constitution', లేదా world constitutions 'patchwork' అని వర్ణిస్తారు, ఇందులో కొత్తది, original ఏదీ లేదని. ఈ విమర్శ సాధారణంగా అన్యాయమైనదిగా భావిస్తారు, ఎందుకంటే framers borrowed features ను India పరిస్థితులకు తగినట్టుగా మార్చారు, వాటి తెలిసిన లోపాలు నివారించారు — Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, Constituent Assembly లో బదులిస్తూ, 1940ల చివరికి constitutional fundamentals ఎంతగా స్థిరపడ్డాయో దృష్ట్యా, "అన్ని Constitutions ప్రధాన నిబంధనల్లో ఒకేలా కనిపించాలి" అని, గుడ్డి copying ఆరోపణ కేవలం రాజ్యాంగంపై సరిపడని అధ్యయనాన్నే ప్రతిబింబిస్తుందని అన్నారు.

(2) A Carbon Copy of the 1935 Act — critics such as N. Srinivasan called it "both in language and substance a close copy of the Act of 1935"; Sir Ivor Jennings said many provisions were "copied almost textually"; and P.R. Deshmukh called it "the Government of India Act of 1935 with only adult franchise added." Ambedkar's reply was that there is "nothing to be ashamed of in borrowing... it involves no plagiarism" — no one holds patent rights over fundamental constitutional ideas; his only regret was that the borrowed 1935 Act provisions related mostly to administrative detail. (3) Un-Indian or Anti-Indian — critics like K. Hanumanthaiya felt the Constitution did not reflect India's political traditions, saying "we wanted the music of Veena or Sitar, but here we have the music of an English band"; Lokanath Misra called it "a slavish imitation of the west." (4) An Un-Gandhian Constitution — critics felt it should have been built upon village and district panchayats, reflecting Gandhian philosophy; Hanumanthaiya again: "exactly the kind of Constitution Mahatma Gandhi did not want."

Current Affairs Update
Current Affairs Update — Amendment Count, Article 370, and What Hasn't Changed (as of 16.08.2026, verified via live web search on this date — re-verify closer to your exam date, as amendment counts and pending bills change on their own schedule independent of the book's text)

Total enacted Constitutional Amendments — still 106, not "131": The book (8th edition) references amendments only up to the 101st (the GST Amendment, 2016). Since then, five more amendments have actually been enacted: the 102nd (National Commission for Backward Classes, 2018), 103rd (EWS reservation, 2019), 104th (extension of SC/ST reservation, removal of Anglo-Indian reservation, 2020), 105th (restoring states' power to identify SEBCs, 2021), and — most notably — the 106th Amendment Act, the Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam (2023), which provides for 33% reservation of seats for women in the Lok Sabha and state legislative assemblies (to take effect after the next delimitation exercise). As of this date, the 106th Amendment remains the latest one actually enacted. Students should not be misled by Constitution Amendment Bill numbers running well past 106 — Parliament introduced a 131st Amendment Bill, 2026 (relating to delimitation/J&K matters) in 2026, but it was defeated in the Lok Sabha and never became law; a separate 130th Amendment Bill, 2025 (on removal of the PM/CMs following arrest for serious offences, introduced August 2025) has also not been enacted. Bill numbers count every Bill introduced, whether or not it passes — only enacted Amendment Acts count towards the "106" figure.

మొత్తం enacted Constitutional Amendments — ఇప్పటికీ 106, "131" కాదు: పుస్తకం (8th edition) 101st Amendment (GST Amendment, 2016) వరకే ప్రస్తావిస్తుంది. అప్పటి నుండి, మరో ఐదు amendments వాస్తవంగా enact అయ్యాయి — 102nd (National Commission for Backward Classes, 2018), 103rd (EWS reservation, 2019), 104th (SC/ST reservation పొడిగింపు, Anglo-Indian reservation తొలగింపు, 2020), 105th (SEBCs గుర్తించే states అధికారం పునరుద్ధరణ, 2021), మరీ ముఖ్యంగా — 106th Amendment Act, Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam (2023), ఇది Lok Sabha, state legislative assemblies లో women కు 33% seats reservation ఇస్తుంది (తర్వాతి delimitation తర్వాత అమల్లోకి వస్తుంది). ఈ తేదీ నాటికి, 106th Amendment నే వాస్తవంగా enact అయిన తాజాది. Students 106 దాటి నడుస్తున్న Constitution Amendment Bill సంఖ్యల వల్ల confuse కాకూడదు — Parliament 2026లో 131st Amendment Bill, 2026 (delimitation/J&K విషయాలకు సంబంధించినది) ప్రవేశపెట్టింది, కానీ అది Lok Sabha లో ఓడిపోయింది, చట్టం కాలేదు; వేరే 130th Amendment Bill, 2025 (తీవ్రమైన నేరాలకు అరెస్ట్ తర్వాత PM/CMs తొలగింపుపై, August 2025లో ప్రవేశపెట్టారు) కూడా enact కాలేదు. Bill numbers ప్రవేశపెట్టిన ప్రతి Bill నూ లెక్కిస్తాయి, పాస్ అయినా కాకపోయినా — కేవలం enacted Amendment Acts మాత్రమే "106" సంఖ్యకు లెక్కిస్తాయి.

Article 370 abrogation — upheld by the Supreme Court: The book's discussion of J&K losing its special status (2019) predates a major follow-up development: on 11 December 2023, a 5-judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court, led by then-CJI D.Y. Chandrachud, unanimously upheld the constitutional validity of the 2019 abrogation of Article 370, in the case popularly known as In Re: Article 370 of the Constitution. The Court also directed that Jammu & Kashmir's statehood be restored "at the earliest" and endorsed a timeline for Assembly elections, while declining to rule on the specific validity of the J&K Reorganisation Act's downgrade to Union Territory status on the ground that statehood restoration had already been assured. This verdict is directly relevant to this chapter's "Lengthiest Written Constitution" section, since it settles — at least for now — the constitutional question left open by the book's brief mention of the 2019 order.

Article 370 రద్దు — Supreme Court సమర్థన: J&K ప్రత్యేక హోదా కోల్పోవడం (2019) గురించి పుస్తకం చర్చ ఒక ముఖ్యమైన తదుపరి పరిణామానికి ముందుది — 11 డిసెంబర్ 2023 న, అప్పటి CJI D.Y. Chandrachud నేతృత్వంలోని Supreme Court 5-judge Constitution Bench, 2019 Article 370 రద్దు constitutional validity ను ఏకగ్రీవంగా సమర్థించింది — ఈ కేసును సాధారణంగా In Re: Article 370 of the Constitution అని పిలుస్తారు. Court J&K statehood ను "వీలైనంత త్వరగా" పునరుద్ధరించాలని కూడా ఆదేశించింది, Assembly ఎన్నికలకు ఒక timeline ను సమర్థించింది, అయితే statehood పునరుద్ధరణ ఇప్పటికే హామీ ఇవ్వబడింది కాబట్టి J&K Reorganisation Act Union Territory స్థాయికి తగ్గించడం నిర్దిష్ట validity పై తీర్పు ఇవ్వకుండా ఉంది. ఈ తీర్పు ఈ అధ్యాయం "Lengthiest Written Constitution" section కు నేరుగా సంబంధించినది, ఎందుకంటే ఇది — కనీసం ప్రస్తుతానికి — పుస్తకం 2019 order సంక్షిప్త ప్రస్తావన వదిలిన constitutional ప్రశ్నను పరిష్కరిస్తుంది.
