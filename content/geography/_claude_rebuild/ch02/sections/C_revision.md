## Also fixed on the full student read (not in the review)
- Aryabhata was called the first person to say Earth rotates. Rewritten as "మన దేశంలో స్పష్టంగా చెప్పింది", because Greek thinkers had said it earlier. His boat analogy was dropped to keep one analogy per concept.

- Solstice rows had been given the Telugu names కర్కట/మకర సంక్రాంతి. These were removed, because those sankrantis are sidereal dates (mid-July, mid-January) and would mislead.
- The Equinox definition sat after the "two kinds of year" intro line; it now comes first.
- Exam line added for Precession: 26,000 vs 25,800.
- The "difference ≈ 20 min" sentence now shows its arithmetic.
- A long sentence about the Tropics and a long list of calendars were split into short sentences.
- Lint: the remaining hits are false positives. "మాత్రమే"/"ఎప్పుడూ" are flagged as absolute claims. The flagged "long sentences" are the circumference formula and an exam bullet list. The flagged Telugu terms are first-mention brackets and fact lines, as the style guide requires.

## Patterns the writer kept repeating
1. **Name without a reason or a story.** People and labels (Coriolis, Foucault, Pope, Easter, Tropics, February) were dropped in with no why. This produced most of the DANGLING and MISSING-WHY items.
2. **Undefined geography and astronomy terms.** Latitude, Longitude, Time zone, Equinox, Focus and cos were used as if already known, and Telugu brackets stood in for an explanation.
3. **The same number given two ways, or with a wrong qualifier.** 48 vs 49 min, 1,667 vs 1,670, "24 h = 360°" vs "361°", a 1,610 "for all places", a 23.5° "earlier value". The precise figure and the textbook figure were not reconciled in one place.
- Also recurring: translated sentences with colons ("కారణం:", "ముందు సెక్షన్ లో చూశాం:") that bury the subject.
