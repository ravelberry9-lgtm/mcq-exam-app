### `#pacific`

### `#floor-profile`
- Hypsographic curve = cumulative area at each height/depth; associated with Erwin Kossinna (1921). BK p126 ("కోసిన్న"); V.
- Book divisions: continental shelf, continental slope, deep-sea (abyssal) plain, oceanic trench. BK p126. NCERT adds continental rise in the margin sequence (shelf → slope → rise → abyssal plain).
- Shelf: gently sloping (~0.1°), from shore to shelf break; book "100 fathoms (180 m)"; average shelf-break depth ~130–140 m, range up to ~200 m (NOAA/Brit V); average width ~80 km (NCERT); narrow where mountains/convergent margins (e.g. Chile), wide on passive margins; widest = Siberian shelf (~1,500 km; book). ~7.6% of ocean floor (book; NCERT similar). Book share by ocean: Atlantic 13.3%, Pacific 5.7%, Indian 4.2%.
- Shelf importance: fishing grounds, oil and gas, placer minerals, river sediment. BK p126; NCERT.
- Slope: gradient 2°–5° typical (NCERT); book "2°–35°" local; depth from ~200 m to ~3,000 m (book "200–8,000 m" — V write ~3,000 m); canyons and fans; ~8.5% of floor (book).
- Abyssal plain: 3,000–6,000 m; flattest surfaces on Earth; fine sediments (clay, ooze, volcanic ash); book 82.7% (book's deep-sea share); most in Pacific? (book says Pacific most, Atlantic least — keep book with "సుమారు").
- Trench: > ~6,000 m, long, narrow, steep-sided; at subduction zones near island arcs; associated with active volcanoes and deep earthquakes; book 2.2% of floor. BK p127; NCERT.
- Pacific trenches (book list): Mariana, Tonga (Horizon Deep ~10,800 m, NOAA/Five Deeps V), Kermadec, Kuril–Kamchatka, Philippine ("Emden Deep" 10,540 m V); Atlantic: Puerto Rico, Romanche (fracture zone, equatorial), South Sandwich; Indian: Java/Sunda, Ob Trench/Diamantina V; "Mauritius Trench 4,000 m" — not a recognised trench → do not teach (retire P127-009).
- Challenger Deep in Mariana Trench, SW of Guam (book "near Mindanao, Philippines" — wrong; owned by L1, link).

### `#floor-features`
- Bank: broad shallow elevation on shelf, rich fishing (Dogger Bank, North Sea; Grand Banks, SE of Newfoundland). BK p128.
- Shoal: shallow sandbank/rise that makes water shallow; navigation hazard. BK p128.
- Submarine canyon: steep V-shaped valleys cut into shelf/slope, often off big rivers; turbidity currents. Zhemchug Canyon (Bering Sea) largest by volume; Pribilof Canyon; Hudson Canyon (off New York). BK p128; NOAA V.
- Mid-ocean ridge (link L5). MAR slow-spreading with central rift valley; East Pacific Rise fast-spreading, no deep rift. NOAA/USGS.
- Ridges per book: Wyville Thomson Ridge (Scotland–Faroe; book "British Isles–Iceland–Greenland" V), Walvis Ridge (off SW Africa; book "South America to Africa" wrong V), Rio Grande Rise (off Brazil); book Pacific "Solomon, Caroline"; Indian "Socotra, Chagos, S Madagascar" V.
- Oceanic plateau: broad elevated area of thick crust/LIP (Ontong Java, Kerguelen); book Telegraph (Atlantic), Albatross (Pacific), St Paul/Amsterdam (Indian). V.
- Seamount: underwater mountain ≥1,000 m relief not reaching the surface; mostly volcanic; most in Pacific. NOAA. BK p128.
- Guyot: flat-topped seamount; summit eroded by waves when at sea level, then subsided. Named by Harry Hess (1946) after Arnold Guyot (Swiss-American geographer). BK p128; NOAA V.
- Abyssal hills: small hills on abyssal plains, very common (most common landform on Earth by area V).
- APPSC Group-1 2022 PYQ: guyot = option 3 (flat-topped seamount showing gradual subsidence). BK p129.

### `#salinity`
- Salinity = total dissolved salts in grams per kg (1,000 g) of seawater; average 35‰ (book: "1000 g లో 35 g"). NCERT; BK p122. (OCR "85 g" is noise.)
- Modern Practical Salinity Unit (PSU, dimensionless, from conductivity) ≈ ‰ numerically. NOAA V.
- Water classes (BK p122): fresh 0–0.5 ppt; brackish 0.5–30; saline 30–50; brine > 50. Drinking water ~<1 ppt (book), irrigation ≤3 (book) — optional.
- Salt table (BK p122; Dittmar 1884): NaCl 77.8%, MgCl₂ 10.9%, MgSO₄ 4.7%, CaSO₄ 3.6%, K₂SO₄ 2.5%, CaCO₃ 0.3%, MgBr₂ 0.2%. NCERT table V.
- Ions by mass: Cl⁻ ~55.0%, Na⁺ ~30.6%, SO₄²⁻ ~7.7%, Mg²⁺ ~3.7%. V.
- Halogens in seawater: chlorine (most abundant), bromine, iodine. Book says iodine most abundant — wrong; state chlorine plainly.
- Challenger expedition (1872–76) samples; Dittmar's analysis (1884) — book "1884 expedition, 47 salts, 7 main". V.
- Forchhammer's principle / Principle of constant proportions (Marcet 1819, Forchhammer 1865, Dittmar 1884): ratios of major ions constant though total salinity varies. V.
- Instruments: salinometer (book), CTD (conductivity–temperature–depth). NOAA.
- Dead Sea ~340‰ (~34%); Great Salt Lake 50–270‰ varies by arm/year; Lake Van ~22‰ (soda lake). Book: Lake Van 330, Dead Sea 238, GSL 220 — Lake Van value wrong. APPSC Group-2 Feb 2024 PYQ key (1) Lake Van > Dead Sea > Great Salt Lake (book) → `::: exam`. V.
- Red Sea surface ~40‰ (highest among open seas); Baltic ~7–8‰ mean (book 3–15, mean 7.2); northern Caspian low (~5–14‰; book 14) due to Volga/Ural. V.

### `#salinity-distribution`
- Controls: evaporation (+), precipitation (−), river runoff (−), ice melt (−), sea-ice formation (+, brine rejection), winds (+ via evaporation; also move water), currents, mixing. NCERT; BK p123.
- Latitude: equatorial 0°–15° ~34.5–35‰ (book); subtropical 15°–40° 35–36‰ (book; max ~37 in N Atlantic subtropics); 50°–70° ~30–31‰ (book; approximate). NCERT V.
- Isohaline = line joining equal salinity. BK p123.
- Baltic low salinity: many rivers (Germany, Poland, Finland, Sweden), cold low evaporation, narrow Danish straits. BK p123; Brit.
- Equator lower than subtropics despite heat: daily convectional rain. BK p123.

### `#temperature`
- Sun is dominant heat source; book's other sources (interior heat, friction of motion, chemical, volcanic) minor. BK p124; NCERT.
- Instruments (BK p124): standard thermometer (surface), reversing thermometer (selected depth), "bathy thermometer" = bathythermograph/XBT (profile); add satellite IR. NOAA V.
- SST decreases poleward ~0.5°C per degree latitude (book, approximate; NCERT also ~0.5°F?) V.
- Mean surface temp 17.2°C; NH 19.4°C; SH 16.1°C (book p125; NCERT says NH oceans warmer because more land contact) — write as approximate historical estimates (P125-001).
- Highest SST slightly north of the equator (thermal equator) rather than at it. NCERT; BK p125.
- Seawater freezes ≈ −1.9°C (35‰). NOAA.
- Book: "Kuroshio raises temperature to 50°C" — wrong; omit, say warm currents raise SST by a few degrees.

### `#vertical-layers`
- Sunlight penetrates ~200 m (photic/euphotic zone ~200 m; book "200 m ≈ 100 fathoms" — 100 fathoms = 183 m). NOAA.
- ~90% of heat absorbed in top ~60 ft (book) — optional, approximate.
- Three-layer: surface mixed layer (~up to 200–500 m? warm, 20–25°C in tropics), thermocline (rapid fall, ~200–1,000 m), deep layer (cold ~0–4°C). NCERT V.
- Thermocline permanent in tropics, seasonal in mid-latitudes, absent in polar waters. NOAA V.
- Halocline = rapid salinity change; pycnocline = rapid density increase with depth. NOAA.
- 1 fathom = 6 ft = 1.8288 m; 100 fathoms ≈ 183 m; 2,200 fathoms ≈ 4,023 m. Std.
- Challenger table (John Murray): 100 fath 60.7°F … 2,200 fath 35.2°F (book p125) — a historical sample, not universal.
- Red Sea and Mediterranean warm (~13–21°C) at depth: shallow sills (Bab-el-Mandeb, Gibraltar) block cold deep ocean water; high evaporation makes dense warm water sink. Brit V. Book adds Sargasso — open ocean; drop or say warm upper layer is thick there (P125-009).

### `#bay-of-bengal`

- Stratification: fresh low-density layer on top; "barrier layer" one line V; keeps SST high → favours cyclone intensification (Lesson 8 later, no link).
- River plume, estuary, distributary, lagoon, mangrove definitions (bank 22 ORIG wording).
- Pulicat Lake = brackish lagoon on AP–TN border (second-largest brackish lagoon in India after Chilika). V.
- Mangroves in AP: Coringa (Godavari), Krishna estuary. V one line.
- Storm surge → link L3; compound flooding = surge + heavy rain + river discharge together.
- Sediment budget = inputs – outputs ± storage; dams trap sediment → delta shoreline erosion (Godavari/Krishna V).
- INCOIS (Hyderabad) issues ocean-state forecasts (OSF), high-wave alerts, potential fishing zone advisories; IMD issues cyclone warnings. INCOIS/IMD.
- AP coastline: 974 km (traditional, second longest after Gujarat); 2025 re-measurement by Survey of India/NHO: AP ~1,053 km, India ~11,098 km (V) → `::: changing`.

### `#waves`
- Wind wave: energy transferred from wind; water particles move in near-circular orbits, little net transport; floating object bobs. NOAA/NCERT.
- Parts: crest, trough, wavelength (crest-to-crest), wave height (trough-to-crest vertical), amplitude = H/2, period (time between successive crests at a point), frequency = 1/T, speed C = L/T. BK p130; NOAA.
- Wave size depends on wind speed, duration, fetch. NOAA.
- Swell: long-period waves that have left the generating area (book defines vaguely). NOAA.
- Shoaling: in shallow water waves slow, wavelength shortens, height grows; breaks when H/depth ≈ 0.78 (one line optional V). Breaker types: spilling, plunging, surging.
- Swash up the beach; backwash returns seaward under gravity. BK p130.
- Refraction: bending of wave crests as they enter shallow water, so crests become parallel to the coast and energy focuses on headlands. NOAA. (Book wrongly calls breaking "refraction".)
- Normal waves ~2 m high (book p130 "రెండు మీటర్లు" V).
- Deep-water relations: L ≈ 1.56 T² m, C ≈ 1.56 T m/s → T 10 s → L ≈ 156 m, C ≈ 15.6 m/s. Std. Book's table in knots/feet (15.6 / 510 ft?) — writer verify reading.
- Instruments (BK p130): Ekman current meter, Carruthers residual meter, … ; modern: ADCP, wave-rider buoy (INCOIS moored buoys) V.
- Tsunami — only one line + link `lesson-05#tsunami`.

### `#wave-types`
- Progressive waves: form moves forward; orbital motion. Standing (stationary) waves: oscillate in place, nodes and antinodes (seiche in bays/lakes). BK p131; NOAA V.
- Free wave: continues after the generating force stops (swell); forced wave: maintained by a continuing force (wind sea, tide). Book's free = "ripples from a stone", forced = "big destructive waves from wind/quake/storm" → teach standard + `::: exam`.
- Destructive (high, steep, strong backwash → erosion) vs constructive (low, strong swash → deposition). Std.

### `#tides-extra`
- Summary values all from Lesson 3 (link each).
- High-to-low ≈ 6 h 12.5 min (half of 12 h 25 min); book prints 6 h 18 min, high-to-high 12 h 26 min, two cycles 24 h 52 min. → `::: exam`: choose 12 h 25 min / 24 h 50 min; if only book values are offered, choose them.
- Equinoctial tides: near equinoxes (sun over equator) — spring tides at equinoxes are slightly larger; solstitial tides: near solstices, larger diurnal inequality. BK p131; NOAA/Brit V.
- Tidal bore: wall of water moving up a funnel-shaped estuary on a rising tide; Hooghly (Bengal), Gulf of Khambhat (Narmada/Mahi), Qiantang (China), Severn (UK). V.
- Book tide benefits (fishermen go out at low tide…, flush sediment, ports) → link `lesson-03#tides-uses`.

### `#current-causes`
- Ocean current = continuous directed movement of seawater. NCERT; BK p132.
- Book causes: wind direction (main), "centrifugal force", "gravity difference equator–pole", rainfall/precipitation, solar energy, … BK p132. Standard (NCERT): primary forces — solar heating, wind, gravity, Coriolis; secondary — density (T, S). → teach standard family; `::: exam`: "main cause of surface currents" = wind.
- Book example: 50 mph wind → 0.75 mph current (~1.5–2% rule; V or present as book's illustration).
- Coriolis: right in NH, left in SH (link L2).
- Ekman transport: net surface-layer transport ~90° right of wind in NH. NOAA.
- Thermohaline circulation (global conveyor belt): cold salty dense water sinks in North Atlantic (Greenland/Norwegian seas) and around Antarctica. NOAA.
- Drift (slow, broad), stream (fast, narrow), current (general) — historical usage (Q132-008). NCERT ("drift, current, stream").
- Warm current = warmer than surrounding water; usually flows equator → poles along east coasts (in low/mid latitudes); cold currents pole → equator along west coasts. NCERT.
- Upwelling: wind pushes surface water offshore, cold nutrient-rich deep water rises (Peru, California, Benguela, Canary, Somalia). NOAA. Downwelling: surface water converges and sinks.
- Gyres: subtropical gyres clockwise NH, anticlockwise SH; five major gyres (N/S Pacific, N/S Atlantic, Indian). NOAA.
- Western intensification: western boundary currents narrow, fast, deep, warm. NOAA.

### `#pacific-currents`
- Per section row; NOAA/Brit/NCERT ch.14 maps. Kuroshio = "black current" (Japanese kuro = black; dark water) — book "dark blue". V.
- Oyashio "parent current"; cold, nutrient-rich; meets Kuroshio off Japan → fishing. Brit.
- Cromwell Current (Equatorial Undercurrent): eastward, ~100 m depth core, ~300 km wide (book), discovered 1952 by Townsend Cromwell (book 1951) V.
- El Niño: Spanish "the (Christ) child", around Christmas; recurs every 2–7 years; warm water off Peru, weak upwelling, fish (anchovy) loss; La Niña = cool phase, stronger trade winds. NOAA.

### `#atlantic-currents`
- Gulf Stream: from Florida Straits along US east coast, leaves coast near Cape Hatteras, continues as North Atlantic Current. NOAA.
- Antilles Current outside (Atlantic side) the island arc; Caribbean Current inside. V.
- Norwegian Current keeps Norwegian coast ice-free; Irminger warm branch near SW Iceland. BK p135; Brit.
- Canary: cold, southward along NW Africa; upwelling. Book "caused Sahara" overstated.
- Labrador: cold, southward, carries icebergs; Grand Banks fog. Brit.
- Brazil (warm, south), Falkland/Malvinas (cold, north) → Brazil–Malvinas Confluence (~38°S V).
- Benguela: cold, northward, upwelling, Namib desert, rich fishery. NOAA.
- Equatorial Counter Current eastward between NEC and SEC → Guinea Current toward Gulf of Guinea. BK p136.

### `#indian-currents`
- North of ~10°S currents reverse with monsoon; south Indian Ocean gyre anticlockwise steady. NIO/Brit V.
- SMC (Jun–Sep) eastward south of India into Bay of Bengal; NMC (Dec–Feb/Mar) westward south of Sri Lanka. V.
- Somali Current: northward, very fast (~ up to 3.5 m/s) during SW monsoon; upwelling; southward in NE monsoon. V.
- Wyrtki jets: eastward equatorial surface jets during Apr–May and Oct–Nov. V.
- Agulhas: warm, fast western boundary current along SE Africa; retroflection south of Africa back into Indian Ocean; leakage rings into Atlantic. Book calls it "Agulhas cold current" — correct plainly.
- West Australian (cool, northward; weak) vs Leeuwin (warm, southward, unusual) V.
- ACC: eastward around Antarctica, largest current by volume (~130–150 Sv V), connects Atlantic, Indian, Pacific; driven by westerlies (West Wind Drift). NOAA.

### `#currents-effects`
- Cold current deserts: Atacama (Humboldt), Namib (Benguela), Sahara Atlantic coast (Canary), Baja California/Sonoran (California), West Australian. NCERT.
- Gulf Stream + westerlies → mild NW Europe; NAC/Norwegian → ice-free Norwegian ports (Murmansk ice-free too V).
- Fog: Grand Banks (Labrador + Gulf Stream), off Hokkaido (Oyashio + Kuroshio). NCERT.
- Fishing grounds: Grand Banks, Dogger Bank (North Sea), NW Pacific off Japan, Peru, Benguela. NCERT.

### `#coral-reefs`
- Coral polyps belong to phylum Cnidaria (older name Coelenterata), class Anthozoa; secrete CaCO₃ (aragonite) skeleton. NOAA; BK p138.
- Zooxanthellae (symbiotic dinoflagellate algae) provide up to ~90% of coral's food via photosynthesis. NOAA V.
- Conditions: warm water ~20–29°C (book: below 20°C cannot live; 23–27°C ideal), clear water, depth usually < ~50 m (book 45–50 m), normal salinity, oxygenated, 30°N–30°S; absent near large river mouths (fresh water, sediment) and on west coasts in tropics (cold currents/upwelling). NOAA; NCERT; BK p138.
- Corals cannot survive long above low-tide level (book). BK p138.
- "Rainforests of the sea": <1% of ocean floor, ~25% of marine species. NOAA V.
- Coral Triangle Initiative (2009) CT6: Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Papua New Guinea, Solomon Islands, Timor-Leste. CTI-CFF.
- Great Barrier Reef: off Queensland; ~2,300 km; ~2,900 individual reefs, ~900 islands; World Heritage 1981; largest reef system. UNESCO/GBRMPA. Book 2,900 km V.
- 2024: world's largest coral (single colony, Pavona clavus, ~34 × 32 m) found near Solomon Islands (Three Sisters) by National Geographic Pristine Seas, Nov 2024. V. Book "underwater cathedral" nickname V.
- India reef areas: Gulf of Mannar, Gulf of Kachchh, Lakshadweep, Andaman & Nicobar (four major) + patchy (Malvan, Maharashtra; Netrani; off Kerala/Vizhinjam V). BK p138 lists five incl. Maharashtra. MoEFCC/ENVIS V.
- Bleaching: heat stress → zooxanthellae expelled → white skeleton; global bleaching events 1998, 2010, 2014–17, 2023–25 (4th global event announced Apr 2024 NOAA) V → `::: changing` optional.

### `#reef-types`
- Fringing: grows close to shore, narrow shallow lagoon or none; Ningaloo (W Australia), Red Sea. NOAA.
- Platform/patch reefs. BK p139; NOAA.
- Barrier: separated from coast by wide deep lagoon; GBR, Belize Barrier Reef; New Caledonia. NOAA; BK p139 (lagoon ½–10 miles, 10–50 fathoms; GBR 32–180 km offshore).
- Atoll: ring/horseshoe-shaped reef enclosing a lagoon, no central volcanic island; low coral islets on rim; Maldives, Lakshadweep, Marshall Islands (Bikini, Kwajalein). NOAA. Book "atoll lagoon has no island" — rim islets do exist (P139-008).
- Darwin (1842) subsidence theory: fringing → barrier → atoll as volcanic island subsides and reef grows upward; confirmed by drilling at Enewetak (1952: volcanic basalt at ~1,400 m below reef). NOAA/USGS V.
- Daly's glacial control theory; Murray's theory (1880, stand-still/solution) one line each. NCERT older texts V.

### `#deposits`
- Classes: terrigenous (lithogenous), biogenous, hydrogenous (authigenic), cosmogenous. Std; BK p140 (three).
- Book shares: terrigenous 45%, hydrogenous 2% (dated statistics; teach as book figures with "సుమారు/ఒక అంచనా").
- Muds (BK p140–141): blue (organic matter, iron sulphide), red (iron oxide; off Brazil, Yellow Sea? book), green (glauconite), yellow (Huang He loess → Yellow Sea), black (carbon-rich, low oxygen; book "Atlantic").
- Hydrogenous: evaporites (halite/rock salt, gypsum, dolomite from enclosed seas: Dead Sea, Great Salt Lake), manganese nodules, phosphorite. BK p141.
- Biogenous ooze ≥30% skeletal remains; calcareous: Globigerina (foraminifera) ooze — most widespread; pteropod ooze (aragonite, snails); siliceous: radiolarian (book: ~2,000 fathoms, Pacific & Indian), diatom (cold seas, 600–1,200 fathoms, Alaska, Japan, Antarctica). BK p141; Std.
- CCD ~4,000–5,000 m (deeper in Atlantic ~5 km, shallower Pacific ~4.2 km V): below it CaCO₃ dissolves, so red clay/siliceous ooze dominate. Std.
- Wentworth sizes: gravel ≥2 mm; sand 1/16–2 mm; silt 1/256–1/16 mm; clay <1/256 mm (0.0039 mm). Std.

### `#living-resources`
- Plankton drift; phytoplankton (book: anabaena, nostoc, chlorella, algae) and zooplankton (book calls "nekton" — wrong; nekton = active swimmers). BK p142; NOAA.
- Benthos: bottom-dwellers (book p142). Epifauna on, infauna in sediment.
- Demersal fish near bottom (cod, haddock, flatfish); pelagic in water column (sardine, mackerel, tuna). FAO.
- DVM: zooplankton rise at night, sink by day. NOAA. (Book says zooplankton rise at night — consistent.)
- Whaling: IWC moratorium on commercial whaling from 1985–86 season; Japan left IWC in 2019 and resumed commercial whaling in its EEZ; Norway, Iceland object. IWC V → `::: changing`.
- Seals: Pribilof Islands (Alaska) breeding (book "Probilish"); Greenland, Newfoundland, Labrador. BK p143.
- Pearls: oyster secretes nacre around irritant; cultured pearls by implanting nucleus (Mikimoto, Japan 1893 V); India: Gulf of Mannar (Tuticorin/Thoothukudi), Gulf of Kachchh; world: Persian Gulf, Sri Lanka, Japan, W Australia, Venezuela. BK p143.
- Sponges: phylum Porifera, sessile filter feeders. Std.

### `#nonliving-resources`
- Sea salt: evaporation + crystallisation in salt pans; Gujarat produces ~80%+ of India's salt (V, as of year). Salt Commissioner.
- Gypsum CaSO₄·2H₂O, evaporite; cement, fertiliser (book p144).
- Placers: heavy mineral beach sands — ilmenite, rutile, zircon, monazite (thorium), garnet, sillimanite; Chavara (Kerala), Manavalakurichi (TN), Chatrapur (Odisha); AP: Bhimunipatnam–Srikakulam beach sands V. IREL/IBM.
- Offshore petroleum system: source rock, maturation, migration, reservoir, seal, trap. Std. Mumbai High (1974 discovery V); KG basin (D6 gas, 2002 V).
- Polymetallic nodules: Mn, Fe oxides with Ni, Cu, Co (book: 15% Mn, 15% Fe, 0.4% Ni, 0.4% Co, 0.03% Cu? V); 1–20 cm; abyssal plains 4,000–6,000 m; grow few mm per million years. ISA/USGS V.
- India: Pioneer Investor status 1987 (first country) and ISA contract 2002 for 75,000 km² in Central Indian Ocean Basin; Deep Ocean Mission approved June 2021; Samudrayaan (Matsya-6000, 6,000 m, 3 crew) — trial status as of 2026 V → `::: changing`.
- CCZ = Pacific between Hawaii and Mexico; largest nodule field. ISA.
- EEZ = 200 nautical miles (UNCLOS 1982). Std.
- OTEC: needs ≥20°C difference surface vs ~1,000 m; closed cycle uses ammonia; India: NIOT plant at Kavaratti (Lakshadweep) desalination/OTEC V.
- Book "800 million kW capacity" = mixes kW capacity with energy (kWh) → how to answer.
