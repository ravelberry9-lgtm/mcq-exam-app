### 1. `## పర్వతం, పీఠభూమి, మైదానం, లోయ: ఒక చూపులో తేడా ఎలా కనిపెట్టాలి? {#landform-words}` — 1,600 words
Purpose: the student can tell the four second-order/relief words apart by **relief, slope and top surface**, not by one height number; knows why elevation alone misleads.
Owns: relief / relative (local) relief (vs elevation, which L1 owns); valley (definition only; river/glacier valley shapes = Lesson 7); hill vs mountain as a relief question (height bands themselves: link L5); comparison table mountain–plateau–plain–valley; orography (word); escarpment (word, one line; used again in B); why landforms matter for people (water towers, minerals, farming, roads — the AP road-route case).
Opening: link `lesson-05#forces` for "second-order landforms" (one sentence), `lesson-05#mountains` for mountain definition and 2,135 m.
Facts:
- Elevation = height above MSL ([పాఠం 1](lesson-01#heights-reference)); Relief (ఉన్నతి/ఎత్తుపల్లాలు; book: నిమ్నోన్నతాలు p44) = difference between highest and lowest points of an area. — book p44 word; NCERT VI *The Earth Our Habitat* ch.6; standard.
- Mountain: high local relief, steep slopes, narrow summit. Plateau: elevated, broad flat-topped, at least one steep side. Plain: broad, low relief, gentle slope. Valley: elongated low land between higher ground, usually with a river. — NCERT VI ch.6 (V wording).
- A plateau can be higher than many mountains (Tibet average >4,500 m) → height alone does not decide; relief and top surface decide. — V (Britannica Tibet).
- Orology = study of mountains (L5 owns, link); Orography = description of relief/mountain terrain; Orogeny = mountain building (L5 #forces). Q P044-F016 needs all three in one line here.
- Escarpment = long steep slope at the edge of a plateau or upland (Western Ghats western face is one; link `lesson-05#mountains` where that is said). — standard.
- Mountains: store water as snow/glaciers, rivers rise there; plateaus: minerals, waterfalls; plains: farming, dense population, transport. — NCERT VI ch.6 (detail of plateau minerals in B, plains population in C; here one sentence each as preview).
- Road through hills: slope, drainage, rock stability, landslide risk, ecology together (Q GEO-ORIG-07-024). AP example: Ghat roads (Tirumala ghat, Araku/Ananthagiri ghat) — V example names only.
Exam block: book bands (hill 305–2,135 m; mountain >2,135 m) belong to L5 — here only: "ఎత్తు సంఖ్య ఇచ్చి 'ఏ భూస్వరూపం?' అడిగితే పుస్తక పట్టీ వాడండి; 'ఏ ప్రమాణం సరైనది?' అడిగితే relief, slope, form" (Q P044-F006/F007, WG05-P44-003).
Trap: "ఎత్తుగా ఉంది కాబట్టి అది పర్వతమే" → Tibet plateau higher than most mountains.
Questions: GEO-ORIG-07-001, 002, 003, 004, 013, 016, 017, 022, 024; GEO-BOOK-P044-F006, F007, F008, F016, M002; WG05-P44-003.

### 5. `## పీఠభూములు ఎన్ని రకాలు? ఏది ఎక్కడ ఉంది? {#plateau-types}` — 2,000 words
Owns: the book's 5 types with world + India examples, the Appalachian Plateau vs Piedmont province, Tibet/Altiplano/Colorado facts, Patagonia, Malwa, Chotanagpur, Ozark, Columbia Plateau + Columbia/Snake rivers, one table of all types.
Types (book p46–47 names): పర్వతాంతర (Intermontane), గిరిపద (Piedmont), కలశ (Domal), అవశిష్ట (Residual/erosional — dissected detail in #plateau-erosion), లావా (Lava/volcanic).
Facts:
- Intermontane: surrounded by mountains. Tibet: largest and highest plateau, ~25 lakh km² (book p46: 25,00,000 km²), average >4,500 m, between Himalaya (south) and Kunlun (north); uplift from India–Eurasia collision (link L5 #boundaries). — book p46; V Britannica.
- Altiplano: central Andes, mainly Bolivia and Peru (extends to Chile, Argentina as Puna); average ~3,750 m; Lake Titicaca; most extensive high plateau outside Tibet. — book p46 ("second largest"); V Britannica → exam block.
- Colorado Plateau: Four Corners (Arizona, Utah, Colorado, New Mexico); Grand Canyon cut by Colorado River; book calls it intermontane (between Rocky ranges). — book p46; V USGS/NPS.
- Mongolian and Iranian plateaus as one-line extra intermontane examples (V).
- Piedmont: at the foot of mountains, other side plain or sea. Appalachian Plateau west of Appalachians/Valley and Ridge; separate Piedmont province east of Blue Ridge (USGS physiographic provinces). Patagonia: Argentina (and Chile), east of the Andes. Malwa: MP, north of Vindhya (book p47). — book p47; V USGS.
- Domal: raised by upward force; Chotanagpur (Jharkhand, parts Odisha, WB, Chhattisgarh; "mineral heartland" — coal, iron, mica); Ozark (Missouri, Arkansas, USA). — book p47; V.
- Residual: Deccan plateau (book: Karnataka, Telangana up to Chotanagpur), famous for minerals. Detail and dissection in #plateau-erosion.
- Lava: fluid basalt from fissures spreads and hardens (process link L5 #volcano-anatomy); Deccan Traps (Maharashtra and neighbours; link L5 for age/area), Malwa (Deccan basalt in its northern part), Columbia Plateau (Washington, Oregon, Idaho; Columbia River Basalt ~17–6 Ma, V). — book p47.
- Columbia River: rises in British Columbia (Canada), flows through Washington, along Washington–Oregon border to Pacific; ~2,000 km; largest river of Pacific Northwest, biggest flow of any North American river into the Pacific; Snake River is its largest tributary. — V USGS/Britannica. Book p47 says "longest river of America" → exam block (Missouri is longest in USA; Mississippi–Missouri system longest).
- One plateau can sit in two classes (Malwa: piedmont by position, lava by material) — trap.
Exam block: Columbia "longest" option; Altiplano "second largest/second highest".
Trap: Malwa appears in two book lists — both right, because one is position and the other is material.

### 6. `## పీఠభూమిని నదులు కోస్తే ఏమవుతుంది? Mesa, Butte, Deccan {#plateau-erosion}` — 1,300 words

- Residual plateaus rich in minerals (book p47).
