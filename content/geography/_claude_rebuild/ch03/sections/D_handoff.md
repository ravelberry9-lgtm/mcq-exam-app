# Concept D handoff: facts in my sources that belong to other concepts

## Distance units / parallax (source 07)
- 07: Finger-parallax demo (each eye in turn): your viewpoint changes, so the finger seems to shift against the background. This is the idea behind Parallax.
- 07: Annual parallax: as Earth moves along its orbit, a nearby star seems to shift slightly against the distant background stars.
- 07: Parallax angle = half the maximum angular shift, seen from opposite points of the orbit six months apart; the baseline is 1 AU.
- 07: Parallax angle of 1 arcsecond means the distance is about 1 parsec (pc); 1° = 3,600 arcseconds.
- 07: 1 pc ≈ 3.26 light-years ≈ 206,265 AU; 1 kpc = 1,000 pc; 1 Mpc = 1 million pc; 2 pc ≈ 6.52 ly. (Verified: IAU astro4edu glossary https://astro4edu.org/resources/glossary/term/238/; distance in pc = 1 / parallax in arcsec.)
- Note: I used and explained "Light-year" in #sky-brightness because Proxima's distance needs it. The distance concept may re-introduce it.

## Concept K: eight-planet comparisons (source 33, all rows)
- 33: Size, Mass and Density are different properties. Rotation = turning on its own Axis; Revolution = going round the Sun; a planet's day and year are different periods.
- 33: Decreasing order by diameter: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Earth, Venus, Mars, Mercury. Uranus has the larger diameter, Neptune the larger mass. Earth is slightly bigger than Venus. Earth is the largest terrestrial planet and 5th of the eight. Saturn's rings are not counted in its diameter.
- 33: Distances from the Sun (semi-major axes): Mercury 0.39 AU / 57.9 Mkm; Venus 0.72 / 108.2; Earth 1.00 / 149.6; Mars 1.52 / 227.9; Jupiter 5.20 / 778.6; Saturn 9.58 / 1433.5; Uranus 19.2 / 2872.5; Neptune 30.1 / 4495.1. Current NASA values: Mars 228.0, Jupiter 778.5, Saturn 1432.0, Uranus 2867.0, Neptune 4515.0 Mkm. 1 AU = 149,597,870.7 km exactly. Do not take 1.52 − 1 as a fixed Earth–Mars distance. Neptune's orbit is not the edge of the Solar System.
- 33: Sidereal rotation: Mercury 58.65 d (solar day ≈176 d); Venus 243.0 d, retrograde, longer than its year; Earth 23 h 56 m (mean solar day 24 h); Mars 24 h 37 m (a sol ≈ 24 h 39 m); Jupiter 9 h 55 m, the fastest; Saturn 10 h 39 m (Voyager radio), while Cassini ring-wave analysis in 2019 gave ≈10 h 33 m 38 s; Uranus 17 h 14 m, retrograde, tilt ≈98°; Neptune 16 h 7 m.
- 33: Orbital periods: Mercury 88 d (the shortest); Venus 224.7 d; Earth 365.26 d sidereal (tropical ≈365.24; calendar 365/366); Mars 687 d ≈1.88 yr; Jupiter 11.86 yr; Saturn 29.45; Uranus 84.02; Neptune 164.79 (the longest).
- 33: Mean density (g/cm³): Mercury 5.43 (large iron core, 2nd); Venus 5.24; Earth 5.51 (highest); Mars 3.93 (lowest terrestrial); Jupiter 1.33; Saturn 0.69 (lowest, below water's ≈1); Uranus 1.27; Neptune 1.64 (highest giant). Giants in decreasing order: Neptune, Jupiter, Uranus, Saturn.
- 33: Reference temperatures: Mercury 167°C mean surface (large day–night range, no thick atmosphere); Venus 464°C (CO₂ greenhouse, hotter than Mercury); Earth 15°C; Mars −65°C; Jupiter −110, Saturn −140, Uranus −195, Neptune −200°C, all at the 1-bar level. Uranus atmospheric minimum ≈49 K (−224°C) is at a different altitude. Mean and minimum are different questions.
- 33: Prograde vs retrograde rotation: Venus and Uranus are retrograde. Gas-giant rotation values depend on the measurement method (JPL values used).
- 33: Natural moons: Mercury 0, Venus 0, Earth 1, Mars 2 (Phobos is larger and closer, Deimos smaller), Jupiter 115 (NASA, checked 30 Oct 2026; Galilean moons Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto), Saturn 293 (NASA, Aug 2026; Titan largest, Enceladus), Uranus 29 (Webb discovery 19 Aug 2025; Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, Oberon; Titania ≠ Titan), Neptune 16 (NASA, checked 30 Oct 2026; Triton largest). Counts change, so give a date with each.
- 22: Largest by diameter and mass is Jupiter; smallest is Mercury. Highest mean density is Earth, lowest Saturn. Highest mean surface temperature is Venus. "Coldest planet" depends on whether mean or minimum is asked. Fastest rotation is Jupiter, slowest Venus; Mercury has the longest solar day. All four giants have rings. Uranus is bigger than Neptune in diameter, but Neptune has more mass.
- 23: Eight planets in order. The first four are rocky and the next four are giants; temperature and the available material at formation explain the difference. Venus diameter ≈95% of Earth's and mass ≈81.5%, hence "twin planet", but conditions differ.

## Dwarf planets / asteroids / comets / meteors (source 22, 23)
- 22: Pluto is still a Solar System object; it was reclassified as a Dwarf planet and still has an orbit. Ceres is in the main asteroid belt. Eris is linked to the scattered population beyond Neptune. The "exploded planet Phaethon" idea is not the accepted explanation; original material, collisions and Jupiter's gravity are.
- 22, 23: Meteoroid (in space), Meteor (the light in the atmosphere), Meteorite (the piece that reaches the ground). Meteors are mostly seen at mesosphere heights.
- 22: Halley's comet last came near the Sun in 1986, next expected 2061, period ≈76 yr (not exact). Shoemaker–Levy 9 fragments hit Jupiter on 16–22 July 1994. A comet's nucleus, coma, dust tail and ion tail are distinct parts.
- 23: An asteroid is mainly rock or metal; a comet holds ice, dust and rock and releases gas near the Sun.

## Sun (source 22, 23)
- 22: Photosphere is the visible layer. Effective temperature ≈5,800 K; old PYQs use 5778 K; IAU 2015 Resolution B3 nominal value is 5772 K.
- 23: The Sun is a star powered by nuclear fusion; photosphere ≈5,800 K, not a "solid surface". Sunlight drives photosynthesis, evaporation, winds through uneven heating, and the water cycle. Atmosphere and Weather are different.

## Satellites / ISRO (source 22, 23)
- 22: WESAT payload (LBS Institute of Technology for Women, Kerala) flew on PSLV-C58/XPoSat, 1 Jan 2024, on POEM-3; it measured solar irradiance and UV index. It is a payload, not a free-flying satellite.
- 23: Natural vs artificial satellite; Payload = an instrument carried on a satellite.

## Universe / cosmology (source 23)
- 23: Definitions of Universe, Milky Way and Galaxy. Steady-state theory (Bondi, Gold, Hoyle, 1948): new matter forms as the universe expands; later observations favoured the Big Bang. Cosmology vs Climatology (the latter is answer-key residue).

## Earth / Moon / eclipses (source 23)
- 23: Earth's age ≈4.54 billion years by radiometric dating; the oldest rock's age ≠ Earth's age. Conditions for life; the magnetic field deflects charged particles; distance from the Sun alone does not guarantee life.
- 23: Moon: rotation ≈ revolution, so we mostly see one face. Phases come from how much of the sunlit half we see. Solar eclipse = Moon in the middle; lunar eclipse = Earth in the middle; the Moon's tilted orbit means there is no eclipse every month.
- 23: Day and night from rotation. Oblate spheroid; equatorial diameter ≈12,756 km. Evidence for a round Earth: its curved shadow in lunar eclipses, ships disappearing hull-first.
- 23: Self-test: rotation vs revolution; why Venus is hotter than Mercury; phases vs eclipse.
