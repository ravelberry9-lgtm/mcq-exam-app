### `#forces`
- Endogenic (అంతర్జనిత) forces come from inside: diastrophism (folding, faulting, warping), volcanism, earthquakes. BK p44, NCERT ch.6.
- Exogenic (బహిర్జనిత) forces work at the surface with energy from the Sun and gravity: weathering, mass wasting, erosion, transportation, deposition. NCERT.
- Diastrophism → Orogeny (mountain building, tight folding, faulting) vs Epeirogeny (broad uplift or sinking of continents, little folding). NCERT.
- Gradation = Degradation (lowering) + Aggradation (building up by deposition). NCERT (term "gradation"); V wording.
- Denudation = weathering + mass wasting + erosion + transport together. NCERT.
- Landform orders: 1st = continents, ocean basins; 2nd = mountains, plateaus, plains (book: "ప్రధాన భూ ఉపరితల స్వరూపాలు"); 3rd = erosional/depositional landforms by exogenic agents. BK p44.
- Endogenic builds relief, exogenic wears it down; landscape = balance. O05 balance items. NCERT.
- Weathering vs erosion: link `#sedimentary` (owner); fact line here only as "weathering = in place" (feeds O05-001/016).

### `#drift`
- Ortelius (Dutch/Flemish cartographer) 1596 suggested continents were joined. BK p32 (OCR "ఆగ్టీలుయస్"), Britannica. [B32-F2]
- Wegener, German meteorologist/climatologist, proposed drift 1912; book *Die Entstehung der Kontinente und Ozeane* 1915 (English *The Origin of Continents and Oceans*). BK p32 (1912 only), USGS. [B32-F1]
- Pangaea = all continents in one; Panthalassa = surrounding ocean; Tethys between Laurasia (N. America, Europe, most of Asia) and Gondwana (S. America, Africa, India, Australia, Antarctica). BK p31–32. [B31-F1..F5]
- Book: Pangaea broke in the Triassic and continents drift west and toward the equator ("pole-fleeing" force). BK p32; Wegener's two forces: Pohlflucht + tidal force — NCERT. V.
- Evidence: jigsaw fit (continental shelf edge fits better than coastline), matching rocks (e.g. ~2,000 Ma belts Brazil–W Africa), Glossopteris flora in India, Africa, Antarctica, Australia, S. America; Mesosaurus in S. Africa and Brazil; Permo-Carboniferous tillite in India (Gondwana) and other southern continents; placer gold Ghana from Brazil rocks. NCERT ch.4. [B31-F10]
- Weakness: no convincing force/mechanism. USGS. [B32-F3]
- p31 map dates on modern scale: 225 Ma → Late Triassic (book "Permian"); 200 Ma → earliest Jurassic (Triassic–Jurassic boundary 201.4 Ma; book "Triassic"); 150 Ma → Late Jurassic; 65 Ma → earliest Paleogene (K–Pg 66.0 Ma; book "Cretaceous"). ICS. → `::: changing` + `::: exam`. [B31-F6..F9, M2]
- India was part of Gondwana and moved north (rate ~ up to 15–20 cm/yr in Late Cretaceous V); oceans open (Atlantic) and close (Tethys). BK p31, USGS. [B31-F11, F12]
- Breakup sequence Pangaea → Laurasia + Gondwana → present. BK p33. [B33-F12]

### `#seafloor-spreading`
- Arthur Holmes: mantle convection currents as driving force, 1928 (paper 1931). Book says 1980 — wrong. Britannica, NCERT. V year. [B32-F4]
- Mid-ocean ridge: continuous underwater mountain chain at divergent boundaries, ~65,000 km long (V; some sources 60,000–80,000), central rift valley; Mid-Atlantic Ridge S-shaped. BK p32 ("మిడ్ ఓషనిక్ రిడ్జ్"), NOAA. [B32-F15]
- Harry Hess 1960 (published 1962 "History of Ocean Basins"); term "seafloor spreading" by Robert Dietz 1961 V. BK p32 (Hess 1960). [B32-F5]
- Magnetic reversals recorded as symmetric stripes on both sides of ridge: Vine–Matthews (and Morley) 1963. USGS.
- Ocean crust youngest at ridge, older toward trenches; oldest in-place ocean floor ~180 Ma (W Pacific; Jurassic) V; continental rocks up to ~4 Ga. NOAA/USGS. Trap.
- Sediment thin near ridge, thicker away; heat flow high at ridge. NCERT.

### `#plates`
- Plate = rigid slab of lithosphere (crust + uppermost mantle) moving over asthenosphere; may carry both continental and oceanic crust. USGS; link `#mechanical-layers`.
- Plate tectonics synthesis 1960s: Wilson 1965 (transform faults; "plates"), McKenzie & Parker 1967, W. Jason Morgan 1967 talk / 1968 paper; book: "Morgan 1967 explained", "Wilson coined 'plate tectonic' 1965" — say "Wilson 1965 first described plates and transform faults" V. [B32-F6, F7]
- Book classes: major >20 million km² (7), minor 1–20 million km² (14), micro <1 million km² (~60; OCR "66"). BK p32. [B32-F17..F19, M5]
- 7 major (book): Pacific 102.9 M km² (largest), North American 75.9 M, Eurasian 67.8 M, African, Antarctic, Indo-Australian (now often India + Australia), South American. BK p32; areas match Wikipedia/Bird-model values V. [B32-F20, F21]
- Minor (book list): Cocos, Nazca, Caribbean, Scotia, Arabian, Burma, Somali, Philippine Sea, Caroline, New Hebrides (+ Juan de Fuca on p33 map). BK p32–33. One table with location. [B32-F23..F32, B33-F4..F9]
- Micro (book): Iranian, Aegean, (Molucca Sea), Okhotsk. BK p32. Same table. [B32-F33..F36]
- Plate counts are model-dependent: 7 or 8 major (Indo-Australian split → 8); Bird (2003) model 52 plates. `::: changing`. [B32-F16]
- Driving forces: mantle convection (Holmes), ridge push, slab pull (slab pull now thought strongest). USGS/Britannica V.
- Speeds: few cm/yr (fingernail rate — the analogy is Lesson 4's; just link). USGS.
- Pangaea Proxima (Ultima): one of several future-supercontinent models, ~250 Myr. BK p32 ("250 మి.సం"). [B32-F22]
- Plate boundary ≠ political boundary; Pacific plate split at map edges by projection. [B33-F1, F13]
- Antarctic/African/Australian plates include surrounding ocean floor. [B33-F7..F9]

### `#boundaries`
- Divergent: plates move apart, magma rises, new oceanic crust; on land a rift valley (East African Rift; Red Sea = young ocean). USGS. [O06-001, B32-F9, B33-F10, F11]
- Mid-Atlantic Ridge divergent; Iceland sits on it. [B32-F14]
- Convergent: O–C → denser oceanic plate subducts, trench + volcanic arc on land (Andes: Nazca under South America; Cascades: Juan de Fuca). O–O → trench + island arc (Japan, Aleutians, Mariana). C–C → neither subducts easily, collision, thick crust, folded mountains, few volcanoes (Himalaya: India–Eurasia; Alps). USGS. [O06-002, O06-022, B32-F8, F11, F12, B33-F2, F3]
- Book p32: compression at convergent → fold mountains; Himalaya in Tethys, Tertiary; Rockies and Andes by Americas moving west against Pacific. BK p32.
- Rockies: Laramide orogeny (~80–55 Ma) by low-angle subduction of Farallon plate. USGS V. [B32-F13]
- Transform: plates slide past horizontally, crust neither made nor destroyed; San Andreas (Pacific–North American); Wilson 1965. [O06-003, B32-F10]
- Names: constructive / destructive / conservative (NCERT-era terms).
- Hotspot: fixed-ish mantle plume, volcanism inside plates; Hawaii chain gets older to NW; Iceland = ridge + hotspot. USGS. [O06-004, O06-017]
- Deccan Traps linked to Réunion hotspot (one line, V); detail in `#volcano-anatomy`.

### `#folds-faults`
- Fold = layers bend without breaking under compression; anticline = upfold (arch), syncline = downfold (trough). BK p45 (అభినతి/అపనతి — book prints them swapped? check: book "ఎత్తయిన భాగాన్ని అభినతి (ఏంటీక్లైన్), క్రిందకు వంగే భాగాన్ని అపనతి (సింక్లైన్)" = correct). [O06-008]
- Fold types: symmetrical, asymmetrical, overturned, recumbent, isoclinal; nappe = recumbent fold thrust far. NCERT/standard V.
- Fault = fracture with displacement; hanging wall above inclined plane, footwall below. USGS.
- Normal fault: tension, HW moves down. Reverse: compression, HW up. Thrust: low-angle reverse (Himalayan Main Boundary Thrust V). Strike-slip: horizontal along strike. USGS. [O06-005, O06-006, O06-007, O06-024]
- Horst = block raised between faults; graben = block dropped; rift valley = long graben. BK p46 (హార్స్ట్ భ్రంశోద్ధ శిలావిన్యాసం; పగులు లోయ). Great Rift Valley largest (book). [examples in `#mountains`]
- Narmada and Tapi flow in rift valleys (graben) — standard India fact V; one line here.

- Orology = study of mountains. BK p44.

BK p44–45.

### `#earthquake`
- Earthquake = shaking from sudden release of energy, travelling as seismic waves. BK p34, USGS. [B34-F1]
- Focus/hypocentre (నాభి) = point inside where rupture starts; epicentre (అధికేంద్రం) = point on surface directly above; strongest shaking usually near epicentre. BK p34 + PYQ APPSC Gr-2 2024 (incorrect: "epicentre is directly below focus"). [O04-015, O04-016, O04-020, O04-028, B34-F2, F3, M1]
- Book says waves reach epicentre first — standard: P waves reach nearest surface point first; keep as "first and strongest near epicentre" V.
- Elastic rebound: stress builds, rock bends, then slips; H.F. Reid after 1906 San Francisco. USGS. [B34-F4]
- Causes (book list + standard): surface (landslides, avalanches, mine/cave roof collapse, nuclear tests = small); volcanic (magma movement before/after eruption); isostatic adjustment (load change, e.g. sediment, ice); tectonic (strongest, most common). Book's "underground uranium/thorium explosions" is not a recognised cause → one-line correction. Human: reservoir-induced (Koyna 1967, M ~6.3 V). [B34-F5..F9, M2]
- Depth: shallow 0–70 km, intermediate 70–300 km, deep 300–700 km (USGS); book shallow 0–60, intermediate 60–300. `::: changing`/`::: exam`. [B34-F10..F12, M3]
- Book: deeper focus → wider felt area, lower intensity at surface; shallower → smaller area, stronger. BK p34–35. [B34-F13 says depth alone doesn't decide damage]
- Belts: circum-Pacific (~81% of largest quakes, USGS), Alpide/Mediterranean–Himalayan (~17%), mid-ocean ridges (shallow, mostly small). USGS V.
- India: IS 1893:2016 map — zones II, III, IV, V. A 2025 revision added Zone VI (whole Himalayan arc) but BIS withdrew it by gazette 3 March 2026, so the 2016 four-zone map applies (Vision IAS / ET, 7 Mar 2026). `::: changing` as-of 2026-03. AP detail link `#ap-link`.

### `#seismic-records`
- P, S body waves: link `#seismic-evidence` (owner). Book speeds P "~8 km/s", S "~4–5"? OCR garbled — do NOT restate; link.
- Surface waves (L waves) formed when body waves reach the surface; travel along it; slowest; arrive last; largest ground motion, most damage. BK p35, USGS. [B35-F10, F13]
- Love wave: horizontal side-to-side, perpendicular to travel; Rayleigh: rolling, elliptical (like ocean wave), vertical + along-travel. USGS. [B35-F11, F12, M3]
- Book: L waves low frequency, long wavelength; S high frequency, short wavelength — keep general: frequency depends on source and path; wavelength = speed / frequency. [B35-F14]
- Propagation direction vs particle motion. [B35-F18]
- Seismology = study of earthquakes and seismic waves. [B36-F1]
- Seismometer/seismograph (భూకంప లేఖిని) records; seismogram (భూకంప తరంగలేఖిని) = the record. BK p35. Mass stays still (inertia) while frame moves. USGS. [B35-F15, F16, M4, B36-F17]

### `#magnitude`
- Magnitude = size of the event (one value); intensity = shaking/damage at a place (many values). USGS. [B34-F14, B36-F4, O04-031]
- Mercalli (1902) → Modified Mercalli Intensity, I–XII, qualitative, from effects. BK p36 ("1–12"), USGS. Book: damage from 8? V. [B36-F2]
- Richter 1935, local magnitude ML, California. BK p36. Book: "0–9, 10 points" — wrong as limit; scale is open-ended. [B36-F3]
- +1 magnitude = 10× amplitude; ~31.6 (≈32)× energy (book 30×); +2 = 1,000×. USGS. [B36-F6..F9, M2]
- Book: 5–5.9 moderate; ≥6 damage begins — keep only as rough guide. BK p36.
- Moment magnitude Mw (Hanks & Kanamori 1979) from fault area × slip × rigidity; does not saturate; USGS uses Mw for large quakes. Media still say "Richter". [B36-F5]
- Same magnitude, different damage (depth, distance, soil, buildings, population, time). [B36-F10, O04-031]
- Book: Richter based on TNT energy — not the definition; skip or one line V.

### `#tsunami`
- "Tsunami" Japanese tsu (harbour) + nami (wave). BK p36, NOAA. [B36-F11]
- Cause: sudden vertical seafloor movement lifts water column; also submarine landslides, volcanic eruptions/collapse (Krakatau 1883), rare impacts. NOAA. [B36-F12, F13]
- Deep ocean: very fast (~700–800 km/h V), long wavelength, low height; near coast slows, wavelength shortens, height grows. NOAA. [B36-F14]
- Most in Pacific (Ring of Fire). BK p36.
- 26 December 2004 (book OCR "6") Sumatra–Andaman Mw 9.1 (USGS), Indian Ocean; Nagapattinam (Tamil Nadu) worst-hit Indian district. BK p36. [B36-F15, F16, M4]
- Not a tidal wave (not caused by tides). Trap.
- INCOIS, Hyderabad: Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre, operational 2007. INCOIS V.
- Book "7.5+ near/under sea → tsunami" = rough; size and vertical motion matter. BK p36.

### `#volcano-anatomy`
- Magma/lava: link `#igneous`. Book: magma contains gases, loses them at surface and is then lava. BK p37. [B37-F1, F2]
- Rise: buoyancy, gas pressure, fractures, tectonic setting. [B37-F20]
- Gases: water vapour dominant, then CO₂, SO₂ (book "99%" together). BK p40, USGS. [B37-F3, B40-F1]
- Magma chamber (శిలాద్రవపు నిలువ), volcanic pipe/conduit (అగ్నిపర్వత పైప్), vent (కుహరం), crater (జ్వాలాబిలం/క్రేటర్), crater lake (జ్వాలాబిల సరస్సు), caldera (కాల్డెరా; collapse after chamber empties; >1 km V). BK p37, USGS. [B37-F4..F8, M1, M2, O06-011]
- Lonar Lake (Buldhana, Maharashtra) = meteorite impact crater in Deccan basalt; book lists it under craters. [B37-F9]
- Central eruption (vent) vs fissure eruption (long cracks, fluid lava, plateaus). BK p37. [B37-F10, F14]
- Deccan Traps: fissure flood basalt; main phase ~66 Ma (book "60–70 Ma"); area today ~5 lakh km² (book 5,20,000?); layers 15–100 m (book). [B37-F15, F16, M4]
- Silica ↑ → viscosity ↑ → gas trapped → explosive; basaltic 45–52% SiO₂, fluid (NPS). Book acid >65%, basic <45% (old terms; not pH). [B37-F17..F19, M5, O06-023]

### `#volcano-types`
- Shield (broad, gentle, fluid basalt; Mauna Loa largest active), composite/stratovolcano (lava + pyroclast layers; Fuji, Vesuvius), cinder cone (fragments around vent; Parícutin V), lava dome (viscous lava piles at vent). USGS. [B37-F11..F13, O06-009, O06-010, O06-020]
- Active / dormant / extinct: book definitions; modern: "active" = erupted in Holocene / could erupt; extinct only if no future eruption expected; labels can change (Vesuvius). USGS, GVP. [B38-F1..F3]
- Counts: USGS ~1,350 potentially active (Holocene) worldwide (book 1,350 "active" p38) vs book p40 "about 500 active"; differ by definition and time window. `::: changing`. [B38-F4, B40-F16]
- Cotopaxi (Ecuador, ~5,897 m), "one of highest active" (book: highest active V); Ojos del Salado (Chile–Argentina, 6,893 m GVP; book 6,887) = highest volcano. [B38-F5, F6]
- Etna (Sicily), Vesuvius (Naples, 79 CE Pompeii), Stromboli (Aeolian/Lipari Is.; "Lighthouse of the Mediterranean", frequent small eruptions). BK p38. [B38-F7, F8]
- Mauna Loa (Hawaii; largest active shield); Pelée (Martinique, 1902 pyroclastic flow killed ~28,000 V); Merapi, Bromo, Semeru (Java); Agung (Bali); Mayon (Philippines). BK p38. [B38-F9..F11, F13]
- Barren Island (Andaman Sea) = India's only active; Narcondam = dormant (book). [B38-F12, F15]
- Fuji, Krakatau dormant per book; quiet ≠ extinct. Haleakalā (Maui) last ~1600s V. [B38-F14, F15]
- Book "extinct": Kilimanjaro (Kibo dormant, fumaroles V), Ararat (Holocene uncertain V), Rainier (USGS: active, lahar hazard). Correct in prose. [B38-F16, F17]
- Pyroclastic density current: fast ground-hugging hot gas + ash + rock; lahar: volcanic mudflow, can happen after eruption (rain). USGS. [B38-F18, F19, O06-012, O06-021]

### `#intrusive`
- Discordant (cut across layers: dyke, neck) vs concordant (parallel: sill, laccolith, lopolith, phacolith). NCERT.
- Dyke = vertical wall-like sheet cutting layers; Great Dyke of Zimbabwe (~550 km, layered mafic–ultramafic complex V); book: Dhuandhar falls (Jabalpur), Singhbhum, Western Ghats. BK p38. [B38-F20, F22]
- Sill = horizontal sheet parallel to layers; stays a sill even if later tilted. Whin Sill (England). Book: Kadapa minerals due to basalt sill → standard: dolerite sills in Cuddapah basin, chrysotile asbestos at Pulivendula where sill meets Vempalle dolomite (GSI V). BK p38–39. [B38-F21]
- Laccolith = dome-roofed, flat floor (Henry Mts, La Sal); bysmalith = laccolith pushed up along faults, plug-like; phacolith = lens in fold crest/trough (book: Ajmer); lopolith = saucer (book: Amboli Hills, Jogeshwari Mumbai V; Bushveld largest V). BK p39. [B39-F1..F4, M1]
- Batholith = huge granite mass deep in crust, exposed after erosion; stock <100 km² exposed area; boss = small rounded; volcanic neck = solidified conduit exposed (Ship Rock). Book: Bhongir, Ramanagaram hills (Karnataka), Ranchi plateau. BK p39. [B39-F5..F8, M2]
- PYQ APPSC Gr-1 2022 on batholith/phacolith/dyke/sill matching. BK p39.

### `#volcano-products`
- Pyroclast: Greek pyr (fire) + klastos (broken). BK p39. [B39-F10]
- Ash <2 mm, lapilli 2–64 mm, bomb (molten, shaped in flight) / block (solid) >64 mm. USGS. [B39-F11..F14, M3, M4]
- Pumice floats (very vesicular, low bulk density); scoria darker, mafic, denser. Rock definition link `#igneous`. [B39-F15, F16]
- Pele's hair = thin glass threads (Hawaiian goddess Pele). BK p39. [B39-F17]
- Hot lava glows red/white by temperature; cooled rock colour from minerals (book "black after cooling" — not always). BK p40. [B40-F2]
- Book p40 lava by silica (rhyolite 65–75%, andesite 50–60%, basalt <50%, kimberlite) → link `#igneous`; kimberlite = link `#igneous` / `#ap-link`.

### `#hot-springs`
- Hot spring: groundwater heated at depth rises naturally. USGS. [B40-F9]
- Geothermal energy = Earth's internal heat. [B40-F10]
- Geyser: hot spring with constricted plumbing → intermittent eruptions (Old Faithful, Yellowstone USA; Geysir, Iceland; New Zealand). BK p40. [B40-F12, F13]
- Fumarole: vent of steam and gases (solfatara if sulphurous). Book: sulphur minerals deposit. [B40-F14]
- Mud volcano: gas/water pressure pushes mud; not magmatic. Book: Sicily, N. New Zealand. India: Baratang (Andaman) V. [B40-F15]
- Book table (state-wise): HP – Manikaran (Parvati valley; pilot geothermal plant), Vashisht (near Manali), Tattapani, Kheerganga (Parvati), Jwalamukhi (Kangra; natural gas flame, not hot spring); Uttarakhand – Gaurikund, Tapovan (Joshimath); Ladakh – Panamik (Nubra; book "near Siachen"); Sikkim – Yumthang; WB – Bakreshwar (Birbhum); Odisha – Taptapani (Ganjam); Karnataka – Bendru Theertha (Puttur, Dakshina Kannada); Telangana – Pagideru (Manuguru, Bhadradri Kothagudem; Singareni geothermal pilot V). BK p40. [B40-F17..F28, M3..M5]

### `#volcano-distribution`
- Ring of Fire: ~75% of world's volcanoes, ~90% of quakes (USGS/NatGeo V); horseshoe of subduction zones. BK p40 ("రెండవ వంతు" OCR — likely "మూడింట రెండు వంతులు" two-thirds). [B41-F1]
- Aleutian–Alaska (Pacific under N. America); Kamchatka–Kuril–Japan (Pacific subducting); Andes (Nazca). [B41-F2..F4]
- Indonesia: book "about 7 active" — wrong; ~120+ Holocene volcanoes (GVP ~130 V). `::: changing`/correction. [B41-F5]
- Mediterranean–Asian (Alpine–Himalayan) belt: mixed processes; Etna, Vesuvius, Stromboli, Elbrus (Caucasus), Ararat (E. Turkey), Damavand (Iran). BK p41. [B41-F6, F7]
- African rift: Kilimanjaro, Mt Kenya; Mt Cameroon is on the separate Cameroon Volcanic Line (book puts it in rift; book also says "Guinea" country — Cameroon). BK p41. [B41-F8, F9]
- Mid-Atlantic: Iceland (ridge + hotspot), Azores, Canary, Cape Verde, St Helena — settings differ. BK p41. [B41-F10, F12]
- Hawaii: hotspot, islands older to NW. [B41-F11]
- Generalisation: most at plate margins, some intraplate. [B41-F15]

### `#weathering`
- Definition: link `#sedimentary`; fact line not repeated.
- Physical: frost wedging needs water in cracks + repeated freeze–thaw; thermal expansion/contraction + exfoliation (onion-skin) in deserts (book p47 pediments: "temperature differences → expansion/contraction"); unloading/sheeting joints in granite; salt crystal growth (coasts, deserts); granular disintegration. NCERT. [O05-005]
- Chemical: solution; carbonation (CO₂ + water → carbonic acid, dissolves limestone/calcite → caves); oxidation (O₂ + iron minerals → red/brown, rust); hydration (minerals absorb water, swell); hydrolysis (feldspar → clay). NCERT, BGS. [O05-006, O05-007, O05-014, O05-018, O05-019]
- Biological: roots widen cracks (physical) + organic acids (chemical); burrowing animals, lichens. [O05-008]
- Climate: cold/dry → physical; hot-humid → chemical (deep weathering, laterite link `#metamorphic`? no — lateritic soils next soil lesson, one line). NCERT.
- AP: exfoliation domes and tors in Rayalaseema/Anantapur granites V; Borra Caves (Ananthagiri, ASR district) limestone karst by solution V.

### `#mass-wasting`
- Mass wasting/mass movement: downslope movement under gravity; no carrying agent. NCERT. [O05-023]
- Water adds weight, raises pore pressure, reduces friction; gravity is the driver. [O05-011]
- Slow: soil creep (tilted posts, trees, walls), solifluction (thawed soil over frozen ground). [O05-010]
- Rapid flows: earthflow, mudflow, debris flow (water + mud + rock + wood; fast, channelled), lahar. [O05-012]
- Landslides: slump (rotational, curved surface), debris slide, rockslide (along plane), rockfall (free fall/bounce from cliff). [O05-009, O05-020, O05-021, O05-015]
- Triggers: heavy rain, earthquakes, undercutting, road cuts, deforestation. India: Himalaya, Western Ghats; Wayanad (Kerala) 30 July 2024 V; Kedarnath 2013 V (one each max).

### `#erosion-deposition`
- Erosion = agent (running water, wind, glacier, waves, groundwater) removes material. [O05-002]
- Transportation: material carried while in motion; modes solution, suspension, saltation, traction (bed load). NCERT V. [O05-003, O05-017]
- Deposition: when velocity/carrying capacity falls. [O05-004, O05-013]
- Erosion and deposition together: meander outer bank cut, inner bank point bar. [O05-022]
- Differential erosion: hard rock stays high. [O05-024]
- Each agent's landforms → next lesson (one sentence, no link).
