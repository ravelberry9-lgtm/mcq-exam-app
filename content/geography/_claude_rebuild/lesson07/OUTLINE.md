### `#agents-overview`
- Running water, groundwater, glaciers, waves/currents, wind are the main geomorphic agents; each erodes and deposits. Link L5 `#forces`. NCERT ch.7.
- Running water is the most widespread agent on land; wind dominates only in hot dry deserts; glaciers in high mountains and polar areas; groundwater landforms need soluble rock. NCERT ch.7.
- Erosion processes: hydraulic action (force of water, air compressed in cracks), abrasion / corrasion (అపఘర్షణ; load used as tools), attrition (load pieces knock and round each other), solution / corrosion (chemical dissolving). Plucking (ice), deflation (wind) are agent-specific, owned in `#glacier-basics`, `#wind-erosion`. NCERT; BK p053 (అపఘర్షణ, ఉత్పాటనం). V wording.
- River course: upper (youth; steep, vertical erosion, V valley, waterfalls), middle (maturity; lateral erosion, meanders, floodplain begins), lower (old age; gentle, deposition, floodplain, levees, delta). NCERT (youth/maturity/old stages).
- W. M. Davis "geographical cycle" (1899): youth → maturity → old age ending in a peneplain; Walther Penck (1920s) stressed uplift and erosion acting together — one line. Britannica. V year 1899.
- APPSC Group-2 2024 PYQ: delta – running water, cirque – glaciers, cliffs – sea waves, sand dunes – wind. BK p056. [B54-F23, O08-023]

### `#river-valleys`
- Vertical erosion (downcutting) dominates in the upper course; lateral erosion widens valleys downstream. NCERT.
- V-shaped valley: river deepens floor; weathering and mass wasting push the sides back, giving V. NCERT; link L5. [B50-F1, W50-1]
- Gorge: very deep, narrow, steep (near-vertical) walls, width top ≈ bottom; canyon: steep step-like sides, often wider at top, commonly in horizontally bedded sedimentary rock in arid areas; canyon is a variety of gorge; no fixed size rule. NCERT ch.7 (gorge/canyon distinction). [B50-F2 F3, W50-2 W50-3, B49-F38]
- Grand Canyon: Colorado River, Arizona, ~446 km (277 river miles) long, up to ~1.8 km (6,000 ft) deep. NPS. V. [B50-F4, W50-4]
- Gandikota gorge: Penna (Pennar) river through the Erramala hills near Gandikota fort, Jammalamadugu mandal, YSR Kadapa district, AP; called "Grand Canyon of India". BK p052 ("15 km" in book — V, do not quote unless verified). APTDC. V. [B50-F5, W50-5]
- Book "బైసన్ గార్జ్" on Godavari in Eastern Ghats = Papikondalu stretch (Bison Gorge V). BK p052. V — teach only if a standard source names it.
- Potholes: pebbles/sand rotated in eddies drill round holes in bedrock river beds; plunge pools are large potholes at waterfall bases. NCERT. [B50-F6, W50-6, B50-F10, W50-9]
- Interlocking spurs: river winds round alternating projecting ridges in the upper valley. BK p052. [B50-F11, W50-10]
- Rapids (fast turbulent flow over steep/rocky bed, often hard–soft bands), cascades (series of small falls), cataracts (large rapids / falls; Nile cataracts) — continuum, no universal height cut-off. BK p052 (book also "pointed rapids = cataracts"). [B50-F9 F12, W50-11]
- Waterfalls: sudden drop; causes: hard rock over soft rock (differential erosion; link L5), fault scarp, hanging valley, plateau edge; waterfall retreats upstream as plunge pool undercuts. NCERT, USGS. [B49-F40, B50-F8, W49-23, W50-8]
- Knickpoint = abrupt change of gradient in the long profile (often a waterfall/rapid). Britannica. [B50-F13]
- Indian waterfalls (one table max 5 rows): Jog (Sharavathi, Karnataka), Dudhsagar (Mandovi, Goa), Chitrakote (Indravati, Chhattisgarh; "Niagara of India"), Kunchikal (highest? — avoid), AP: Talakona (Tirupati dt), Ethipothala (Chandravanka, Palnadu dt). V each.
- Rock benches (రాతి బల్లలు): steps where hard rock resists and soft rock is removed; strath terrace = bedrock bench with thin alluvium. BK p052. [B50-F7, W50-7]

### `#river-residual`
- Peneplain (పెనిప్లెయిన్): broad surface reduced to very low relief by long erosion; end of Davis cycle; Latin paene (almost) + planus (flat). BK p048 (Telangana plateau Godavari–Krishna basins, Ranchi plateau, Subansiri/Manas areas of Assam, around Hudson Bay). [B50-F14, W50-12]
- Monadnock: resistant residual hill above a peneplain; named after Mount Monadnock, New Hampshire. Britannica. [B50-F15, W50-13]
- Badlands (కందర మండలాలు): dense gullies and sharp ridges in soft sediment, sparse vegetation, intense runoff; India: Chambal ravines (MP–Rajasthan–UP); USA Badlands National Park, South Dakota. NPS; V Chambal. [B50-F16, W50-14]
- Escarpment: long steep slope separating two level surfaces; by faulting or erosion. Britannica. [W50-15]
- Embankment (common English) = raised bank for road/railway/flood control; not a natural landform. [B50-F17] (retire candidate)

### `#river-deposition`
- Depositional plain = broad low-relief land built by accumulated transported sediment. [B48-F2, W48-2]
- Floodplain (వరద మైదానం): built by channel migration, point-bar growth and overbank flood deposits; Ganga, Brahmaputra, Godavari, Mississippi. BK p049, NCERT. [B48-F3, B50-F18, W48-3 W48-4, W50-16]
- Natural levee (సహజ లెవీ / సహజ కట్ట): floodwater spilling over slows, drops coarse sediment next to the channel first; low ridge parallel to river; Mississippi, Po, Ganga. Human embankments are artificial levees (trap). BK p049, p052. [B48-F5, B50-F19, W48-7 W48-8, W50-17]
- River terrace (నదీ వేదిక): step left above the river when it cuts down again (rejuvenation/uplift/base-level fall); fill (alluvial) vs strath (bedrock) terraces; not every bench is a terrace. BK p049. [B48-F4, B50-F20, W48-5 W48-6, W50-18]
- Alluvial fan (ఒండలి విసనకర్ర): where a mountain stream leaves a confined valley, slope and speed fall, sediment spreads as a fan; channels shift; Kosi and Gandak megafans in Bihar (Kosi "Sorrow of Bihar"). BK p049 ("వండలి వీవనలు", Shiwalik foot, Gandak, Kosi), NCERT. [O08-003 O08-017, B48-F6, B50-F21, W50-19, B48-G1]
- Meander (నదీ వంకర): outer bank = cut bank (erosion; river cliff), inner bank = point bar (deposition) → meander migrates downstream and sideways. BK p053, NCERT. [O08-001 O08-016 O08-022, B51-F1 F2, W50-20, W51-1]
- Oxbow lake: neck narrows → river cuts through in a flood → old loop sealed by deposits → crescent lake. BK p053. [O08-002, B51-F3, W51-2]
- Braided channel: high/variable coarse load, fluctuating discharge, erodible banks → bars split the flow; Brahmaputra in Assam. BK p053; V Brahmaputra. [B51-F4, W51-3]
- Lacustrine plain (సరోవరీయ మైదానం): lake-bed sediments exposed when lake drains; Kashmir Valley Karewas (lacustrine–fluvial deposits as raised tablelands; saffron cultivation). BK p049 ("కాశ్మీర్ లోయ మైదానం"), GSI. V Karewa saffron. [B48-F19 F20, W48-9 W48-10]

### `#delta-estuary`
- Delta forms when sediment supply at the mouth exceeds removal by waves, tides and currents; needs large load, shallow sea, sheltered coast. NCERT. [O08-004 O08-024, B48-F8, W48-11]
- Name from the Greek letter Δ, used for the Nile delta (Herodotus). Britannica. V.
- Plan shapes: arcuate (fan, Nile), bird-foot / digitate (long finger-like distributaries; Mississippi, modern Balize lobe), cuspate (tooth-shaped, waves spread sediment both sides; Tiber, Ebro), estuarine (filling an estuary). These are ideal types; real deltas are classed by river / wave / tide dominance (Galloway 1975 triangle; one line). Britannica. [B48-F9 F11, W48-12 W48-13 W48-16]
- Book also lists "lobate" (Krishna, Penna, Mahanadi) and puts Godavari, Cauvery in arcuate — model-dependent; see Changing table.
- Ganga–Brahmaputra–Meghna delta: largest delta in the world (~1 lakh km² V), tide-influenced, Sundarbans mangroves (UNESCO site). Britannica, V. [B48-F10, W48-14]
- East-coast rivers Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery build deltas; west-coast Narmada and Tapi form estuaries (fast flow in rift valleys, hard rocks, strong tides of Gulf of Khambhat — V). NCERT India Physical Environment ch.3. [B48-F12, W48-15 W48-17]
- Amazon: enormous load, but strong tides/currents and deep shelf carry sediment away; mouth is an estuary-like system; some sediment settles on the shelf and drifts NW. Britannica, V. [B48-F13]
- Estuary = drowned river mouth where tide mixes fresh and salt water; funnel-shaped; good harbours. NCERT.
- Delta outlines change (sediment supply, subsidence, dams, sea level); compare map dates. [B48-F14] (retire candidate)

### `#glacier-basics`
- Glacier (హిమనీనదం): mass of ice on land, formed from compacted snow over years, moving slowly under its own weight. USGS / NSIDC. [B49-F35]
- Types: valley/alpine glacier; continental ice sheet (Antarctica, Greenland). NSIDC.
- Iceberg: block calved from a glacier or ice shelf, floating; about 90% below water (density ~0.9). NOAA/NSIDC. V. [B49-F36]
- Crevasse (హిమనీనద బిలం): deep crack in the brittle upper ice where tension builds (bends, speed differences); book p053. NSIDC. [B51-F12, W51-11]
- Glacial erosion: plucking (ఉత్పాటనం; ice freezes onto jointed rock and pulls blocks out) and abrasion (అపఘర్షణ; embedded rock grinds bedrock, striations, polish). BK p053, NCERT. [W51-19]
- India one line: Siachen (Karakoram, ~76 km V), Gangotri (source of Bhagirathi, Uttarakhand). V.

### `#glacial-erosion`
- Cirque (హిమగర్తం; armchair / amphitheatre hollow at valley head); tarn = lake in it after ice melts; corrie (Scotland), cwm (Wales). BK p049, p053. [O08-011, B48-F1, B51-F5, W51-4]
- Arête (ఎరిటే; knife-edge "comb ridge" between two cirques/valleys). BK p053 (book "2 or 3 cirques"). [B51-F7, W51-6]
- Horn (హార్న్; pyramidal peak where three or more cirques erode headward; Matterhorn, Alps). BK p053 ("3 or 4"). [B51-F6, W51-5]
- Col (కాల్; saddle / pass where two cirques from opposite sides meet; useful as passes). BK p053. [B51-F8, W51-7]
- U-shaped valley / glacial trough: a valley glacier widens and deepens a former V valley; broad floor, steep sides. BK p053. [B51-F9, W51-8]
- Hanging valley (లంబమాన లోయ): tributary glacier is thinner, erodes less, so its floor is left high above the main trough; waterfalls drop from it. BK p053. [B51-F10, W51-9]
- Fjord (ఫియార్డ్): deep glacial trough later flooded by the sea; Norway (Sognefjord), Chile, New Zealand, Greenland, Alaska; Scottish "sea loch" similar ("loch" = lake too). BK p049, p053. [B49-F37, B51-F13, W49-21, W51-12, B51-G1]
- Roche moutonnée (షీప్ రాక్): stoss (up-ice) side smooth, gentle by abrasion; lee (down-ice) side steep, rough by plucking. BK p053 (book reverses abrasion/plucking weighting in OCR; teach standard). [B51-F14 F15, W51-13]
- Crag and tail: resistant crag faces up-ice and shelters a tapering tail of softer rock/till on the down-ice side; Edinburgh Castle Rock and the Royal Mile V. BK p053. [B51-F11, W51-10]

### `#glacial-deposition`
- Till: unsorted, unstratified mixture (clay to boulders) dropped directly by ice; outwash: sand and gravel sorted and layered by meltwater streams. NCERT, USGS. [B49-F3 F4, B51-F17 F18, W49-3 W49-4, W51-15]
- Moraine (మొరైన్): ridge or heap of till; terminal/end moraine marks maximum advance; lateral (sides), medial (where two glaciers join), ground moraine (sheet). NCERT. [O08-012 O08-021, B51-F16, W49-2, W51-14 W51-20]
- Till plain (ground-moraine plain): gently rolling; central USA (Midwest), eastern England. BK p050. [B49-G1]
- Outwash plain (sandur): Holland, Germany (book); Iceland sandur V. BK p050. [B49-F2, W49-5]
- Drumlin (డ్రమ్లిన్): streamlined oval till hill, long axis parallel to ice flow, blunt end up-ice; drumlin field = "basket of eggs" (కోడిగుడ్ల గంప). BK p053. [B51-F19 F20, W51-16]
- Esker (ఎస్కర్): long winding ridge of sorted sand/gravel laid by meltwater in a tunnel in or under the ice. BK p053. [B51-F21, W51-17]
- Kame (కేమ్): irregular mound of sorted meltwater sand/gravel deposited against ice; height varies (book "20–60 m" not a rule). BK p053. [B51-F22, W51-18]
- Glacial plains: from ice-laid till and meltwater outwash. [B49-M1]

### `#wind-erosion`
- Desert defined by aridity (very low precipitation), not by sand; most of the Sahara is rock and gravel V (~20–25% sand). USGS "Deserts: Geology and Resources". V. [B49-F34 linked here, W49-22]
- Aeolian processes: deflation, abrasion, attrition, transport, deposition. USGS. [B52-F1, B52-F10, W52-20]
- Deflation hollow / blowout (Qattara Depression, Egypt — partly deflation, V). [B52-F11, W52-7]
- Desert pavement / deflation armour: closely packed pebbles left after fines are removed; shields fines below; modern view also adds dust accretion beneath clasts. USGS. [B52-F12 F13, W52-8 W52-9]
- Reg (gravel plain) vs hamada (bare rock plateau) vs erg (sand sea). BK p048, p054. [B52-F14, W52-10, B52-G1]
- Mushroom / pedestal rock (కుక్కగొడుగు శిల / పీఠాకృతి శిల): sand is lifted only ~1–2 m, so abrasion is strongest near the ground and undercuts the base (plus weathering of softer base). USGS, NCERT. V height. [B52-F2, W52-1]
- Zeugen (జ్యూగెన్): tabular ridge: hard cap over soft layers, undercut along joints by weathering + abrasion. NCERT. (Book's alternative name "Pilzfelsen" is German for mushroom rock — do not repeat.) [B52-F3, W52-2]
- Yardang (యార్దాంగ్): streamlined ridge, parallel to prevailing wind, in vertically banded hard/soft rock. [B52-F4, W52-3]
- Inselberg (ఇన్సల్‌బర్గ్; German "island mountain"): isolated residual hill rising abruptly from a plain; bornhardt = bare rock dome type; Uluru (Australia) V. [B52-F5 F6, W52-4]
- Ventifact (కోణీయ శిల): stone faceted and polished by wind-blown sand; dreikanter = three faces. [B52-F7 F8, W52-5]
- Honeycomb weathering / stone lattice / tafoni (తేనెతుట్టె క్రమక్షయం): pits from salt crystallisation and wetting–drying; wind clears loosened grains. Link L5 `#weathering` (salt growth). [B52-F9, W52-6]
- Mesa (Spanish "table") and butte: flat-topped remnants under a resistant cap rock; mesa broad, butte small; Monument Valley (USA). [B49-F44, B52-F16, W52-12]
- Demoiselle / earth pillar: column of soft material protected by a cap stone from rain-wash (book attributes to wind — teach rain-wash). Britannica. [B52-F15, W52-11]
- Wadi (వాడీ): dry valley in arid land, flash floods after rare rain. [B52-F17, W52-13]

### `#desert-deposition`
- Sand dune (ఇసుక దిబ్బ): mound/ridge of wind-deposited sand where wind slows; gentle windward slope, steep lee slip face (~30–34° angle of repose V). USGS. [O08-005, B49-F45, B52-F18, O08-018]
- Dune type controlled by wind regime, sand supply, vegetation, moisture, topography. [W52-14]
- Barchan (బార్ఖాన్): crescent; horns point downwind; one dominant wind, limited sand. BK p054. [B52-F19, W52-15]
- Seif (సీఫ్; Arabic "sword") / linear dune: long narrow ridge; two wind directions. BK p055. [B52-F20, W52-16]
- Transverse and star dunes one line. USGS. V.
- Erg (ఎర్గ్): sand sea / large dune field; Rub' al Khali largest continuous sand sea V. [B48-F15, B52-F21, W52-17]
- Loess (లోయిస్): wind-blown silt (some clay, fine sand), yellow-buff colour from iron oxides/hydroxides (limonite per book); Chinese Loess Plateau supplies the Huang He's silt ("Yellow River"); also Mississippi valley, Ukraine, Central Europe; forms from desert dust and glacial outwash dust. BK p049, p054. Britannica. [O08-006, B48-F16 F17 F18, B52-F22, W48-18 W48-19 W48-20]
- Punjab–Haryana: book calls them loess plains; standard view: Indo-Gangetic plain is river alluvium, aeolian silt occurs in parts (SW Haryana, Rajasthan margin). V. [W48-21] → exam block.
- Dune (sand) vs loess (silt). [W48-22, W52-18, O08-018]
- Pediment: gently sloping rock surface at the foot of desert mountains, thin debris cover (book under erosional plains, Atlas example). NCERT. V.
- Bajada (బజాడా): continuous apron of coalesced alluvial fans along a desert mountain front; built by streams and debris flows, not wind. BK p054–055. NCERT. [B48-F7, B52-F23, W52-19]
- Bolson (బోల్సన్): internally drained basin between mountains in arid lands. BK p055. [B53-F1, W53-1]
- Playa (ప్లేయా): flat lowest floor of a bolson; temporary (playa) lake after rains; salts left by evaporation; salt flats. BK p055. [B53-F2, W53-2 W53-3]
- Oasis: surface water or groundwater in desert. [B49-F33 linked]

### `#coast-erosion`
- Coastal erosion processes = the family named in `#agents-overview` (hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, solution) applied by waves. [W53-4]
- Sea cliff (సముద్ర భృగువు): waves cut a notch at the base, overhang collapses, cliff retreats; also weathering + mass wasting + rock structure. BK p055. NCERT. [B49-F41, B53-F3, W53-5]
- Wave-cut platform (సముద్ర కృత వేదిక): gently sloping rock bench left in front of the retreating cliff. BK p055. [B53-F4, W53-6]
- Sea cave (సముద్ర గుహ) → arch (సముద్ర కృత వంతెన) → stack (సీ స్టాక్) → stump (స్టంప్); cave starts at a joint/weak zone in a headland. BK p055. NCERT. [O08-008, B53-F6 F8 F9, W53-7 W53-8]
- Blowhole (బ్లోహోల్): vertical shaft from a sea-cave roof to the surface; waves force air and spray up with noise. BK p055. [B53-F7, W53-9]
- Sea cave (waves, along cracks) vs karst cave (dissolution by groundwater). [B53-F5, O08-019]
- India/AP: Visakhapatnam natural rock arch near Thotlakonda/Mangamaripeta V; Varkala cliffs, Kerala V. One line each, only if verified.
- Headland / cape (అగ్రం): land projecting into sea; Cape Comorin (Kanyakumari). [B49-F25, B53-F13, W53-12]
- Bay (అఖాతం) and gulf (సింధుశాఖ): both sea inlets; gulf usually larger/more enclosed; names not strict (Bay of Bengal larger than many gulfs). [B49-F24, B53-F11, W53-11]
- Cove (కోవ్): small sheltered bay, narrow entrance. [B53-F12]
- Creek (క్రీక్): regional — small stream (USA, Australia) or narrow tidal inlet (India: Sir Creek, Gujarat V). [B53-F10, W53-10]
- Inlet: narrow arm of the sea into land. [B49-F46]

### `#coast-deposition`
- Longshore drift: waves at an angle move sediment along the beach. NCERT.
- Beach (బీచ్): loose sand, gravel, shells shaped by waves, tides, currents. BK p055. [B49-F42, B53-F14]
- Beach ridge (wave/swash-built, often above normal high tide) vs coastal dune (wind-built from dry beach sand; storm buffer). [B53-F16, W53-13 W53-15 W53-16]
- Shoal (షోల్): submerged/partly exposed shallow bank of sand, mud or rock. [B53-F15, W53-14]
- Bar (బార్): ridge of sediment roughly parallel to the coast; offshore bar / barrier bar in the water some distance out; baymouth bar closes a bay; lagoon forms behind. BK p055. NCERT. [B53-F17 F18, W53-17 W53-18]
- Spit (స్పిట్): sediment ridge attached to land at one end, other end free in the sea; longshore drift; if it closes a bay → baymouth bar. BK p055–056. [B53-F19, W53-19 W53-20]
- Book: Kerala spit ~80 km near Vembanad; Kakinada (Coringa) spit ~16 km (Hope Island V). V; lengths vary with survey date. [B53-F20] (retire candidate)
- Tombolo (టొంబోలో): sand/shingle ridge joining an island to the mainland or another island; forms in the wave-shadow behind the island (wave refraction). BK p056. [B54-F1, W54-1 W54-2]
- Rama Setu / Adam's Bridge: chain of limestone shoals, sandbanks and small islands (~48 km V) between Pamban (Rameswaram) and Mannar; book calls it a tombolo. GSI/Britannica V. [B54-F2, W54-3]
- Lagoon (లాగూన్): shallow coastal water body partly or wholly cut off by a bar, spit, barrier island or reef; haff = Baltic lagoon behind a spit. BK p056. [B49-F43, B54-F3, W49-19, W54-4 W54-5]
- Chilika (Odisha): largest brackish-water lagoon in India, ~1,100 km² (monsoon) V; first Indian Ramsar site (1981) with Keoladeo V. Pulicat (AP–TN): second largest brackish lagoon; Sriharikota lies on the barrier V. Vembanad, Ashtamudi: Kerala backwaters. Kolleru (AP): freshwater lake between Krishna–Godavari deltas, not a lagoon. Wetlands Intl / Ramsar / state govt. [B54-F4 F5 F6, W54-6]
- Marine depositional plain: shallow-sea and shore deposits emerge or are reworked; shaped by sediment supply, waves/currents, sea-level change. BK p050. [B49-F1, W49-1]

### `#karst`
- Karst: landscape on soluble rock (limestone, dolomite, gypsum) shaped by solution; name from Kras/Karst region (Slovenia–Italy), Dinaric Alps. USGS "Karst". BK p056 ("అంతర భూజల (కార్స్ట్) భూస్వరూపాలు"). [O08-007, B54-F7 F13, W54-7]
- Carbonic acid: CO₂ (air, soil) + water → H₂CO₃; dissolves calcite. Link L5 `#weathering`. [B54-F8, W54-8]
- Lapies / karren (లాపీలు): solution grooves and ridges on bare limestone. BK p056. [B54-F9, W54-9]
- Sinkhole / doline (సింక్‌హోల్ / డోలైన్): closed depression by solution or collapse of a cave roof; sizes from metres to hundreds of metres; book "1 m², 3–9 m" is not a rule. USGS. [B54-F10, W54-10 W54-11 W54-12, B54-G1, W48-1]
- Swallow hole (stream disappears underground); blind valley (valley ends abruptly where its stream sinks). BK p056. [B54-F11, W54-13 W54-14]
- Uvala (యువాలా): large irregular depression from merged dolines. BK p056. [B54-F17, W54-17]
- Polje (large flat-floored closed basin, Dinaric) — one line V.
- Caves/caverns (గుహలు / కందకాలు): dissolution along joints and bedding planes; definition: natural underground opening large enough for a person. [B49-F39, B54-F14, W54-15]
- Mammoth Cave (Kentucky, USA): longest known cave system (>676 km mapped V, number changes). NPS. [B54-F12 F15, W54-16]
- Borra Caves: Ananthagiri Hills, Eastern Ghats, Alluri Sitharama Raju district, AP; limestone; Gosthani river origin V. Belum Caves: Nandyal district (formerly Kurnool), AP; ~3.2 km, second-longest natural cave in Indian plains V. AP Tourism. [B54-F22, B54-M5]
- Kotumsar and other caves: Kanger Valley National Park, Bastar, Chhattisgarh. [B54-F16]
- Meghalaya: longest caves of India (Krem Liat Prah system, >30 km V). One line.
- Terra rossa: red clay residue left on limestone after solution; soils lesson later. BK p048.

### `#cave-deposits`

## Traps (max 1 per section; real confusions only)
| Section | Trap |
|---|---|
| `#agents-overview` | Wind is not the main agent even in most deserts' landform history; running water (flash floods) shapes much of the desert too. |

| `#river-residual` | Monadnock is a hill left standing on a peneplain, not a hill built by deposition. |

| `#glacier-basics` | Iceberg is calved land ice (fresh water), not frozen sea water. |

| `#glacial-deposition` | Till = unsorted (ice), outwash = sorted (meltwater). |

## Book errors (outline only; in the lesson state the correct fact plainly, exam block only where an exam may ask the book value)
- Mushroom rock: book says wind a little above ground is faster, so base erodes — correct reason: sand is carried near the ground, so abrasion is strongest at the base.
- Zeugen also called "Pilzfelsen" (book) — Pilzfelsen = mushroom rock; do not repeat.
- Demoiselle formed by wind (book) — rain-wash under a cap stone.
- Bajada listed under wind deposition (book) — fluvial / debris-flow fans.
- Horn "3 or 4 cirques", arête "2 or 3" — horn three or more, arête between two.
- Kame height 20–60 m — no fixed height.
- Sinkhole "1 m², 3–9 m deep" — sizes vary widely.
- Rama Setu as tombolo — chain of shoals (exam block).
- Doline / sinkholes "Yugoslavia" — Dinaric karst, Slovenia (Kras) gives the name.
- Punjab–Haryana as loess plains (exam block).
- "Embarkments" (OCR word) as river landform — teach residual hard-rock mounds/benches without that name.
- Ganga called "digitate" delta in the book list — teach GBM as a tide-influenced delta; bird-foot = Mississippi.
- Roche moutonnée OCR implies plucking less, abrasion more overall — teach stoss = abrasion, lee = plucking.
