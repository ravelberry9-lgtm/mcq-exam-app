Chapter N2 — Nuclear Power Reactors and Power PlantsESSENTIAL

Syllabus clause: "Indian Nuclear Reactors and Nuclear Power Plants." Second of four Nuclear Technology chapters — focuses only on civil POWER reactors and POWER PLANTS (research reactors are N3; radioisotopes are N4).
N2A — From Institutions to Power Plants

The previous chapter (N1) introduced the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE) and its operating company, NPCIL, along with the three-stage nuclear programme concept. This chapter does not re-teach those institutions — it asks a narrower, more exam-relevant question: which reactor TYPES does India actually run to generate electricity, where are they, and what is each plant's current status? Classification is the heart of this chapter, exactly as it was for the Defence missile chapters.

మునుపటి చాప్టర్ (N1) లో DAE మరియు దాని operating company NPCIL గురించి, మూడు-దశల అణు కార్యక్రమం (three-stage programme) గురించి పరిచయం అయింది. ఈ చాప్టర్ వాటిని మళ్ళీ బోధించదు — దీని ప్రశ్న చాలా స్పష్టంగా ఉంటుంది: India ఏ reactor TYPES ద్వారా విద్యుత్ ఉత్పత్తి చేస్తోంది, అవి ఎక్కడ ఉన్నాయి, ఒక్కొక్క plant status ఇప్పుడు ఏమిటి? Classification ఈ చాప్టర్‌కు Defence missile chapters లానే కేంద్ర బిందువు.
Reader assumption: you already know DAE → NPCIL (operating PSU) → AERB (regulator) and the Stage-1/Stage-2/Stage-3 programme logic from N1. This chapter builds on top of that, it does not repeat it.
N2B — The Four Reactor Types in India's Civil Power Programme

India's commercial nuclear power fleet is built from four distinct reactor types, each tied to a different technology-supply history. Learn the type → fuel/moderator → example-site chain for each; this is the single most examinable structure in this chapter.

India యొక్క commercial nuclear power fleet నాలుగు వేర్వేరు reactor types తో నిర్మించబడింది, ప్రతి ఒక్కటి వేరే technology-supply చరిత్రతో ముడిపడి ఉంది. ప్రతి దానికి type → fuel/moderator → example-site chain నేర్చుకోండి; ఇది ఈ చాప్టర్‌లో అత్యంత examinable structure.

1. PHWR (Pressurised Heavy Water Reactor) — India's workhorse. An indigenous design evolved from the Canadian CANDU design, using natural uranium as fuel and heavy water (D₂O) as both moderator and coolant. The majority of India's operating reactors are PHWRs, and because they run on natural (unenriched) uranium, PHWRs are the type most aligned with India's own Stage-1 fuel-cycle self-sufficiency logic from N1.

1. PHWR (Pressurised Heavy Water Reactor) — India యొక్క workhorse reactor. ఇది Canadian CANDU design నుండి evolve అయిన ఒక indigenous design, natural uranium fuel గా, heavy water (D₂O) ను moderator మరియు coolant రెండింటిలాగా వాడుతుంది. India యొక్క operating reactors లో మెజారిటీ PHWR రకానికి చెందినవే, ఇవి natural (unenriched) uranium పైనే run అవుతాయి కాబట్టి, N1 లో చెప్పిన Stage-1 fuel-cycle self-sufficiency logic కు ఇవే బాగా సరిపోతాయి.

2. BWR (Boiling Water Reactor) — Tarapur Units 1 & 2, Maharashtra, are India's oldest commercial reactors. They were US-supplied by General Electric and commissioned in 1969, under a 1963 India-US nuclear cooperation agreement — years before India's 1974 nuclear test and decades before the 2008 India-US deal covered later in this chapter. This pre-NPT-era supply later became a point of diplomatic friction over continued fuel supply after 1974.

2. BWR (Boiling Water Reactor) — Maharashtra లోని Tarapur Unit 1 & 2 India యొక్క అత్యంత పాత commercial reactors. వీటిని అమెరికా GE (General Electric) supply చేసింది, 1969 లో commission అయ్యాయి, ఇది 1963 నాటి India-US nuclear cooperation agreement కింద జరిగింది — 1974 India test కన్నా చాలా ముందు, ఈ చాప్టర్‌లో తర్వాత వచ్చే 2008 India-US deal కన్నా దశాబ్దాల ముందు. 1974 test తర్వాత fuel supply కొనసాగింపుపై diplomatic friction కు ఈ పాత, pre-NPT-era supply ఒక కారణం అయింది.

3. PWR / VVER — Kudankulam Nuclear Power Plant (KKNPP), Tamil Nadu, uses the Russian VVER-1000 design (a pressurised-water reactor family), built under Indo-Russian cooperation. KKNPP's units are India's largest-capacity individual reactor units in commercial operation.

3. PWR / VVER — Tamil Nadu లోని Kudankulam Nuclear Power Plant (KKNPP) Russian VVER-1000 design (ఒక pressurised-water reactor family) వాడుతుంది, ఇది Indo-Russian cooperation కింద నిర్మించబడింది. KKNPP units commercial operation లో ఉన్న India యొక్క అతిపెద్ద-capacity individual reactor units.

4. PFBR (Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor) — at Kalpakkam, Tamil Nadu, this is India's Stage-2 demonstration reactor under IGCAR (technical lead) and BHAVINI (the DAE PSU building it). Status check (dated): PFBR reached first criticality on 7 April 2026, after construction that began in 2004 and an original target year of 2010 — criticality is NOT the same as full power or grid connection; as of this chapter's writing, phased grid injection is planned from December 2026 and had not yet occurred.

4. PFBR (Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor) — Tamil Nadu లోని Kalpakkam వద్ద ఉన్న ఇది India యొక్క Stage-2 demonstration reactor, IGCAR (technical lead) మరియు BHAVINI (దీన్ని నిర్మిస్తున్న DAE PSU) పర్యవేక్షణలో ఉంది. Status (dated): PFBR 7 April 2026 న first criticality సాధించింది — నిర్మాణం 2004 లో మొదలై, original target 2010 గా ఉండేది. కానీ criticality అంటే full power లేదా grid connection కాదు; ఈ చాప్టర్ రాసే సమయానికి, phased grid injection December 2026 నుండి మొదలవ్వాలని plan చేయబడింది, అది ఇంకా జరగలేదు.

💡 Why / How
  • PHWR dominance follows directly from India's limited uranium-enrichment capacity historically — natural-uranium PHWRs sidestep the need for enrichment plants.
  • BWR and PWR/VVER both need enriched uranium, which is why both arrived via foreign supply (USA for Tarapur in 1969; Russia for Kudankulam), not as indigenous designs.
  • PFBR uses no moderator at all by design — it is a fast reactor, deliberately keeping neutrons fast (unmoderated) to "breed" more fissile plutonium-239 than it consumes, the core idea of Stage-2.
PHWR ఆధిక్యానికి కారణం India యొక్క చారిత్రాత్మకంగా పరిమిత uranium-enrichment capacity — natural-uranium PHWR లకు enrichment plants అవసరం లేదు. BWR, PWR/VVER రెండింటికీ enriched uranium అవసరం, అందుకే అవి foreign supply ద్వారా వచ్చాయి (1969 లో Tarapur కోసం USA; Kudankulam కోసం Russia). PFBR design ప్రకారం ఎలాంటి moderator వాడదు — ఇది ఒక fast reactor, neutrons ను ఉద్దేశపూర్వకంగా fast (unmoderated) గా ఉంచి, అది వినియోగించే దానికంటే ఎక్కువ fissile plutonium-239 ను "breed" చేస్తుంది — ఇదే Stage-2 యొక్క ముఖ్య ఆలోచన.

N2C — NPCIL: The Operator of India's Power Fleet

NPCIL (Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited) is the central operator of every commercial nuclear power reactor in India — a Public Sector Undertaking (PSU) under the DAE. Regardless of reactor type (PHWR, BWR, or VVER), it is NPCIL that runs the plant day to day; AERB (introduced in N1) regulates safety across all of them.

NPCIL (Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited) India లో ఉన్న ప్రతి commercial nuclear power reactor యొక్క కేంద్ర operator — ఇది DAE కింద ఉన్న ఒక Public Sector Undertaking (PSU). Reactor type ఏదైనా (PHWR, BWR, లేదా VVER), plant ను రోజువారీగా నడిపేది NPCIL యే; N1 లో పరిచయం అయిన AERB అన్నింటి safety ను regulate చేస్తుంది.
Dated snapshot — do not teach as a permanent number As of a Rajya Sabha written reply reported 31 July 2026, India had 24 nuclear power reactors in commercial operation with a total installed capacity of 8,780 MW. A separate PIB-sourced Parliament answer (Dec 2025) gives the same order of magnitude — 8.78 GW installed (excluding RAPS-1's 100 MW) — and projects capacity reaching 22.38 GW by 2031-32, with the government's Nuclear Energy Mission targeting 100 GW by 2047. NPCIL has separately stated plans to add 18 more reactors (~13,800 MWe) by 2032.
ఇది శాశ్వత సంఖ్య కాదు — 31 July 2026 నాటి Rajya Sabha written reply ప్రకారం, India లో 24 reactors commercial operation లో ఉన్నాయి, total installed capacity 8,780 MW. Dec 2025 నాటి ఒక వేరే PIB-ఆధారిత Parliament answer కూడా దాదాపు అదే సంఖ్య (8.78 GW) చూపిస్తుంది, 2031-32 నాటికి 22.38 GW కి, 2047 నాటికి Nuclear Energy Mission ప్రకారం 100 GW కి చేరాలని target పెట్టుకుంది.
Treat every reactor-count/capacity figure as a dated snapshot — these numbers move every year as new units are commissioned.

N2E — Kovvada: Andhra Pradesh's Nuclear Anchor

The Kovvada Nuclear Power Park, proposed in Ranasthalam mandal, Srikakulam district, Andhra Pradesh, is the one civil-power project directly inside AP's own geography — and it is India's flagship proposed site for US-design AP1000 reactors built by Westinghouse Electric Company, under the broader India-US civil nuclear cooperation framework.

Andhra Pradesh లోని Srikakulam జిల్లా, Ranasthalam mandal లో proposed అయిన Kovvada Nuclear Power Park — AP యొక్క స్వంత భూభాగంలో ఉన్న ఏకైక civil-power project ఇది — మరియు Westinghouse Electric Company నిర్మించే US-design AP1000 reactors కోసం India యొక్క ప్రధాన proposed site, ఇది broader India-US civil nuclear cooperation framework కింద ఉంది.

Verified current status (dated 10 September 2026, via Westinghouse's own India country director): NPCIL and Westinghouse remain "in discussions" to deploy six AP1000 reactors at Kovvada. About 190.7 acres have been acquired for a rehabilitation-and-resettlement colony, and the district administration has identified land for an employees' township. No binding agreement has been confirmed as signed, and no construction-start date is confirmed — the honest status is "long-stalled since the mid-2010s, discussions ongoing, land work partially done." This session could not confirm either a revival with a signed deal, or a formal shelving/cancellation — both extremes should be avoided in an answer.

ఇప్పటి వరకూ verify అయిన స్థితి (10 September 2026 నాటి Westinghouse India country director statement ప్రకారం): NPCIL మరియు Westinghouse Kovvada వద్ద ఆరు AP1000 reactors పెట్టాలని ఇంకా "చర్చల్లో (discussions)" ఉన్నాయి. rehabilitation-and-resettlement colony కోసం 190.7 ఎకరాలు సేకరించారు, employees' township కోసం district administration భూమిని గుర్తించింది. ఎటువంటి binding agreement సంతకం అయినట్లు నిర్ధారణ కాలేదు, నిర్మాణం మొదలైన తేదీ కూడా నిర్ధారణ కాలేదు — నిజమైన స్థితి "2010ల మధ్య నుండి చాలాకాలంగా ఆగిపోయింది, చర్చలు కొనసాగుతున్నాయి, భూసేకరణ పాక్షికంగా జరిగింది" అని చెప్పొచ్చు. ఈ session లో దీని revival/signed-deal గానీ, పూర్తి shelving/cancellation గానీ నిర్ధారించలేకపోయాం — జవాబులో ఈ రెండు అతి-తీవ్రమైన ముగింపులను తప్పించాలి.
💡 Why / How — the long stall, two factors
  • 2017 Westinghouse bankruptcy: Westinghouse's parent, Toshiba, drove it into a Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing in 2017, disrupting the original timeline for Kovvada's AP1000 units — widely reported, uncontested background.
  • CLNDA liability friction: India's Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act, 2010 (CLNDA), Section 17(b), lets the plant operator recover compensation it pays out from the equipment/service supplier if there is a "patent or latent defect." This departs from the international norm (liability resting on the operator alone) and is widely cited as a hurdle that made US, French and Russian suppliers cautious — though no single source in this chapter's research confirms it as the one proven cause of Kovvada's specific stall; it is taught here as a major contributing friction point, not an unhedged single cause.
2017 Westinghouse bankruptcy (parent Toshiba driven to Chapter 11) Kovvada AP1000 units timeline ను disrupt చేసింది. అదే సమయంలో, India యొక్క CLNDA, 2010 లోని Section 17(b) — operator supplier మీద recourse తీసుకునే వీలు ఇచ్చే clause — international norm (operator-only liability) నుండి వైదొలగడం, US/French/Russian suppliers ను జాగ్రత్తగా ఉండేలా చేసింది. కానీ ఇది Kovvada స్తంభనకు ఒక్కటే, నిరూపితమైన కారణం అని ఏ ఒక్క source చెప్పలేదు — దీన్ని ఒక ప్రధాన friction point గానే, పూర్తి causal certainty లేకుండా బోధిస్తున్నాం.
N2F — CLNDA, 2010: The Liability Law Behind the Friction

Internationally, nuclear liability conventions place compensation liability on the plant operator alone. India's CLNDA departed from this: Section 17(b) gives the operator a right of recourse against the supplier if the supplied equipment or service had a defect. Parliament adopted this, citing India's experience with industrial-accident compensation (the Bhopal gas tragedy is the cited precedent) — but major supplier countries (USA, Russia, France) have resisted it as inconsistent with global practice.

అంతర్జాతీయంగా, nuclear liability conventions compensation liability ను plant operator మీదే పెడతాయి. India యొక్క CLNDA దీని నుండి వైదొలగింది: Section 17(b) ప్రకారం, సరఫరా చేసిన equipment/service లో defect ఉంటే, operator supplier మీద recourse హక్కు తీసుకోవచ్చు. దీన్ని Parliament ఆమోదించింది, India యొక్క industrial-accident compensation అనుభవం (Bhopal gas tragedy ఉదాహరణగా చెప్పబడింది) ఆధారంగా — కానీ ప్రధాన supplier దేశాలు (USA, Russia, France) దీన్ని global practice కు విరుద్ధంగా భావించి వ్యతిరేకించాయి.
N2G — International Framework: 123 Agreement, NSG Waiver, NPT/IAEA

The 2008 India-US Civil Nuclear Cooperation Agreement (commonly the "123 Agreement", after Section 123 of the US Atomic Energy Act) opened the door to civil nuclear trade with India. It was made possible by a 2008 Nuclear Suppliers Group (NSG) waiver, which exempted India from NSG trade-restriction guidelines that would otherwise have blocked such trade with a state outside the NPT.

2008 నాటి India-US Civil Nuclear Cooperation Agreement (సాధారణంగా US Atomic Energy Act లోని Section 123 పేరుతో "123 Agreement" అని పిలుస్తారు) India తో civil nuclear trade కు తలుపులు తెరిచింది. ఇది సాధ్యమైనది 2008 నాటి Nuclear Suppliers Group (NSG) waiver వల్ల — ఇది NPT కి బయట ఉన్న ఒక దేశంతో trade ను అడ్డుకునే NSG guidelines నుండి India కు మినహాయింపు ఇచ్చింది.
The classic trap India is NOT a signatory to the NPT (Non-Proliferation Treaty) — yet it participates in safeguarded civil nuclear cooperation through an India-specific IAEA safeguards agreement, which covers only India's declared civil facilities, not its military/strategic ones. NPT non-membership and IAEA civil-facility safeguards are NOT contradictory — they are two separate, parallel tracks.
India NPT సంతకం చేయలేదు — అయినా, ఒక India-specific IAEA safeguards agreement ద్వారా safeguarded civil nuclear cooperation లో భాగం పంచుకుంటోంది, ఇది India యొక్క ప్రకటించిన civil facilities నే కవర్ చేస్తుంది, military/strategic facilities ను కాదు. NPT non-membership, IAEA civil-facility safeguards ఒకదానికొకటి విరుద్ధం కాదు — ఇవి రెండు వేరు వేరు, సమాంతర ట్రాక్‌లు.
NPT = not a member. IAEA safeguards (India-specific, civil-only) = yes, participates. Two different things.

Fleet snapshot: 24 reactors operating, 8,780 MW installed (as of 31 Jul 2026 RS reply) — dated, moving number
Kovvada (AP): Ranasthalam mandal, Srikakulam · 6 × Westinghouse AP1000 proposed · status "in discussions," not operating, not confirmed signed (as of Sep 2026)
CLNDA, 2010, Section 17(b): operator's right of recourse against supplier — departs from global operator-only-liability norm · cited friction point for foreign deals
Framework: 123 Agreement (2008, India-US) + NSG waiver (2008) → India not an NPT member but under an India-specific IAEA safeguards agreement (civil facilities only)
🎯 Traps & Confusables
Common confusions
TrapCorrect distinction
"PFBR is operational / generating power"PFBR achieved first criticality (Apr 2026); grid power injection was only planned from Dec 2026 — criticality ≠ grid-connected ≠ full commercial operation.
"Kovvada is India's newest operating nuclear plant"Kovvada is proposed, not operating — long-stalled since the mid-2010s, "in discussions" as of Sep 2026, no confirmed construction start.
"India is a member of the NPT, which is why it gets IAEA safeguards"India is not an NPT member. Its IAEA safeguards are via a separate, India-specific agreement covering only civil facilities.
"Tarapur reactors are indigenous PHWRs"Tarapur units are BWR, US/GE-supplied (1969) — not PHWR, and not indigenous.
"Kudankulam is a PHWR"Kudankulam is PWR/VVER (Russian design) — PHWR is the indigenous CANDU-derived type used elsewhere (Kaiga, Narora, etc.), not at Kudankulam.
"Section 17(b) of CLNDA is proven to be THE single reason Kovvada stalled"It is a widely cited major friction point, but this book's research found no single source proving it as the sole, confirmed cause — the 2017 Westinghouse bankruptcy is an equally cited factor.
