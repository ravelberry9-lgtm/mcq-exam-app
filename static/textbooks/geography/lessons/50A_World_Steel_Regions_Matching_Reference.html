Deferred reference — world iron-and-steel regions (APPSC Group I PYQ)

This is not part of the active APPSC Group 2 India–AP textbook reading order. It preserves a complete explanation for the previously verified Group I world-regions matching question. The active India-industries chapter keeps only the historically relevant Ruhr analogy for Damodar Valley.

First separate a region from a country. Ruhr / రూర్ is the historic coal–steel industrial region of western Germany, especially North Rhine-Westphalia. Essen's Zollverein coal/coke complex is a map anchor. Therefore a paper pair placing Ruhr in Eastern Europe is wrong: its German location is in western Europe. UNESCO: Zollverein, North Rhine-Westphalia.

The Ural / యూరల్ industrial region lies around the Ural Mountains in Russia; Chelyabinsk and Sverdlovsk are examples of Russian metallurgical geography. In the United Kingdom, north-eastern Teesside and centres including Sheffield–Rotherham and Scunthorpe illustrate the older steel map. In the United States, the historic steel belt was not restricted to an ocean beach: it extended from the Atlantic-side Mid-Atlantic through the Great Lakes towards Illinois. Thus the paper's broad US, Russia and UK region-country pairs can be retained, but “Ruhr—Eastern Europe” cannot. USGS: Russian metallurgical facilities; UK Government steel strategy, site geography; USGS: historic US steel belt.

This is a historic regional-location exercise, not a statement of 2026 plant output, ownership or ranking. Read each pair as country → region → resource/industry history, never as “all plants in that country are in this one region.”
